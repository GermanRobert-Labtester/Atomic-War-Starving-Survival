# EXPANSION CW63-02 — The Tree That Ate Light

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 63: Children’s Inherited Outside

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** folklore_children_the_tree_that_ate_light — The Tree That Ate Light.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Tree That Ate Light is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore.json. It calls the little sapling in Level 4 a beloved mythic eater of sadness and says that when it grows forty feet tall, its branches will break through sixty feet of reinforced concrete ceiling, bringing real green rain and sweet yellow apples to everyone. This plan expands the myth as a shared future image, keeping the sapling, the concrete, and the impossible scale visible while refusing to turn plant growth into a forecast or construction route.

## 2. Story question

When children believe a Level 4 sapling drinks sadness from the air and will break through sixty feet of concrete for green rain and yellow apples, what kind of future can a living thing carry without becoming a promise?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the sapling’s species, age, caretaker, actual height, the children who tell the myth, or whether anyone expects the ceiling to break. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, greenhouse, child, botanical, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "A beloved myth that the little sapling in Level 4 is drinking up all the sadness from the bunker air, and when it grows forty feet tall, its branches will break through the sixty feet of reinforced concrete ceiling, letting in real green rain and sweet yellow apples for everyone." The fuller reading keeps the sapling beloved without making it responsible for curing the bunker. Children can place sadness in the air because sadness has no obvious container. A small tree gives that feeling a trunk, leaves, and a future large enough to reach beyond the ceiling. The forty feet and sixty feet are not measurements to validate. They are the sizes of hope when a room has no direct view of the weather. Green rain and yellow apples bring color and taste into a myth that begins with grief. A candidate scene may show children checking the sapling, a botanist refusing to call it a cure, or an adult quietly watering it while knowing the branches will not break through concrete. Those moments let the myth remain beloved without making the teller foolish. The folklore record does not identify the plant, the children, or the caretaker. Keep those gaps open. The existing greenhouse or botanical consumer can provide context if it is current, but this plan must not add growth timers, emotional state, weather routes, or a reward for belief. A return can change the audience around the sapling. Someone who once laughed may repeat the myth to a younger child; someone who believed may find a new way to love the plant after the future fails to arrive. The story is strongest when the tree continues to grow at its own scale while the myth grows at the community’s scale. Verify the source and current greenhouse owner before placement. If the route requires the myth to become true, leave the passage as editorial folklore. The ceiling can remain intact and the future can still be green.

The emotional center is a plant made responsible for feelings no adult system can absorb. **02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. **04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence.
