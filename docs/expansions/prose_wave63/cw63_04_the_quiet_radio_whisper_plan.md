## 1. Expansion thesis

The Quiet Radio Whisper is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore.json. It says children believe that if someone presses an ear to the copper lead of the long-wire receiver while everyone in the bunker holds their breath, static names every child born underground in alphabetical order. This plan expands the belief as a wish for a complete communal memory, not as radio technique or a claim about the receiver’s signal.

## 2. Story question

When children believe held breath beside a long-wire receiver makes static say every underground child’s name in alphabetical order, what does a bunker hope its machines remember?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish who invented the belief, which receiver or copper lead is meant, how many children were born underground, or whether anyone hears names. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, radio, child, naming, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "A belief that if you press your ear to the copper lead of the long-wire receiver while everyone in the bunker is holding their breath, the static says the names of every child born underground in alphabetical order." The fuller reading keeps the radio whisper as a wish for complete memory rather than a method for producing it. The copper lead is intimate because the children imagine a machine being brought close enough to hear a name. Holding breath turns the whole bunker into a participant, but the plan must not turn that image into a challenge or a listening instruction. Static is useful in the folklore because it can contain anything when a room needs it to contain something. Alphabetical order gives the impossible list a child-sized shape. It suggests fairness without proving that a list can be complete. The record does not tell us who invented the belief, which children were born underground, or whether a single name has ever been heard. A candidate passage can let a teacher protect the belief as metaphor, a child insist on its truth, or a later reader notice that names can be remembered without being broadcast. These positions can coexist without a registry. The radio consumer, if current canon provides one, may carry the source as an attributed folklore fragment, but no decoder, signal route, or birth record should be added. A return can show a name spoken beside the receiver by a person who never expected the static to answer. That changes the audience, not the machine. Keep the list unnamed and the promise open. Verify source ownership, radio canon, and privacy boundaries before placement. If the route would require a real response from the receiver, keep the whisper editorial. The bunker wants a machine that remembers everyone. The story matters because the bunker must still remember by hand.
