# EXPANSION CW63-06 — The Name Under the Bunk

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 63: Children’s Inherited Outside

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** folklore_children_name_under_the_bunk — The Name Under the Bunk.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Name Under the Bunk is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore.json. It describes a secret ritual among orphans in Dormitory 4: when a guardian or bunk-mate dies, a child scratches the first letter of the name into the underside of a pine bunk slat with a rusty roofing nail and seals it with candle grease so adults do not sand it off. This plan treats the ritual as a quiet memorial practice, preserving its grief and privacy without providing marking instructions or creating a memorial registry.

## 2. Story question

When orphaned children scratch the first letter of a dead guardian or bunk-mate into pine and seal it with candle grease, what kind of memorial can remain hidden from adults and still belong to a room?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the children’s names, the dead, the bunk number, the letter, the adults, or how many marks exist. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, memorial, child, bunk, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "A secret ritual among orphans in Dormitory 4: when a guardian or bunk-mate dies, the child uses a rusty roofing nail to scratch the first letter of their name into the underside of the pine bunk slat, sealed with a dab of candle grease so the adults do not sand it off." The fuller reading treats the first letter as a memorial that protects both the dead and the child who remembers. The ritual is secret because adults may sand it away, but secrecy does not make it a puzzle. It is a boundary around grief. A first letter can stand for a whole name without reducing the person to a symbol. The pine slat is part of a sleeping place, so the memorial remains in the ordinary room rather than moving to a formal archive. The rusty nail and candle grease are source details that should remain attributed to the folklore. This plan must not turn them into a how-to sequence. A candidate scene may show a child feeling beneath a bunk, an adult noticing a sealed patch, or a later sleeper inheriting a letter without knowing its story. Each moment can preserve privacy without creating a hidden-mark mechanic. The memorial consumer, if one exists, should carry the text as an attributed cultural practice rather than as a registry of deceased names. A return can let someone speak the full name aloud after years of leaving only the initial hidden. That is a change in audience and courage, not a new grief state. Keep the guardians, bunk-mates, orphans, and adults unnamed unless current canon proves otherwise. Verify the current memorial and child-content owners before placement. If the route would expose a child’s mark as a collectible or database entry, keep the ritual editorial. The underside of the bunk is not empty. It is a small place where love remains when a whole name cannot safely remain in view.

The emotional center is a memorial small enough for a child to control. The ritual does not make death disappear; it gives grief a surface adults may not own. The underside of the bunk holds a first letter because a whole name might be too exposed, too difficult, or too likely to be removed.
