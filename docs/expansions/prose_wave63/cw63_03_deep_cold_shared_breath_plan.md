# EXPANSION CW63-03 — Deep Cold, Shared Breath

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 63: Children’s Inherited Outside

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** folklore_children_deep_cold_lullaby — Deep Cold, Shared Breath.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Deep Cold, Shared Breath is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore.json. It gives a four-line lullaby about biting iron, a thin blanket, tucking two elbows, breathing on a brother or sheet, and morning arriving with porridge and heat. The record says elder siblings sing it in bottom tiers when auxiliary heaters cycle down during sub-zero nights. This plan expands the lullaby as sibling care and temporal endurance while avoiding thermal, health, or heater instructions.

## 2. Story question

When elder siblings sing of biting iron, thin blankets, tucked elbows, shared breath, and morning porridge during heater cycles, how does a lullaby hold a room together without pretending the cold is kind?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the singers’ names, the heater cycle, the bottom tier layout, the relationship of the siblings, or whether porridge arrives every morning. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, shelter, child, heater, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "'The iron is biting, the blanket is thin, / Tuck the two elbows and pull your knees in. / Breathe on your brother, breathe on the sheet, / Morning comes early with porridge and heat.' Sung by elder siblings in the bottom tiers when the auxiliary heaters cycle down during sub-zero nights." The fuller reading treats the lullaby as a promise made by a person who cannot control the heater. Biting iron and thin blankets keep the cold honest. The song does not say the night is safe; it gives the body a rhythm and gives siblings a reason to stay close. Tucking elbows is an image of making oneself small, not a posture to teach. Breathing on a brother or sheet is a lyric of shared warmth and should not become medical or thermal guidance. Morning comes early because the song needs time to move, not because a schedule has been established. Porridge and heat are promised in the same register: ordinary, desired, and not guaranteed by the record. A candidate scene may show an elder singer forgetting a line, a younger child supplying it, or a listener who cannot sleep until the final word. These details make care visible without turning the lullaby into a sleep system. The auxiliary-heater context belongs to the source record and should remain a boundary around the song, not an invitation to describe equipment. A return may let the song be sung after the cold has passed, or by someone who has become the elder. The new voice can change the emphasis while preserving the original text. Keep the siblings unnamed unless current canon establishes them. Verify shelter and child-content ownership before placement. If a consumer cannot preserve the difference between comfort and instruction, use the lullaby as editorial reference. The song does not defeat the cold. It gives the night another sound. The song can end with a room listening for the next breath and finding that the next line has already been remembered by someone else. Its care remains a relationship, not a remedy.
