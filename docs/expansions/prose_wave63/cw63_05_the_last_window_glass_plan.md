# EXPANSION CW63-05 — The Last Window Glass

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 63: Children’s Inherited Outside

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Last Window Glass is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore.json. It is a legend passed down from an elder’s sketch, describing the sky as a sheet of cold blue glass extending four hundred miles without a ceiling bolt, with birds that did not bump their heads on pipes. This plan expands the legend as a visual inheritance and a gentle argument about scale, keeping the impossible ceiling image separate from a factual map of the surface.

## 2. Story question

When an elder’s sketch says the sky was cold blue glass without a ceiling bolt and birds never bumped their heads on pipes, how does a bunker inherit an outside it can only draw?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

When birds flew, they did not bump their heads on the pipes.'" The fuller reading keeps the last window as a legend of absence. Cold blue glass is a familiar material turned into an unlimited ceiling, and the missing ceiling bolt tells children what the sky is by saying what it does not contain. Four hundred miles is an impossible scale inside a bunker, which is exactly why the number can carry wonder. Birds become witnesses because they move through the space without touching the pipes that define underground life. The elder’s sketch is important as transmission, not proof. A child can inherit a line without inheriting certainty about the person who drew it. A candidate scene may place the legend beside an artwork, let a child challenge the phrase about birds, or let an elder admit that memory changes when it is repeated. None of those moments should become surface navigation, flight guidance, or a map. The folklore record gives the outside a visual shape while keeping the outside unreachable. Keep them open. Verify current child-content and artwork consumers before placement. If a route requires the legend to establish a factual sky, keep the plan in editorial review. The window is last because it survives as a picture after physical windows are gone. Its glass is made of memory and still lets something through. The legend can end with the child folding the sketch back into its sleeve, carrying the cold blue space without claiming to own the sky.
