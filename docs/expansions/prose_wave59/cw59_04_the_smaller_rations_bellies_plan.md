## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Day Three Of The Smaller Rations is graffiti triggered by ration_scoop_rotated, available from day 70 with weight 1.0. Its text says the bellies ring at odd hours, two people set the alarm for the morning bell and the bell rang anyway, and that the bellies wrote their own schedule and were correct. This bank treats the posting as hunger speaking through time. It does not provide ration calculations, food planning, alarm use, or a needs system. It keeps the body’s correction more authoritative than the schedule without turning that truth into a mechanic.

## 2. Story question

When bellies begin their own schedule on the third day of smaller rations and the morning bell rings anyway, what does a bunker owe the body that refuses to follow the clock?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, trigger flag, minimum day, and weight. It does not establish the ration amount, the two people, the bell time, the alarm device, or the bunker’s food stores. Prose may widen the emotional context but cannot replace the existing ration owner or add a hunger simulation. The local record is especially careful about time: it names the third day, odd hours, two people, and the morning bell, but it does not turn those details into a nutritional schedule. The plan keeps that distinction visible because hunger is easy to flatten into a meter and difficult to portray as lived time. The bell can be punctual while the body is correct. That small contradiction gives the posting its humor and its ache. A reader may imagine someone waking before the expected signal, hearing another stomach answer from the dark, or discovering that the people who set an alarm have become listeners to a different clock. Those images are editorial possibilities only. They do not establish an alarm object, a meal interval, a quantity, or a medical condition. The phrase smaller rations should remain a source phrase whose bodily consequence is felt rather than calculated. The two people can stay a pair without names, histories, or a new relationship record. Their shared voice matters because the graffiti refuses the idea that scarcity happens to an abstract population. It happens to bodies that continue to hear bells, carry cups, and answer one another. The plan therefore lets a scoop keeper remember policy through a person who receives it, while refusing to turn that memory into advice. The morning bell is communal, but it cannot guarantee that the communal schedule fits everyone. A candidate scene may place the posting beside a wall clock, an empty cup, or a hand that remains on a blanket after the signal. Such props are atmospheric and must be checked against current visual and narrative canon before use. The existing ration flag is a possible content hook, not a license to add a hunger subsystem. The final claim that the bellies were correct can be read as a joke, a protest, or a plain statement of embodied truth. It does not require the institution to become villainous, nor does it require the body to win. The plan is strongest when it lets both clocks remain audible and asks the player to notice who is forced to live by which one. Before placement, verify the source record and the existing owner. If a current consumer cannot preserve that separation between felt scarcity and operational data, use the passage as reference prose only.
