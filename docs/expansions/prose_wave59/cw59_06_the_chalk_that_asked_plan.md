# EXPANSION CW59-06 — The Chalk That Asked

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 59: Chalk, Doors, and Return

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_challenge_to_the_lead — The Chalk That Asked.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Chalk That Asked is graffiti triggered by escalation_challenge_acknowledged, available from day 80 with weight 1.0. Its text addresses whoever left chalk under the leadership doorway, says the council sheet has been read, says the doorway does not vote on it, and describes the doorway’s long practice of filing complaints through apparent nothing that bunker leadership reads as everything. This bank treats the note as institutional satire and pressure, not as a leadership mechanic or a request to add a complaint system.

## 2. Story question

When chalk under a leadership doorway says the council sheet has been read and that the doorway files complaints by doing nothing, how does a bunker hear a challenge that refuses to become a formal petition?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, trigger flag, minimum day, and weight. It does not establish the council sheet’s content, the leader, the chalk writer, the doorway’s previous complaints, or whether leadership understands the joke. The prose preserves the note’s double meaning and routes selections through existing escalation or graffiti consumers. The local record also makes the doorway a carefully chosen audience. The chalk is beneath leadership rather than inside a council room, so the complaint can be seen by someone who looks down without being granted the dignity of a formal agenda. The council sheet has been read, but reading has not become response. That gap is where the satire lives. The doorway is said to file complaints by doing nothing, a phrase that can mean repeated silence, accumulated delay, or the community’s habit of reading architecture as part of an institution. The plan should not decide which interpretation is correct. It should let a reader feel how a small mark can become larger when the formal channel has become difficult to trust. Nothing and everything are intentionally unstable words. Nothing can describe the lack of an answer; everything can describe the pressure that leadership hears in every quiet sign. A candidate scene may give the chalk satirist a dry joke, a tired hand, or a moment of regret after the mark is noticed. None of those possibilities establishes a named dissident or a complaint procedure. The council sheet remains an existing document reference whose contents are unknown. The doorway remains an image and a location, not a new governance actor. The acknowledged challenge flag can support an existing escalation or graffiti consumer, but it cannot become a new queue, petition, vote, reputation delta, or leadership interface. The best passages will keep the physical gesture small: a person pauses, looks at the chalk, and realizes that being read is not the same as being answered. Other people can disagree about whether the mark is clever, unfair, childish, or necessary. Their disagreement should remain human rather than procedural. The plan’s purpose is to widen the reading of the posting while preserving the source’s double meaning. **13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The proposed bank keeps returning to The Chalk That Asked because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention graffiti_challenge_to_the_lead by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

The strongest callback is often a correction. Someone says a word too confidently, another person supplies the source’s narrower wording, and the room continues. That is enough movement for a scene whose subject is memory.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

Where a source names an institution, show its paperwork and its consequences without making the institution omniscient. A form can be current and still miss a person. A schedule can be obeyed and still be unjust.

The handoff question is simple: can a reviewer point from each important claim to a current source and from each proposed passage to an existing consumer? If either answer is no, keep the passage in editorial review.

The document’s length does not widen the canon. It gives the writer room to choose among small moments at The Chalk That Asked while keeping the source boundary visible in every form. A longer bank is useful only when its alternatives remain honest and distinct.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.

The last editorial pass should ask whether the passage could belong to someone standing near The Chalk That Asked with limited time, imperfect memory, and no need to impress a reviewer. If the answer is yes, the bank has done its work: it has made a place available for play without pretending the place belongs to the plan.
