# EXPANSION CW59-02 — The Two Chalks of the Hallway

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 59: Chalk, Doors, and Return

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_on_the_two_sides — On The Two Sides Of The Hallway.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

On The Two Sides Of The Hallway is graffiti triggered by friction_radio_dial_argument, available from day 12 with weight 0.7. Its text says there are two sides, one with older chalk and one with newer chalk; the newer chalk is doing fine, the older chalk is just there, and the hallway is bigger than the chalk expects. This bank treats the wall as an argument about age, novelty, and the limits of taking a side. It preserves the posting’s humor and spatial image without adding faction geometry or a hallway system.

## 2. Story question

When older and newer chalk divide a hallway and the newest mark insists it is doing fine, how does a community keep a wall from becoming a map of permanent sides?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, trigger flag, minimum day, and weight. It does not establish the chalk writers, what the marks say, why the radio argument produced them, or which side is right. The prose keeps the hallway larger than its marks and uses only the existing graffiti or friction consumer. The local record also gives this chalk a useful restraint: it names a hallway without mapping the hallway. That distinction keeps the plan interested in the social life of a shared passage. Older chalk can mean memory, habit, or simply a mark that survived a cleaning that did not last. Newer chalk can mean answer, irritation, or a person trying to be visible before anyone has decided whether visibility is welcome. None of those readings needs to become a faction. The wall can hold two temporal layers and still belong to people who have not chosen a side. The friction flag supplies context for why a mark might appear, but it does not tell the prose who argued, what was said, or whether the radio was right. The plan therefore uses the radio as an absent pressure, a sound whose effects are visible after the dial has gone quiet. The corridor is allowed to be wider than the argument because other people still need to pass, carry water, pause, and return. Every candidate passage should preserve that ordinary traffic. A line of chalk may be noticed at eye level, beneath a pipe, or near a turn, but its exact placement remains a writerly option until an existing consumer and current visual canon confirm it. The central promise is modest: an argument can leave a trace without owning the space around it. The humor of saying the newer mark is doing fine should remain gentle. It can cover embarrassment, invite a reply, or simply be a joke made by someone who knows that confidence is easier to write than to sustain. The older mark is not a wise elder by default, and the newer mark is not progress by default. Their value comes from the fact that both are partial. The hallway gives the player permission to read across them, around them, or past them. In that sense the source is less a division than a warning against mistaking a visible division for the whole community. The prose may let a passerby clean a sleeve, lean close, or decline to add a third side. Those gestures remain observations, not operations. The data record stays the only authority for the anchor, trigger, day threshold, and weight. Any later implementation review should ask whether the selected passage keeps the joke, the uncertainty, and the shared floor intact. If it cannot, the passage should be shortened or left unused rather than repaired with invented history.
