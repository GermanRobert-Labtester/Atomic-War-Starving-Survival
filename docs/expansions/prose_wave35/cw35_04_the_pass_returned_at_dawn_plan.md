# EXPANSION CW35-04 — The Pass Returned at Dawn

## A stronghold story about a checkpoint document, an exhausted garrison, and the people behind the perimeter.

### Prose Wave 35: Places That Keep Their Questions

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first playable-content expansion plan with scene drafts, diegetic records, conversation fragments, and conditional callbacks.
**Content bank:** 48 story beats with four alternative authored forms per beat (192 candidate passages).
**Current location anchor:** `loc_settlement_fort_karkov` — Fort Karkov Marshalling Yard.
**Tone:** material, restrained, human, and careful with uncertainty.
**Canon sensitivity:** current location and linked authored records define known facts; old plans or JSON presence do not prove a current runtime route.
**Scope:** game-content plan and original prose drafts only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Fort Karkov is a militarized railhead bastion controlled by the Deserter Coalition. Its settlement record describes pass checks and disarming at the gate, then says the fort does not sleep and neither do its veterans; the two facts are related. The society record names dwindling medical stores as a source of rising friction among wounded garrison veterans. This plan stays with people and language around the gate rather than weapons or tactics: a pass is checked, a visitor waits, a watchful institution has to hear that its members are tired. No one is diagnosed and no supply consequence is simulated. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

What does a gate record about the people who guard it, and what does it leave outside the ledger?

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The fort is controlled by the Deserter Coalition. The settlement text describes pass checks and visitor disarming, sleepless veterans, and an internal tension involving dwindling medical stores and wounded garrison veterans. It does not name an individual, explain the shortage, define a treatment, or provide a gate procedure beyond the broad description. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the distance between a uniformed role and the body expected to stay awake behind it. A visitor can feel the gate as an institution; a veteran can feel it as another shift that never quite ends. Let the tension sit in a returned pass, a pause, or a sentence about being tired. Avoid making injury a test of loyalty. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A pass clerk uses exact, sparse language. A visitor asks a concrete question. A veteran’s voice is an editorial role only; no source character is created. Do not give any speaker weapon expertise, medical advice, or a general explanation of the Coalition. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Candidate passages may accompany the settlement record after route and dialogue consumers are verified. A pass line, waiting exchange, or notice is proposed content only, not a new permit, guard system, roster, or resource ledger. Existing faction access and trade owners keep all outcomes. All fragments are candidates. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. Before selecting any text, verify the current map/location owner, content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which the text can appear.

## 8. Player agency

The player may present themselves through current access authority, wait, ask what a line means, decline further conversation, or leave. The prose does not teach evasion or invite the player to take a side in the garrison’s internal shortage. Existing faction, medical, combat, and travel systems remain authoritative. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not detail weapons handling, perimeter weaknesses, guard rotations, or bypass methods. Do not diagnose insomnia or wounded veterans. Do not provide treatment or rationing instructions. Do not invent a named casualty, injury, medical supply count, or a pass consequence. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Gate — the pass is checked

Establish the source’s broad gate practice without reproducing tactics.

### 2. Return — a visitor gets the document back

Use the pass as a proposed text object; do not create a permit system.

### 3. Interior — the fort keeps watch

Let the militarized railhead describe institutional posture, not battle action.

### 4. Human cost — the veterans are named collectively

Acknowledge sleeplessness and the recorded medical-store friction without inventing diagnosis.

### 5. Conversation — what the gate does not record

A visitor asks a question, and the answer stops at what a speaker can know.

### 6. Dawn — the document leaves with its holder

End at the exchange of a pass, without claiming the fort or its people have rested.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use a returned pass and one short exchange. Keep the garrison’s internal conditions attributed to settlement text.

### Interlinked

If a verified current access route exposes faction standing, use only its existing result; do not infer a new pass rule.

### Wild card

Write the same gate moment as clerk note, visitor recollection, and an unsigned line about the shift that continued.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
