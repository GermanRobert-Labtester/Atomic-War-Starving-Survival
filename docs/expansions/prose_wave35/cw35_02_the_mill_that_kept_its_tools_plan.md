# EXPANSION CW35-02 — The Mill That Kept Its Tools

## A forestry-yard story about craft records that outlast a silent machine.

### Prose Wave 35: Places That Keep Their Questions

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Forestry Compound is a pre-war timber station and log sorting yard in the northern pines. Its diesel sawmills are silent, while maintenance sheds retain spare parts and tools. Five antler-and-horn sawing records are associated with the compound as discovery producers; they document separate work entries, not evidence that the sawmills are running or that their anonymous writer worked at this site. This plan builds game prose from the contrast between an idle industrial yard and meticulous small-material notes. Its people notice what was maintained, what was set aside, and what a record preserves without turning craft text into a how-to system. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

When the large machine falls silent, what kind of work still counts as keeping a place alive?

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The compound is a forestry station and log yard; its diesel sawmills stand silent and sheds hold tools and spare parts. Five antler/horn logs are linked through the manifest. The catalog records small-work entries but no named maker or explicit statement that they were written at this compound. No mill operation, production rate, or working crew is established. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The people at the center are not a heroic crew restoring a machine. They are readers deciding which work a place remembers: the log yard’s scale, a small blank of material set aside, a blade mentioned as wearing, a tool returned to a shed. Let competence appear through attention and limits. Do not reduce the anonymous makers to an industrial “labor system.” The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A yard reader describes timber, shed doors, and a silent mill without inventing a foreman. The author of each antler log remains unknown unless a separate source provides a name. A tool custodian is an editorial role, not proof of an existing NPC. Craft records can be paraphrased as evidence of a recorded task, never expanded into a tutorial. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player may inspect the quiet yard, compare a proposed work note with an existing record, ask who wrote it, or leave the attribution blank. They are not required to restart the mill or choose a preferred form of labor. Existing crafting, inventory, expedition, and discovery owners retain any real consequence. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not provide step-by-step saw, boiling, shaping, or tool-use instructions from the logs. Do not claim antler work happened in the forestry compound unless a record says so. Do not state that any shed tool is safe, usable, or compatible with a machine. No animal harvest or forest ecology facts are invented. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Yard — the large work has stopped

Open on the silent diesel sawmills and the log-sorting space. Do not add an engine restart scene.

### 2. Shed — parts and tools remain

A reader encounters retained equipment without claiming its current condition.

### 3. Small record — work in another scale

A linked antler/horn log offers an authored record of small craft. Keep its source author and worksite unknown.

### 4. Attribution — five entries, not one life

Compare record IDs as catalog entries; do not merge material types, sessions, or hands.

### 5. Question — what counts as maintenance

A visitor asks whether keeping tools is work even when the large mill stays still.

### 6. Return — no machine is promised

Close with an intact note and a silent mill. The story does not make either one a feature state.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use the location description and one verified small-work log; present any new tag as editorial copy.

### Interlinked

If a current content consumer exposes the five records, preserve each source ID and do not attribute them to a named compound worker.

### Wild card

Write the scene as a tool’s return note, but make clear the tool has no sourced identity or canonical memory.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
