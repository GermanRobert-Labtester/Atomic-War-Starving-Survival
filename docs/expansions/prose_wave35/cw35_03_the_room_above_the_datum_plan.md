# EXPANSION CW35-03 — The Room Above the Datum

## A tower story about an inaccessible lamp room and a question that need not become a key.

### Prose Wave 35: Places That Keep Their Questions

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first playable-content expansion plan with scene drafts, diegetic records, conversation fragments, and conditional callbacks.
**Content bank:** 48 story beats with four alternative authored forms per beat (192 candidate passages).
**Current location anchor:** `loc_south_beacon_tower` — South Beacon Tower Ruin.
**Tone:** material, restrained, human, and careful with uncertainty.
**Canon sensitivity:** current location and linked authored records define known facts; old plans or JSON presence do not prove a current runtime route.
**Scope:** game-content plan and original prose drafts only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The South Beacon Tower Ruin is partially collapsed. Its lower floors are accessible and serve as a survey datum; the lamp room is not accessible, and the location says nobody has stopped wondering what is up there. This plan treats the inaccessible room as a boundary with narrative force, not a locked feature asking to be solved. The people below can disagree about what the lamp once did, but the source does not tell us what remains above. The game content gives players several ways to notice, ask, speculate, or turn back without converting any of those responses into a promised route. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

Can a place keep a mystery without making every visitor responsible for opening it?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The location record explicitly distinguishes accessible lower floors used as a survey datum from an inaccessible lamp room. It does not say why access is impossible, what lies inside, whether the lamp remains, or whether the tower once guided a specific voyage. The description supplies persistent curiosity, not an unlock condition.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The tower is partially collapsed. Lower floors are accessible and serve as a survey datum. The lamp room is not accessible. The location’s final sentence says people still wonder about it. Nothing says what is in that room, why it cannot be entered, or what would happen if someone tried. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The people at the center are readers who are comfortable asking a question and leaving it unanswered. A surveyor can use the lower floors without promising access above. A visitor can wonder without claiming to know. The tower should not punish a character for respecting the boundary or imply that curiosity is always a demand for entry. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A datum reader speaks in references and limits; a visitor speaks in guesses clearly marked as guesses. No named surveyor or keeper is in the source. Do not give the lamp room a voice, invent sounds from behind it, or narrate an interior no one can access. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Candidate text may accompany the location description after current route and consumer verification. Proposed margin notes, repeated questions, and lower-floor observations are drafts only. This plan adds no key, repair, access route, climb mechanic, map coordinate, or hidden room content. All fragments are candidates. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. Before selecting any text, verify the current map/location owner, content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which the text can appear.

## 8. Player agency

The player may use the known lower-floor description, ask what can be known, share a guess, refuse to speculate, or leave. No action is treated as failure to complete an upper-room objective. Current route and exploration state remain with the owner. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not describe how to enter a collapsed tower or climb inaccessible structure. Do not invent the lamp’s contents, failure, signal, or keeper. Do not reward trespass or turn a safety boundary into a puzzle. Do not provide survey instructions or coordinates. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Lower floor — the known point

Establish the accessible area and its datum role without detailing a route or measurement method.

### 2. Question — what is above

Let the location’s stated curiosity reach the player but keep every imagined answer attributed to a speaker.

### 3. Boundary — inaccessible means not described

The text refuses to stage an interior or explain why access is impossible.

### 4. Conversation — speculation stays labeled

Different visitors can invent guesses, then correct each other when guesses sound like facts.

### 5. Choice — leave the question alone

The player may stop, and the prose treats that as a complete response.

### 6. Return — the datum remains below

A later line can revisit the question without changing tower access or supplying a reveal.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Keep the entire visit on the described lower floors and outside the lamp room.

### Interlinked

Only include verified location or exploration state; do not create a branch that exposes upper-room content.

### Wild card

Offer three anonymous guesses on a proposed card, each crossed out as speculation rather than clue.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
