## 1. Expansion thesis

The Bedrock Sings expands a liturgy about ten minutes of Geiger clicks, musical transcription, improvised instruments, atonal performance, and the belief that radiation composes unique scripture. The plan treats the canticle as collaborative interpretation without adding dosimeter operation, radiation exposure, music-generation, or revelation mechanics.

## 2. Story question

When Geiger clicks are transcribed as notes and performed on cups, pipes, and a generator, who is the composer: the stone, the instrument, or the people listening for a pattern?

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains liturgy_b2_canticle_of_the_geiger_psalm (The Bedrock Sings). The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Bedrock Sings from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Bedrock Sings record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Bedrock Sings should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. **09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention liturgy_b2_canticle_of_the_geiger_psalm or the surrounding region. **11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. **12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Bedrock Sings should be felt as a change in attention. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.
