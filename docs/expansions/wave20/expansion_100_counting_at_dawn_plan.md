# EXPANSION 100 — Counting at Dawn

## A prose-led follow-through for Anton Renn’s herd count, the well no one has asked about, and the word that costs more than milk.

### Wave 20: Terms of Staying

## 1. Expansion thesis

Keep the herd story with Anton and the people who live from its work. His existing quest offers a public, shared count at dawn or the option to take milk and leave his account undisturbed. Supplemental prose can deepen both outcomes while refusing to turn an animal decision, a well, or a ledger discrepancy into a diagnostic tutorial. This is a prose-first game-content plan: the main deliverable is scene text, dialogue, records, and state-aware return passages. Any proposed prose is supplemental to the current authored content and remains a proposal until accepted through the game's existing narrative owner.

## 2. Story in one sentence

Anton’s herd and its ledger disagree, and the player can help him count what the word “cull” costs without pretending the arithmetic is kind.

## 3. Verified local anchor and current story

`characters.json` defines `npc_anton_renn` as the stockman at `loc_grain_silo` in the allotment region. His record says the herd is smaller than the ledger, the milk is thinner than his story, and he has begun dosing the well; it does not say what is used or why. `npc_arcs.json` contains initial, evolved, late-honest, late-blamed, and unmet states. `quests_npc_arcs.json` contains `quest_arc_anton_01_herd`; `narrative_encounters_npc_arcs.json` contains `enc_arc_anton_01_herd`. The existing choice is to do the cull arithmetic together at dawn or say nothing, take milk off the top, and leave his story intact. Later state text distinguishes an honest smaller herd, a public sickness-and-ledger gap, and an unmet conversation. Those are current authored outcomes and must not be blended. The JSON record proves that these names, places, and story conditions are authored; it does not by itself prove a route is currently reachable in every campaign. Keep the plan's proposed insertions subordinate to the live content and its current-state conditions.

## 4. Fixed canon and proposed text

The Grain Exchange location is a neutral market under the leaning silo; its scale is described as fair, and food moves through it in large share of the district. Anton’s source states what he wants and will not sell. It does not establish why the herd shrank, the well’s substance, or a causal link between the well and sickness. The quest’s cull outcome is non-graphic and counted aloud. Existing quest IDs, choice IDs, character states, names, location descriptions, and branch conditions remain the authority. Every additional visitor, line, paper, gesture, or callback below is proposed writing, not new established history.

## 5. Human center

Anton is not hiding from arithmetic because he cannot count. The source says he knows what the word costs. The scene lets him keep care and avoidance in the same person: he does not sell stock he knows is sick, but he also asks to keep the story that the herd is fine. The player can witness and respond, but the character's life does not become a demonstration designed for the player's moral growth. Let material detail carry emotion; do not tell the player which feeling to have.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Anton repeats himself when the question is too close, then changes the subject to what the visitor needs. He is a stockman, not a public speaker. Let him talk about the animal in front of him and the next thing he can do. Neighbors can speak from what they observed, not from invented expertise. Keep narration close to evidence: what someone counts, moves, refuses to sign, or leaves within reach. People speak from their job and their fatigue. Nobody explains their own symbolism.

## 7. Placement in the current story

Keep every fragment within the existing `quest_arc_anton_01_herd` and its current state variants. The public market, the fence, and dawn are source-supported anchors. This plan adds no disease event, animal-health system, well-treatment procedure, supply item, or livestock number. The sequence below proposes alternate or additional prose for existing content surfaces. It does not introduce a parallel quest chain, duplicate a registered encounter, or promise a new arrival. Use the current quest or location owner to decide where an accepted line belongs.

## 8. Player agency and consequence

The current player choice distinguishes a shared count from leaving the story intact and taking the milk. The proposed prose may show the cost of each without declaring that silence is cruelty or the cull is triumph. Allow the player to ask about the well, accept that Anton will not answer fully, and leave. Preserve the player's ability to help, refuse, wait, or leave. Consequence should be visible in an authored state that already exists or in text conditional on an existing branch; do not imply new state from a line alone.

## 9. Dignity, safety, and scope

Keep animal and water content restrained. No graphic culling, dosing advice, disease diagnosis, or unsupported safety claim. Do not link the well to a sickness without explicit source evidence. Keep every hazardous, medical, private, or coercive subject within the boundaries of the source. The prose can show limits and uncertainty without explaining a dangerous workaround, exposing a hidden person's identity, or making an unverified treatment promise.

## 10. Content integration boundary

This plan adds no production code, JSON, quest ID, encounter ID, location, faction, route, resource rule, score, or save field. If an editor later selects a fragment, verify the existing content schema and state consumer first, then place it with the current owning data. The plan itself is review material only.

## 11. Content bank: proposed prose

The following beats each have four editorial forms: a scene, a diegetic record, an optional conversation, and a return vignette. They are alternatives for the same moment, not four mandatory encounters. Select a coherent subset and keep each fragment inside the current character state described above.
