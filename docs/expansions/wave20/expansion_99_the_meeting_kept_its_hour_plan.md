# EXPANSION 99 — The Meeting Kept Its Hour

## A prose-led vote and aftermath for Ivor Lasko, the Verge militia, and a hall whose neutral rules outlast individual certainty.

### Wave 20: Terms of Staying

## 1. Expansion thesis

Give the Grange Hall’s vote enough room to be a civic act rather than a shortcut to a combat scene. Ivor waits for the neighbors to decide whether he will be returned under a publicly posted standing order. The expansion develops the room, the people carrying the question, and the aftermath without making the vote’s result current canon. This is a prose-first game-content plan: the main deliverable is scene text, dialogue, records, and state-aware return passages. Any proposed prose is supplemental to the current authored content and remains a proposal until accepted through the game's existing narrative owner.

## 2. Story in one sentence

In a hall that has never missed a meeting, a deserter waits outside the table’s reach while his neighbors decide what the posted order means for them.

## 3. Verified local anchor and current story

`characters.json` defines `npc_ivor_lasko` as a Garrison deserter hiding in a Verge outbuilding. It says the Militia is holding a vote on whether to return him, Voss’s standing order is publicly posted and unambiguous, and Ivor waits rather than running. `locations.json` defines `loc_grange_hall`: an oil-lit neutral meeting hall, a porch sign requesting weapons be left outside, and an attendance ledger that has not skipped a meeting since the first week after the Exchange. `world_history.json` includes the Iron Garrison’s order that deserters will be shot. No NPC arc, registered quest, or encounter keyed to Ivor was found in the inspected current catalogs. The vote is stated in Ivor’s character biography, but the current data inspected does not provide its ballot, procedure, or result. Any vote scene, abstention, result, or callback in this plan is new proposed prose and must remain visibly proposed until authored by the current narrative owner. The JSON record proves that these names, places, and story conditions are authored; it does not by itself prove a route is currently reachable in every campaign. Keep the plan's proposed insertions subordinate to the live content and its current-state conditions.

## 4. Fixed canon and proposed text

The Hall is neutral and its weapon-leaving request is honored. Attendance is recorded and meetings continue. Ivor does not run or beg. The standing order is posted; this plan does not rewrite it or reveal new command details. The vote’s procedure and outcome are not established in current data. Existing quest IDs, choice IDs, character states, names, location descriptions, and branch conditions remain the authority. Every additional visitor, line, paper, gesture, or callback below is proposed writing, not new established history.

## 5. Human center

A vote can protect the people who hold it, or expose them to the next authority that asks what they decided. Nobody in the hall has to pretend this is an abstract question. The proposed writing keeps their uncertainty and their responsibility in the room rather than granting the player a speech that resolves it for them. The player can witness and respond, but the character's life does not become a demonstration designed for the player's moral growth. Let material detail carry emotion; do not tell the player which feeling to have.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Ivor answers without begging and does not make a performance of refusing to run. Hall members speak from different kinds of risk: fear of a reprisal, fear of becoming the people who deliver a neighbor, and fear of a rule that can be used against them later. The chair or clerk uses plain meeting language, not grand declarations. Keep narration close to evidence: what someone counts, moves, refuses to sign, or leaves within reach. People speak from their job and their fatigue. Nobody explains their own symbolism.

## 7. Placement in the current story

Treat this as a proposed extension of the character-bio premise, not an existing quest. The scene can be staged only if the current narrative owner adds a valid content surface. Use the existing Grange Hall description as the setting and leave the formal vote procedure and outcome open for an implementation decision. No new faction, oath, standing order, or execution scene is proposed. The sequence below proposes alternate or additional prose for existing content surfaces. It does not introduce a parallel quest chain, duplicate a registered encounter, or promise a new arrival. Use the current quest or location owner to decide where an accepted line belongs.

## 8. Player agency and consequence

The player can listen, speak within an authored choice, decline to influence the vote, or leave. The writing should not imply that the player’s word secretly counts twice. If the eventual content has a result, its authority and consequences must be established separately; this plan supplies possible text tones, not an outcome selector or new reputation variable. Preserve the player's ability to help, refuse, wait, or leave. Consequence should be visible in an authored state that already exists or in text conditional on an existing branch; do not imply new state from a line alone.

## 9. Dignity, safety, and scope

Keep the story nonviolent in presentation. Do not turn the character into a target, make a threat into spectacle, or instruct a player how to intercept a person. Leave the resolution to future authored content and current faction authority. Keep every hazardous, medical, private, or coercive subject within the boundaries of the source. The prose can show limits and uncertainty without explaining a dangerous workaround, exposing a hidden person's identity, or making an unverified treatment promise.

## 10. Content integration boundary

This plan adds no production code, JSON, quest ID, encounter ID, location, faction, route, resource rule, score, or save field. If an editor later selects a fragment, verify the existing content schema and state consumer first, then place it with the current owning data. The plan itself is review material only.

## 11. Content bank: proposed prose

The following beats each have four editorial forms: a scene, a diegetic record, an optional conversation, and a return vignette. They are alternatives for the same moment, not four mandatory encounters. Select a coherent subset and keep each fragment inside the current character state described above.
