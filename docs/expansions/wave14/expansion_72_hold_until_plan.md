# EXPANSION 72 — HOLD UNTIL
## An Evidence Sub-Basement Story Plan
### Wave 14: Authority Without a Witness

**Document type:** prose-first game-content expansion plan  
**Primary location anchor:** Evidence Sub-Basement  
**Zone anchor:** Court District  
**Status:** proposed narrative content, subject to current source and canon review  
**Wave theme:** an old record can preserve custody after the people empowered to end it are gone  
**Scope:** authored dialogue, scenes, present-day notes, player choices, and journal outcomes through current narrative owners  
**Non-goal:** no new legal, inventory, evidence-custody, access, or save system

## 1. The story in one sentence

A courthouse transfer sheet says property must be held “until trial or dissolution”; the player helps a present-day community distinguish that old order from what it owes to people now.

## 2. Creative promise

The player reads fragments of a justice archive with people who have lived on both sides of keeping and needing. A stores keeper wants a coat on the drying line to help someone cold tonight. A neighbor remembers placing a similar coat there but cannot prove it is the same one. A record keeper has learned that “unclaimed” describes a file, not a person.

The story uses this small present-day disagreement to make the old court’s language matter. The player does not discover a named defendant, a famous case, or a stash of proven valuables. They help the group make a careful note about an unresolved shelf and a separate, immediate choice in their own community.

The emotional reward is a shared understanding that custody can be responsible and still become harmful when “until” loses its ending.

## 3. Verified local source anchors

The Court District damaged-map zone names the Evidence Sub-Basement. Its description says the courthouse evidence store was sunk below the record halls, inventoried to the box, and sealed for the evacuation. It says the freight lift was stripped for copper and the custody ledgers list every shelf that was never claimed.

The fragments are:
- Property Index: an index-card sheet maps case numbers to sub-basement shelving rows; later entries are blurred, while row K remains readable.
- Service Corridor: a courthouse fire-escape plan shows a records corridor absent from public floor plans; mold marks the margin.
- Transfer Sheet: a typed transfer authorization has a torn corner and reads, “Sub-basement, row K — hold until trial or dissolution.” Clerk initials follow.

The location entry describes subterranean storage in a pre-war justice archive, with steel security cages holding confiscated contraband, logbooks, and salvageable trade goods behind tamper-sealed gates. Current records give danger 3, travel 1.5 hours, and 15 base rads per hour. They do not establish any specific object or claimant in row K.

## 4. Current map and custody boundary

The world-map node is currently locked, not discoverable at start, and not starting-unlocked. Current cartography documentation places this zone in the existing damaged-map reveal chain. This story does not unlock the node, draw the service corridor as a route, or bypass any fragment requirement.

The freight lift being stripped for copper is part of the Court District installation description. The story does not restore it, navigate a replacement route, open security gates, or claim that the interior has been entered.

If the content has no current journal or dialogue route after fragment discovery, report that gap. Do not build a parallel quest or case-management system to deliver this story.

## 5. The legal-language boundary

The phrase “hold until trial or dissolution” is quoted from the transfer-sheet fragment. The data does not identify the court, governing law, owner, defendant, trial, dissolution, or whether either condition occurred. “Dissolution” is not explained.

The expansion does not interpret the sentence as a current legal order, a permanent seizure, an automatic release, or permission to salvage. It is evidence that a transfer authorization used a time-bound phrase. The end condition is absent from the fragment description.

Likewise, “never claimed” is wording in the installation description about shelves in custody ledgers. It does not prove that the property lacked an owner, that no person tried to claim it, or that the entries remain unclaimed today.

## 6. The present-day coat

The current group’s coat is a newly authored scene object. It is not from the Evidence Sub-Basement, does not appear in the historical fragments, and is not a loot result. It has sat on the camp drying line after someone left it there during a wet week.

Neve Arlo, the stores keeper, wants to lend it for one cold watch. Oren Pell thinks it may belong to a neighbor who left without saying whether they would return. Tessa Vaun is the person assigned to the watch and does not want to wear something that could start a fight.

The object is a narrative prop only. The story does not add an item, inventory transfer, clothing stat, or persistent property ledger.

## 7. Why the two questions stay separate

The court fragments concern old evidence custody in a sealed sub-basement. The coat concerns a present-day decision by people who know each other and can ask follow-up questions. The court’s transfer sheet does not govern the coat, and the coat does not reveal what row K contains.

The juxtaposition is thematic, not a continuity link. A journal note must say so if it discusses both:
> The court record and the camp coat raised similar questions about custody. They are separate matters.

This sentence protects the story from turning the new scene into a claim about historical contents.

## 8. Character: Neve Arlo

Neve maintains the community’s ordinary shared supplies. She is good at keeping objects usable and poor at leaving a decision open when someone needs help now.

She believes that a temporary hold should always include a reason a tired person can understand. “Until further notice” feels to her like the beginning of forgetting. She is not careless with property; her impatience comes from seeing useful things remain untouched because nobody wants to own the responsibility of saying what happens next.

Her voice is direct:
> “If it is on the line, somebody needs it dry.”
>
> “Does that make it yours to lend?”
>
> “No. It makes it my problem to ask.”

## 9. Character: Oren Pell

Oren remembers a neighbor leaving a coat near the drying line, but cannot say whether the current coat is the same one. He feels responsible for preserving a possibility without claiming a certainty.

He does not identify the absent neighbor, explain their departure, or connect them to the old courthouse. Oren’s memory is intentionally limited: he remembers a gesture, not ownership.

> “I saw someone leave a coat.”
>
> “This one?”
>
> “I do not know.”
>
> “Would they come back for it?”
>
> “I do not know that either.”
>
> “Then what do you know?”
>
> “That I should not make my memory sound like a label.”

## 10. Character: Tessa Vaun

Tessa is scheduled for the cold watch and has already repaired her own cuffs. She does not want the group to turn her need into a claim of ownership.

She asks for a clear answer because she needs to prepare. Neve’s offer to lend the coat is kind, but Tessa worries that accepting it will make someone else’s absence disappear.

Her voice is gentle and practical:
> “I can be cold for one more shift.”
>
> “That is not the point.”
>
> “It is part of the point.”
>
> “What is the rest?”
>
> “I do not want help that makes somebody else invisible.”

## 11. Character: Ilian Sorn

Ilian keeps the group’s written records and has been comparing the newly found court fragments. He believes “unclaimed” should always be attributed to the record that uses it.

Ilian does not know the court system or claim that the old transfer sheet would be enforceable today. He can explain the difference between a shelf number and a person’s name, but he is not a legal authority.

His recurring question is:
> “Who gets to say that the file is finished?”

The answer in this story is: no one present can answer that about the old cases.

## 12. Relationship map

Neve and Oren disagree about what a careful temporary decision looks like. Neve fears that indefinite holding becomes neglect; Oren fears that useful action can erase the person who might return. Tessa refuses to let her need serve as proof that no one else has a claim.

Ilian uses the court fragments to name the historical limits but is careful not to bring old legal authority into the present camp dispute. The player is the person who can help them keep those two settings apart.

There is no morality score for lending or holding the coat. Each choice affects the characters’ dialogue and the final wording of the present-day note only if the current narrative owner supports such a choice.

## 13. Narrative arc

**Act I — The word is found.** The player reads “hold until trial or dissolution” and hears how indefinite it sounds without the rest of the file.

**Act II — The row is read.** The property index and transfer sheet are compared. The group distinguishes a shelf, a case number, an item, and a person.

**Act III — The present repeats the question.** The camp coat brings custody into an immediate human setting without becoming evidence about the court.

**Act IV — The note ends where authority ends.** The player chooses what to preserve: the exact clause, the fact that its end condition is unknown, and the present group’s separate decision.

## 45. Act II close: the old order has no ending

By the end of the second movement, the group understands that the transfer sheet preserved a condition but not its resolution. Row K remains a shelving reference; the index description does not give them its contents or a person’s name.

The word “unclaimed” remains attributed to the custody-ledger summary. The coat remains a separate present-day object. The player sees that the old record can teach the group to ask when a hold ends without deciding who owns anything today.

The next act brings the story back to the drying line, where the group has an opportunity to practice those questions with people who can still answer.



## 46. Act III: return to the coat

After the archive discussion, the player returns to the drying line. Neve has not moved the coat. Tessa has replaced the threadbare wrap around her shoulders. Oren is still trying to remember who left something similar near the same line.

- What is known about this particular coat?
- Who remembers it being left?
- What did that person say?
- How long should the group wait before asking again?
- What can Tessa accept without pretending she owns it?

The player guides a conversation, not a property hearing.
