# EXPANSION CW53-05 — The Records Below Water

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 53: Places That Remember

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** codex_regions_the_drown — The Drown.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Drown is the industrial flatland to the west. When lock gates lost power, the river reclaimed land kept dry for ninety years, carrying streets, buildings, and basements into a new depth. No faction claims the region. Its deepest pre-war records survived because nobody could reach them. This bank treats the Drown as geography, archive, and absence at once. It does not turn submerged records into a diving mission or a loot route; it lets inaccessible memory shape people who live above it.

## 2. Story question

When a river takes back an industrial district and the deepest pre-war records survive precisely because nobody can reach them, what does inaccessible memory do to a living place?

### Editorial scale notes

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains codex_regions_the_drown (The Drown). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Drown from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Drown record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Drown should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. The later reader may notice a different word, remember a different cost, or decline to repeat the account. The catalog facts remain stable unless a current authorized system says otherwise.

**06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Drown should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. Let a person have a practical task, a private limit, a bad day, a memory that is incomplete, or a reason not to answer. Keep the human center larger than the prop.

**08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention codex_regions_the_drown or the surrounding region. Confirm names, truth classes, branch outcomes, and display ownership. A matching phrase is not proof that a passage remains current.

**10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide diving, salvage, watercraft, flood, lock-gate, archive recovery, route, depth, swimming, engineering, or underwater survival instructions. Do not add a submerged-map system, archive loot table, flood simulation, or faction authority. Preserve the codex’s inaccessible records and broad regional image. Keep that limit visible in scene language. A character may be afraid, cautious, angry, or wrong without the prose becoming an operational guide.

**11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. It may be a torn page, a spoken recollection, a label, a pause at a threshold, or a later sentence repeated with one word corrected. It should not look like a system specification inside the fiction.

**12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Drown should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Drown because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention codex_regions_the_drown by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

The strongest callback is often a correction. Someone says a word too confidently, another person supplies the source’s narrower wording, and the room continues. That is enough movement for a scene whose subject is memory.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

Where a source names an institution, show its paperwork and its consequences without making the institution omniscient. A form can be current and still miss a person. A schedule can be obeyed and still be unjust.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.
