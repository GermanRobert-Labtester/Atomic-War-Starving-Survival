# EXPANSION CW34-02 — The Board Updated for Nobody

## A junction story about schedules that survive the audience they were meant to serve.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Nine Rails Junction is a sheltered concourse where four industrial spurs meet. Allocation clerks register freight weights; armed merchants barter scrap hardware under floodlamps; the board is updated by hand every morning, although nobody has said who it is for. The Scheduling Board values timetable adherence, ledger accuracy, and transit order, while independent scavengers strain that order by bypassing freight weighing. This plan follows the small public acts around an unanswered board: a line copied, a weight disputed, a destination left empty. It does not invent a working railway or an unseen passenger. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

What is a timetable for when the people maintaining it will not say who is expected to read it?

## 5. Human center

The people at the center keep making a public mark after its practical use has become uncertain. A clerk can care about a correct line without knowing whether anyone will travel. A carrier can challenge the scale without becoming a villain. Keep the disagreement specific and ordinary. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A Scheduling Board clerk uses dates and nouns carefully but cannot explain the board’s audience. An independent carrier speaks as someone moving goods, not as an ideological manifesto. A trader wants a weight read back. All voices are editorial roles, not source characters. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player may inspect the board, ask who maintains it, question a weight, or leave. They need not arbitrate every dispute. Existing trade, access, travel, reputation, and faction outcomes keep their owners. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not name a train, destination, operator, date, or passenger. Do not invent a platform number, manifest, dispatch, or reason the board persists. Do not assign motives beyond the recorded bypass of weighing. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Morning — the hand makes the mark

Open with the daily act of updating the board; add no clock beyond every morning.

### 2. Weight — the settlement asks for a number

A clerk records freight weight while a merchant waits; this is prose around the source, not a transaction rule.

### 3. Dispute — the scale has an edge

An independent carrier bypasses weighing. The disagreement is practical and does not decide the carrier’s motive.

### 4. Audience — a question nobody owns

Different people know who writes the board but cannot say who it is for.

### 5. Interpretation — order without guarantee

Timetable adherence is a value, not proof of actual departures.

### 6. Return — the empty column remains

Another morning’s update does not identify the waiting audience.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Center one weight disagreement and a board update; leave its audience unanswered.

### Interlinked

Only verified route or arrival state from its current owner may inform selected text. The board is not route authority.

### Wild card

Write the same board line for clerk, trader, and carrier; each is honest about what they cannot see.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
