# EXPANSION CW34-05 — The Knock That Is Enough

## A sanctuary story where attention is offered without conversation.

### Prose Wave 34: Unanswered Measures

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first playable-content expansion plan with scene drafts, diegetic records, conversation fragments, and conditional callbacks.
**Content bank:** 48 story beats with four alternative authored forms per beat (192 candidate passages).

## 1. Expansion thesis

St. Nicholas Spring Sanctuary is an underground artesian spring beneath old crypts and a monastic hospital. Silent caretakers offer clean water and burn care without conversation. Visitors learn to knock softly. The source says the spring needs no tending and is tended anyway. The Quiet House Order values silent contemplation, ritual cleansing, and nonviolence; its society record notes industrial runoff tension. This plan uses silence as an offered boundary. It lets a knock, a folded cloth, and a visitor’s choice carry the story without turning care into guaranteed treatment or speaking for the caretakers. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

How can a visitor recognize care when the people offering it have chosen not to explain themselves?

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Caretakers are silent and offer care without conversation. Visitors learn to knock softly. The spring is described as needing no tending while care continues. The Quiet House Order is linked to contemplation, ritual cleansing, and nonviolence; the society record notes runoff tension. No cure, purity test, supernatural protection, or guaranteed water quality is established. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

A visitor tries to ask without making an answer compulsory. Care may be legible in what is offered and in space left for refusal. The caretaker is not a riddle or angelic symbol. Visitors can misunderstand, wait, accept, or leave. Protect both the visitor’s need and the caretaker’s silence. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

No dialogue is attributed to a caretaker. Conversation fragments are between visitors, or a visitor thinking aloud while another decides whether to answer. Caretakers communicate only through source-supported silence and clearly proposed actions. Do not claim to know their vows or motives. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Candidate prose may accompany the location after its consumer is verified. A knock or visitor note is not an interaction system. No medical service, treatment outcome, water treatment, contamination model, doctrine, or patient state is added. All fragments are candidates. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. Before selecting any text, verify the current map/location owner, content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which the text can appear.

## 8. Player agency

The player may knock, wait, accept an offer only through existing authority, decline, or leave. Silence is not coercion and does not make a visitor owe a confession. Actual access, healing, inventory, faction, and water outcomes remain with existing owners. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not provide burn-care instructions, dosing, wound classification, or claims of sterile treatment. Do not assert water is safe because it is called clean or holy. Do not identify runoff source or severity. Never put spoken words in a caretaker’s mouth or interpret silence as approval or consent. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Threshold — a soft knock

Begin with a visitor learning the soft knock; do not name a teacher.

### 2. Offer — water and burn care

The offer is sourced; no procedure or outcome is invented.

### 3. Interpretation — another visitor waits

A visitor may wonder what silence means; the other explains only what they observed.

### 4. Pressure — spring and outside are distinct

Place the runoff tension beside the spring description without claiming current water condition.

### 5. Practice — tending without a claim of need

A proposed gesture shows care without inventing engineering or doctrine.

### 6. Return — leave without a verdict

The visitor chooses what is enough; the caretaker remains silent.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use a knock, pause, and named offer of care. No caretaker dialogue or medical detail.

### Interlinked

Mention service outcome only if its live owner confirms the exact effect; otherwise remain descriptive.

### Wild card

Write three visitors’ notes about a knock. Each describes action, none translates silence.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
