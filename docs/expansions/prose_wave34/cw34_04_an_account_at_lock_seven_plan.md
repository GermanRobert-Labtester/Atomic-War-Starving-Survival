## A sluice-fortress story about the words people use when passage has a price.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Lock Seven Hydraulic Bastion is described as a reinforced concrete sluice fortress controlling a canal. Tally enforcers take fuel and machine parts as a gate fee. Downriver, people call it a toll; at Lock Seven, the same demand is an account, and accounts are kept. Independent barges refuse the fee. This plan does not decide whether the word is explanation, justification, or both. One population field conflicts with a nested value in the local record, so no headcount is used. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

When a fee is called an account, does the new word explain the exchange or ask the payer to accept its authority?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The record names a concrete sluice fortress, Tally authority, a gate fee paid in fuel and machine parts, the downriver word toll, Lock Seven’s word account, and independent refusal. Society values include contract enforcement, hydraulic dominance, and debt collection. Top-level and nested population values conflict (85 versus 150); this plan treats the field as unresolved and avoids both counts. No canal operation or legal basis is defined.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Stated values include contract enforcement, hydraulic dominance, and debt collection. Two population numbers conflict; neither is repeated as narrative fact. The source does not establish fee formula, legal basis, result, or machinery operation. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The center is the moment a word arrives before a person can answer. An enforcer may use the institution’s term; a barge representative may use the word of the person giving up fuel. A clerk can be exact without being neutral. The tension comes from hearing both descriptions without assigning private motives. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

The clerk says account as an institutional term; a visitor says toll. Neither becomes narrator fact. A barge representative asks for the line to be read back. All are editorial voices. Avoid legal lectures, villain monologues, and hydraulic detail. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Candidate prose may accompany the location only after reachability and consumer are verified. Account chits and notices are drafts, not actual items or contract state. No toll calculator, debt ledger, water-control action, enforcement rule, or standing result is proposed. All fragments are candidates. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. Before selecting any text, verify the current map/location owner, content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which the text can appear.

## 8. Player agency

The player may ask which word a speaker used, listen, decline to carry a message, or leave. They need not decide who owns the canal. Trade, faction, route, and debt outcomes keep existing authority. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not give instructions for operating, bypassing, damaging, or sabotaging a sluice. Do not state fees, penalties, interest, debt balance, or violence absent from source. Do not infer settlement size. Refusal is not automatically theft; enforcement is not proof of lawful authority. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Approach — the fortress is named

Establish the sluice fortress without mapping passage or machinery.

### 2. Demand — fuel and parts are named

State the source goods without calculating a fee.

### 3. Argument — toll and account diverge

Attribute both words to speakers.

### 4. Refusal — an independent barge declines

Show refusal without inventing pursuit or consequence.

### 5. Review — authority remains disputed

Preserve words while leaving legal and moral status open.

### 6. Return — no ledger signs for everybody

A later reader sees the language, not a universal outcome.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use one fee notice and a two-person disagreement; specify no amount.

### Interlinked

Use an existing trade or faction outcome only if its owner exposes it.

### Wild card

Copy one notice twice, in clerk and downriver hands; the noun changes with its speaker.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
