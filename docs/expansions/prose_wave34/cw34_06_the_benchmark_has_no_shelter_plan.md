## 1. Expansion thesis

Iron Crest Peak is a high-ridgeline survey station for geodetic triangulation benchmarks. Its description says the view is total, shelter none, and the readings worth both. The discovery manifest lists two Iron Synod canons under this location-inspection producer while labeling their institutional identity unresolved and display-only. The canon file gives each text its own chapter and rule. This plan holds these facts apart: a survey point is not proof that a doctrine was authored there. It asks what a benchmark can witness and what remains outside the reading. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

What can a fixed point prove about a changing world, and what remains outside the reading even when the view is total?

## 5. Human center

The center is the person deciding whether a reading is enough to leave with. A surveyor can make a precise mark and still not promise what lies beyond it. A reader can hold an institutional canon and notice that it says more than a measurement proves. Keep the total view from becoming omniscience and exposure from becoming a hero test. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A survey reader names the benchmark and the limits of the page. A canon reader treats the text as attributed institutional prose, not narrator speech. New roles are anonymous. No line claims a team heard or endorsed the canons. Avoid engineering detail beyond the location’s purpose. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Approach — a station on the ridgeline

Use the location’s exact role and scale. Add no trail, instrument, or coordinate.

### 2. Reading — a point made useful

Show why a benchmark matters while leaving actual readings unspecified.

### 4. Association — the canon arrives labeled

Keep Iron Synod records attributed and unresolved/display-only.

### 5. Interpretation — doctrine is not geology

### 6. Return — the benchmark stays in place

The point remains fixed and the reader’s claim narrows.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
