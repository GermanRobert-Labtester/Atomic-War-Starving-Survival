## A river-exchange story about passage remembered and debts written down.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Ferry Point Exchange is a river landing and floating-barge exchange at western estuary crossings. Salters, trappers, and haulers barter passage and water rations. Its source says ferrymen remember every debt, the river remembers every crossing, and only one ledger gets settled. A separate note records silt buildup in a dredged channel after heavy acid rain. This plan treats the line as a question about records and unequal kinds of memory, not as crossing instructions. It stays with people who write, remember, dispute, or refuse an account while leaving route conditions and water safety to their current authorities. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Passage and water-ration barter are described. The source contrasts remembered debts, remembered crossings, and a ledger that gets settled. Its internal tension is silt buildup after heavy acid rain. No terms, writer, creditor, borrower, or individual balance is given. The river’s memory remains figurative, not supernatural testimony. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The center is the gap between what one person says happened, what another recalls, and what a clerk puts on a page. A ferryman may remember a voice without owning a traveler’s story. A traveler can question an entry without being dishonest. Let the river remain physical and outside anyone’s control. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

The exchange clerk writes compactly; a hauler asks what an entry refers to; a salter is careful about what they witnessed. These are editorial roles, not named residents. The river never literally testifies. Its memory is metaphor, not an oracle. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read, ask what a line means, decline to add their recollection, or leave. They are not forced to adjudicate a debt. Existing trade and route systems retain authority over actual access and outcomes. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not provide navigation, dredging, acid-rain, or water-treatment instructions. Do not claim water is potable beyond the source’s trade wording. Do not assign quantities, prices, or legal force. Do not identify anonymous travelers or literalize the river’s memory. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Landing — exchange beside the water

Name the place and the people who barter; do not make this a route guide.

### 2. Witness — a crossing told in pieces

Two people remember different parts; neither automatically defeats the other.

### 3. Entry — a ledger accepts one line

The proposed document has a narrow purpose and does not establish debt mechanics.

### 4. Silt — the channel tension arrives

Mention the recorded condition without turning it into an operational hazard sequence.

### 5. Settlement — one ledger closes

A page may be settled while memory remains unsettled.

### 6. Return — no river testimony

A later reader sees what was written and what was not; water certifies neither side.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use one proposed account and two fallible witnesses. Add no route or water information.

### Interlinked

Use only existing verified trade outcomes; keep remembered accounts distinct from debt authority.

### Wild card

Present clerk line, traveler recollection, and a blank for the river; the blank is not a reward.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
