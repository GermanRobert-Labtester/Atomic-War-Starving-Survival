# EXPANSION 118 — The Mark Beneath the Bend

## A datum survives flood and interpretation; the river keeps changing around it.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The River Bend Datum Monument begins with a useful tension: the marker is permanent, while the low-water bend around it is described as flood-scoured and accessible only at low water. The current catalog says pre-war survey crews set the marker and that it outwaited every flood. The proposed story follows a copyist who wants the datum to make the river legible and a field reader who insists that a reference is not a forecast. The movement is from naming the fixed point to accepting that a durable marker cannot make the whole landscape still. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What can a fixed mark preserve when everything measured beside it keeps moving?

## 5. Human center

The human center is the friction between a person who values a stable reference and one who notices how easily permanence becomes authority. The proposed voices are anonymous custodians of wording, not named surveyors with invented histories. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
