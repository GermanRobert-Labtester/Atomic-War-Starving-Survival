## 1. Expansion thesis

The Frozen Well Station entry describes a permafrost wellhead sealed by ice, with surveyors mounting instruments on a reinforced concrete cap during winter operations. The plan focuses on the separation between the sealed well and the cap’s documented use. A visitor wants to read the site as a promise of water; a copyist keeps the text aligned with survey work. The prose can carry longing, cold, and attention without opening the well, testing contents, or inventing an extraction system. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How can a place named for a well hold a human story when the record speaks only of a sealed station and a survey instrument?

The first remains hopeful about life without turning hope into a fact about this well. Both keep the page intact. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.
