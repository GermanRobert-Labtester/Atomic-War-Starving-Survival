## 1. Expansion thesis

The Rusted Span Bridge is described through an inversion: a partially collapsed iron rail bridge has one remaining abutment stable enough for mounting a survey instrument, and a theodolite reads truer there than on solid ground. The plan makes that line the hinge of a restrained story. A reading can be more dependable at an unexpected point without making the point a route, safe platform, or engineering recommendation. Two unnamed readers try to preserve the comparison exactly, because every convenient simplification would make it less true. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How do you preserve an accurate observation without letting a later reader mistake it for permission?
