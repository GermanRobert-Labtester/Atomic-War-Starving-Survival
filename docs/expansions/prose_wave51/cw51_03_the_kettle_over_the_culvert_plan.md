## 1. Expansion thesis

Scavenger Kettle Circling Downwind describes a spiral of crows and ash gulls over an overgrown culvert two kilometers down the rail line. The pattern suggests a fresh carcass or an abandoned scavenger haul before ground predators claim it. The existing response dispatches a rapid scout pair to salvage it. This bank treats the kettle as ecological testimony, the culvert as a place where ownership is already uncertain, and salvage as a choice that carries memory and discomfort rather than a guaranteed resource pickup.

## 2. Story question

When crows and ash gulls spiral above a culvert and announce a fresh carcass or abandoned haul, how can a scout read the sign without turning every death into a prize?
