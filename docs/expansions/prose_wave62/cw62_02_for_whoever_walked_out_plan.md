# EXPANSION CW62-02 — For Whoever Walked Out

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 62: Thresholds, Memory, and Duty

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_walkout_door_is_heavy — For Whoever Walked Out.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

For Whoever Walked Out is a local graffiti posting triggered by escalation_walkout, available from day 50 with weight 0.9. It says the reader left at the storm lull, which means they planned and were right that the bunker would not follow. It says the hatch is heavy from both sides, heavier from out there, and that if the wall is read again the person should know their bunk was stripped by regulation while their tin was not touched. The posting closes by saying both facts were argued about and one argument won. This plan treats the note as a farewell that refuses both condemnation and reunion mechanics.

## 2. Story question

When a person leaves during a storm lull, the hatch is said to be heavy from both sides, and a bunk is stripped by regulation while its tin remains untouched, what does a wall say to someone who may read it again?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, triggerWorldFlag, minDay, and weight. It does not establish the walker, the storm, the hatch condition, the regulation, the bunk, the tin, or which argument won. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing walkout, hatch, bunk, keepsake, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "you left at the storm lull, which means you planned, which means you were right that we would not follow. the hatch is heavy from both sides. it is heavier from out there. if this wall is ever read by you again, know that your bunk was stripped by regulation and your tin was not touched. both facts were argued about. one argument won." The fuller reading treats the farewell as a message that crosses a boundary without demanding a reply. The storm lull is a pause in weather and a pause in the decision of those who remain. The note says the walker planned, but that is a claim made by people who stayed and may be trying to make an absence legible. The hatch is heavy from both sides because the door carries memory, not because the prose should describe force or exit. The phrase heavier from out there lets the outside remain undescribed. It may hold distance, weather, regret, or the simple fact that return is not the same as opening. The bunk stripped by regulation places administration next to care. The tin not touched is a small refusal to let every object become policy. The plan should keep the two facts in conflict without declaring which is morally correct. A future reader can feel addressed without becoming a route target. The escalation_walkout flag offers existing context, not a departure simulator or return timer. It may have been about the bunk, the tin, the regulation, or the right to decide for someone absent. Leaving it open lets the plan keep the community divided without manufacturing a council.
