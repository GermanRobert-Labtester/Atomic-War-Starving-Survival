# EXPANSION 140 — A Page for the Next Walker

## A sealed cache offers help and a boundary while leaving its keeper’s identity unresolved.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `stranger_cache` — The Cartographer's Cache.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Cartographer’s Cache is defined by an address passed from a stranger, a clean lock that feels suspicious, and a field report that describes both a warning and a gift. The report’s note invites the next walker to take only what is needed, while its author cannot say whether one person or several maintain the cache. A prose expansion can make that restraint playable: the player encounters hospitality with limits, not a solved mystery about who left it. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

Can a gift remain generous when the giver’s name, number, and next arrival are all unknown?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location describes a sealed pre-war locker in a collapsed residential block, an address handed over on a scrap of paper by a stranger with steady hands, and a clean, oiled lock that raises suspicion. The location itself says a trap is not out of the question; danger is 7 and the base dose is listed as 25 rads per hour. The day-45 expedition report led by the Prepper instead describes a buried steel ammunition box sealed with wax, a noise-making warning, recent footprints, a note, a map, and a blank notebook. It says the handwriting matches prior map markings but concludes that one person or several may maintain the cache. The report’s unit wording differs from the location’s; preserve both without conversion. The location also has distinct clean-water and irradiated-water flags, which this plan does not alter.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the supplied scrap, suspicious lock, report’s alternative container description, warning device, note, footprints, map, notebook, and trade as claims from their respective sources. The keeper may be one person or many. Handwriting resemblance does not establish identity. The report’s 47-person bunker and one-iodine exchange belong to that report only. The location’s water flags distinguish existing outcomes; do not combine or extend them. No route directions or trap mechanics are reproduced. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the care built into an unclaimed object: a note asks strangers to leave something for someone they will never meet. Do not romanticize the risk or make the cache keeper omniscient. A cautious reader can accept the gift, question its wording, or leave without being cast as ungrateful. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use short field language: received, left, unknown, not checked. A report writer can sound grateful without naming an unseen keeper. A second reader can challenge the phrase gift and still accept that the message offers a practical ethic. Keep the map’s contents abstract. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location’s required flag gates its coordinates, and its water-related flags are existing outcome identifiers. The expedition report is a dated authored record. Neither proves a new route or a current physical state. Verify the existing flag consumer and content owner before selecting any fragment; do not add a cache visit, note system, or water branch. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may accept the address, read the note, leave something, or decline to interact. These are prose possibilities only. The current data’s clean-water and irradiated-water paths are not expanded here, and no moral score is attached to cautious behavior. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not describe how to squeeze through collapsed material, cross drainage routes, detect or disarm a trap, or evade another person. No personal identity for the Cartographer, exact map coordinates, inventory promise, route guide, or inference that the stranger and keeper are the same person. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
