# EXPANSION 141 — The Line Outlives the Market

## A painted boundary holds only while people continue to honor it.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_neutral_ground` — Neutral Ground.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Neutral Ground is not a timeless promise. The location describes a plaza where no faction claims sole ownership, a painted boundary people agree to honor because the alternative is worse, and traders who gather on market days. A world-evolution event later records a warlord claim and tolls; several quests place mediation or difficult testimony at the same location key. The expansion should preserve both the fragile agreement and the authored possibility that it breaks, without forcing those branches into one shared afternoon. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When a boundary is kept by agreement rather than force, what can a person promise the next market day?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location record calls this a pre-war interchange plaza where three routes met. It describes a dry fountain, broken benches, a painted line, an agreement not to cross it because the alternative is worse, and market days. A world-evolution event scheduled for day 18 says Sector 4 warlords break the treaty, occupy the plaza, and impose tribute tolls. A dynamic quest template references a salvage-boundary dispute here; branching moral quests also use the location key for several separate mediation, betrayal, and convoy-intelligence stories. Those records establish authored branches, not one continuous scene or a guarantee that the base neutral state persists.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the neutral description and the later warlord-occupation event as distinct state descriptions. Do not assume both occur simultaneously. Market traders, painted line, fountain, and benches come from the location record; the quest narratives remain their own branches. No faction controls the plaza in all states. The event says the treaty is broken; the content cannot make that break harmless or silently restore neutrality. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the labor of a shared boundary: people agree to stop at a line because the alternative has already been costly. A market can be ordinary for an hour without becoming safe forever. Let negotiations sound tired and practical, and let the cost of broken agreement remain visible without making every visitor a combatant. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

A market clerk counts spaces and voices, not victories. A second speaker can ask which version of the plaza is being described. Use ordinary transaction language: here, across, tomorrow, enough. Do not put exposition about factions into traders’ mouths unless the cited branch supports it. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location record and world evolution event define materially different conditions. Quest entries reference the location key, but no new trigger or chronology is established here. Before selecting any fragment, verify the current world-state owner and the actual quest/content consumer. Do not add a parallel treaty flag or assume the market-day line overrides later ownership. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may keep a promise, refuse to mediate, read the record, or leave. The currently authored quest choices own their outcomes. This plan adds no new mediation result, faction standing, toll payment, or event order. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No new treaty terms, faction leader, armed confrontation, toll schedule, or restoration quest. Do not combine separate moral-choice quests into one meeting. Do not portray neutrality as a magical shield or promise of safety. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
