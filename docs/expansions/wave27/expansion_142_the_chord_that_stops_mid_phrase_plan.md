# EXPANSION 142 — The Chord That Stops Mid-Phrase

## A distant broadcast makes a ruined hall feel occupied without naming the player’s unseen listener.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `concert_hall_ruins` — Concert Hall Ruins.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Concert Hall Ruins is a place where sound still behaves as the architecture promises: a whisper from the stage reaches the back row. It is also associated with a three-day piano broadcast whose skilled playing, brief spoken words, and final silence give the hall an intimate radio story. The source never names the pianist, never explains what happened after the signal died, and does not prove that every proposed visitor arrives at the same moment. The expansion can explore listening as an act of attention while leaving those spaces open. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When a signal ends after thanking its listeners, what can the people who heard it responsibly say happened?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location says the roof is open to the sky, the stage is intact, a whisper from the stage reaches the back row, and someone has used the hall as a meeting place. The expedition row gives the site danger 2 and identifies ordinary scavenging categories; it does not author a piano encounter. The 55.1 MHz distress record, The Pianist’s Last Broadcast, has three fragments over three days: a skilled performance, a speaker saying they have played for three days while radiation is present, then a pause, silence mid-phrase, thanks, and a signal that dies. Its location reference points to the hall. A follow-up after an answered signal mentions a brief chord and rustle of sheet music near the stage.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the broadcast’s three-day order and its words attached to the broadcast. The pianist has no supplied name. The signal dying does not say that the performer died. The follow-up’s conditional chord and sheet-music rustle are authored radio text, not proof of an always-present piano, current sheet music, or a guaranteed encounter. The location’s meeting-place detail is separate from the broadcast’s chronology. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the unreturned courtesy of listening. The pianist thanks whoever might hear; the player’s response can be silence, an answer, or an ordinary task resumed. Do not make the listener a rescuer after the fact or a witness who knows the performer’s final hours. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Let the hall carry distance: one small sound from the stage, one pause before a reply. Radio copy should remain radio copy, with fragments and clarity changing as authored. An unnamed voice can still sound practiced. Do not write lyrics, a biography, or a second performance. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location and radio records support an association, not a new signal route or guaranteed map encounter. The expedition configuration does not itself establish the radio condition. Confirm the existing distress-signal consumer and presentation owner before using these drafts. The answered follow-up remains conditional and should not be generalized to every visit. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player can listen, answer where the existing system permits, or let the transmission end. No new rescue branch, pianist identity reveal, concert, reward, or morale result is introduced. The existing signal outcomes remain authoritative. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No instructions for entering ruins, no claim of a body or exact death, no copied musical score or lyrics, and no invented audience. Do not make the roof, stage, and radio all indicate the same timestamp without a source. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
