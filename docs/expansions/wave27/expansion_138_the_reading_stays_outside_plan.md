# EXPANSION 138 — The Reading Stays Outside

## A shut outbuilding leaves the dose measurable, the cause unsettled, and the life inside unnamed.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_the_vessels_cell` — The Vessel's Cell.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Vessel’s Cell begins with a boundary that the location itself insists upon: the door is wedged from within, the jugs are empty, and the measured dose belongs to the outside of the building. The codex and history records preserve two supported explanations for the prophet’s survival, but neither can be disproved. A careful expansion can make the reader feel the weight of that uncertainty without turning evidence into a verdict. It must also avoid joining a different cellar story that currently shares the same discovery key. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What can a witness say about a sealed room when the records support more than one explanation and name no occupant?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location record describes a four-metre-square reactor outbuilding, a door wedged shut from inside, empty opened jugs and their caps arranged in a row, a dose reading of 68 rads per hour at the door that rises as one circles the building, and an internally shuttered window. It says the building gives up nothing else. The codex entry and the black-sky history entry both mention eleven weeks of tally marks and the Vessel’s reported emergence unscathed; each says the miracle and mundane explanation are supported and neither can be disproved. The evolution seed lists the Cult of the Ash Sign as owner. A separate world-history-expansion body about a cistern used to quiet children shares this location ID but describes a different place; it must not be folded into this content. A prior expansion’s low-background-lab reading is also tied to another location and is not this site’s measurement.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the location’s inside/outside distinction and its exact uncertainty. The occupant is unnamed. The source says someone chose the room at the end and someone stacked the jugs and wedged the door, possibly the same person. The eleven-week tally appears in the codex and world history, not in the location description. Do not assign the tally maker, the occupant, or the prophet a shared identity. Keep the two supported explanations open; ownership by the Cult does not establish who was in the room. Do not import the unrelated cistern body or the separate laboratory reading. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the limit of what can be carried forward. A reader may want to name the person who stayed, or to make the ordered caps into a last message. The prose should let that wish be present without pretending the source answers it. The evidence is already intimate: a row made by a hand, and a door that does not open in the account. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use the spare vocabulary of a field transcription and a later archive correction. The reader notices position, arrangement, and wording. No voice claims to have entered the room or to know the occupant. Keep the impossible and the ordinary adjacent on the page, with equal grammatical care. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The codex says it unlocks on a visit to this location, and the world-history record uses location exploration. Those records do not authorize new scene triggers, new access, or an added interpretation choice. The plan remains editorial text for a current owner to assess. Quarantine the incompatible journal-triggered cistern body from this location until its provenance is resolved. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the two explanations, leave the comparison open, or stop at the exterior observation. Do not turn belief into a virtue score, make the player choose between miracle and shielding, or imply that a choice changes the location’s owner or history. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No entry or exposure advice, radiation conversion, ritual instruction, biography, cause of death, or claim that the room protected a person. Preserve the distinct units and locations in other records. Do not reproduce the unrelated children-and-cistern story as a sequel here. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
