# EXPANSION 139 — The Last Entry Was a Week Ago

## A traveler log turns a small cache into a shared place without proving who will come next.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `old_library_cache` — Old Library Cache.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Old Library Cache is already about more than its hidden supplies. Books survived in a dry pocket; a field report describes a notebook that records travelers who sheltered there, ending with a southbound departure and a candle left for the next person. A separate Free Radio tape names the library as a cache for anyone who needs it. These records make a strong human story about custody, uncertainty, and the right to leave a page blank. They also disagree with the location’s dose figure, so the prose must keep its evidence attached to its source. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How does a shared place stay shared when no one can promise who will read the notebook next?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location record describes books in a dry pocket behind the collapsed reference desk and says people left things between pages. The field report is led by Mira the Scavenger, completed on day 34 by a one-person team; it describes a traveler log whose last entry is a week earlier, on Day 27, saying the writer is heading south and has left a candle. The report lists a blanket and two sealed bottles of water. The Free Radio tape set separately identifies the library as a cache and names different intended goods. The location record gives 12 rads per hour while the field report says there was no radiation; keep these as distinct authored statements rather than converting or resolving them.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the Free Radio four-part order and do not invent the broadcaster’s identity or fate beyond the authored tape. The traveler log’s Day 27 line belongs to the field report, not the player’s current visit. Mira is the report leader, not necessarily the notebook’s author. The report’s water and blanket are not the same inventory as the Free Radio list. Preserve the radiation wording discrepancy; no in-world safety advice follows from it. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a modest act of maintenance: someone leaves a candle, another person writes a line, and a later visitor is addressed without being known. Avoid turning that shared courtesy into a flawless community or a guaranteed supply chain. The place can offer trust while remaining quiet about who last used it. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Let the voices sound like someone keeping a common log and someone checking a copy against a report. The writing is warm but spare; it names a departure without inventing a destination beyond south. Paper smells and dry shelves can carry atmosphere, but no narrator knows the next traveler’s story. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location, the Free Radio cassette set, the field report, atmosphere records, and expedition row are the current anchors. They do not establish a newly reachable notebook UI or a route. Any selected prose must have an existing content owner and preserve the source discrepancy about radiation. Do not place a Free Radio broadcast on the notebook page unless the current content authority approves that linkage. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the log, leave a narrow entry, or decline to sign. No choice should award the cache, claim ownership, or promise that a future traveler will arrive. A blank signature line is a valid player-facing outcome. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No extra diary author, lost child, secret bookshelf, inventory guarantee, route directions, radiation conversions, or medical guidance. Do not quote whole cassette scripts. Keep report observations separate from current physical state. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
