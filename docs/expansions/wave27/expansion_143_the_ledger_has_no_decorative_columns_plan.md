# EXPANSION 143 — The Ledger Has No Decorative Columns

## The Dredgers’ careful accounting makes fairness visible without turning the shrine into a system tutorial.

### Wave 27: The Spaces Between Records

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 20 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_the_sump_cathedral` — The Sump Cathedral.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Sump Cathedral’s strongest story is already present in its exchange: Dredgers draw pure water from deep sumps and trade it for UV lamps, iodine, and other necessities; they keep careful ledgers and accept a fair trade or none. The glowing moss and vent-driven air give that agreement a physical setting. A prose expansion can show how people preserve fairness when supplies are scarce, while avoiding invented rites or technical instructions. It must also keep a discovery-manifest mismatch from making unrelated well, clock, and mine records look like observations from this cistern. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How does a ledger remain fair when the people making the exchange cannot promise that tomorrow’s needs will match today?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location description says the underground cistern was rebuilt by the Dredgers into a shrine, bioluminescent moss covers its surfaces to the waterline and moves with air from vents, and the Dredgers trade pure water from deep sumps for UV lamps, iodine, and other supplies. It calls them serious, careful ledger-keepers and says trade is fair or nothing; they have survived three years and intend a fourth. The discovery manifest lists this location as producer for records whose source catalogs identify a clepsydra, a geothermal borehole, and a salt mine. Their record IDs and prose name those facilities, so they are not treated here as observations made in the Cathedral. That provenance seam is an editorial hold, not new shrine lore.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the Dredgers as a collective, without inventing a named elder or doctrine. The source calls the place a shrine but supplies no ritual. The moss gives faint green light and moves with vent air; it is not a lamp. The location’s trade terms and careful ledgers are facts. The fourth year is their stated intention, not a guaranteed outcome. Discovery-manifest associations do not transfer clepsydra, geothermal, or salt-mine details into this cistern. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a fair exchange kept legible. A ledger protects both sides from the pressure to remember a deal differently after the water is gone. The Dredgers can be firm without being cruel; a visitor can decline without being shamed. Let care show in the clear column, the correction made before anyone signs, and the refusal to promise what cannot be supplied. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use practical phrases and small pauses. A Dredger’s voice should not turn the location description into a sermon. The visitor can ask what a line means, and the ledger-keeper can answer in plain terms. Keep sensory prose close to green light, moving air, stone, and the page. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location is directly associated with several discovery-manifest rows, but the rows’ source catalogs point to other facilities. Verify the narrative-discovery owner and record provenance before selecting any of those fragments. This plan does not add a discovery, create a trading UI, or reinterpret the ledger as a new save-backed authority. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may ask for the terms, accept a fair exchange, or walk away. The location record explicitly allows trade to be nothing if unfair. No inventory quantities, bonus terms, or relationship results are added here. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No water collection instructions, radiation or purification advice, medical guidance, ledger mathematics, invented religious practice, faction hierarchy, or guaranteed stock. Do not use the misrouted discovery records as if their well, mine, or clock prose belongs to the Cathedral. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
