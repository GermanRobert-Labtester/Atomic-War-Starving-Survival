# EXPANSION CW68-04 — Say the Names, Do Not Rush

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 68: Children’s Folklore as Shelter Work

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Graveyard Dare is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore_batch_2.json. Children dare each other to walk through the eastern field cemetery, touch every gravestone, and say each name. Skipping a name sends the player back to the start. Petr holds a record of all 31 stones in 4 minutes and 12 seconds. The Undertaker watches from the workshop window and says they know the names, which is more than most adults can say, while Sonya refuses because the dead do not mind touch but mind being rushed. This plan expands the dare as remembrance, disagreement, and the ethics of pace.

## 2. Story question

When children dare one another to touch every gravestone and say every name without running, what does a game teach about the dead when speed becomes the thing to refuse?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the cemetery’s complete list, Petr’s motives, the Undertaker’s private feelings, Sonya’s reasons beyond her statement, or whether the game remains welcome to every child. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, child, cemetery, undertaker, memorial, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local prose: 'Children dare each other to walk through the cemetery at the eastern field and touch every gravestone without running. The rule is: you must say the name on each stone as you touch it. If you skip a name, you go back to the start. Petr holds the record — all 31 stones in 4 minutes, 12 seconds. He says the Undertaker was watching from the workshop window the entire time and did not stop him. When asked why, the Undertaker said: 'They know the names. That's more than most adults can say. Let them walk.' Sonya refuses to play the game. She says the dead don't mind being touched but they mind being rushed.' The fuller reading keeps the dare’s contest and its memorial practice in tension. Petr measures speed, but the rule requires names, so the game cannot be separated from attention. The Undertaker’s permission is not a general cemetery policy; it is a line in a folklore record. Sonya’s refusal gives the children a second ethics: remembering can be an act that refuses haste. A candidate passage may show a player slowing at one stone, forgetting a name, or deciding to begin again without treating the restart as punishment. A return can let Petr discover that a record time is easier to remember than one person’s story, or let Sonya explain why touch and speed feel different. The source supports memory, play, and moral disagreement. It does not establish a race mechanic, memorial route, burial procedure, or grief curriculum. Keep all 31 and 4:12 inside the tale, keep the names human, and let the cemetery remain a place where the children learn from one another rather than from a score. The dare can continue after Petr’s record without making the record its purpose. A child may walk more slowly because one name is hard to say, while another child may discover that touching a stone is not the same as knowing the person beneath it. The Undertaker’s window gives the scene a witness, but the Undertaker need not intervene. Sonya’s refusal can be respected as a complete choice rather than a challenge for the others to overcome. If a later player returns to the cemetery, the content can remember the rule and still let the player decline the game. The names are the heart of the folklore; the stopwatch is its complication. A passage may let Petr repeat the time and then forget which stone he touched first, making the number feel smaller than the field. Another may show the Undertaker saying that a child who walks slowly has not failed. Keep the dead out of competition, keep the children’s disagreement ordinary, and let the game reveal that remembrance has more than one pace.
