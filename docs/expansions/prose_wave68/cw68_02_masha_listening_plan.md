## 1. Expansion thesis

The Three-Legged Dog Tale is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore_batch_2.json. Masha is a three-legged therapy dog whose story is told to children who cannot sleep. The heroic version says she lost her leg holding the hatch against ash; the children know the real story, that she was born with a deformity and had the leg amputated as a pup, but prefer the heroic version. The tale says Masha understands every word, reports dreams to the Therapist, guards the dormitory, and checks a child’s dosimeter with her mind. Sonya tells it to younger children and ends by saying Masha is listening. This plan expands the tale as protection, embellishment, and the difference between a story that comforts and a fact that deserves care.

## 2. Story question

When children prefer a heroic story about Masha protecting the bunker even though they know the real account of her amputation, what does a comforting lie do when a dog is already loved as she is?

In the story, Masha lost her leg protecting the bunker from the ash — she stood at the hatch and held it closed with her body while the storm tried to get in. The children know the real story (Masha was born with a deformity, her leg was amputated when she was a pup) but they prefer the heroic version. In the story, Masha can understand every word spoken in the bunker. She reports the children's dreams to the Therapist. She guards the dormitory at night. It does not establish animal telepathy, therapy outcomes, radiation detection, or a dog behavior system.
