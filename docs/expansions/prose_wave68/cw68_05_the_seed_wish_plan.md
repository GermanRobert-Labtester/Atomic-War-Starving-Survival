# EXPANSION CW68-05 — The Seed Wish

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 68: Children’s Folklore as Shelter Work

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Seed Wish is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore_batch_2.json. When the Botanist plants a new batch, children receive one seed, whisper a private wish into their palm, and press it into soil. The Botanist reads their moving lips and hears common wishes for clean rain, a father’s return, lights that never go out, and Sonya’s repeated wish that Masha lives forever. The Botanist does not correct them. She observes that wished-for seeds grow at the same rate as unwished seeds, but the child who planted one checks on it more often. This plan expands the practice as private hope, care, and the difference between biological rate and human attention.

## 2. Story question

When children whisper wishes into seeds before pressing them into soil and the Botanist knows the words but does not correct them, what does hope change when the plant grows at the same rate anyway?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the seed variety, the children’s names beyond Sonya, the Botanist’s methods, the wishes’ outcomes, or whether every child believes the wish affects growth. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, child, botanist, greenhouse, seed, Masha, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local prose: 'When the Botanist plants a new batch of seeds, the children are allowed to help. Each child is given one seed and told to whisper a wish into their palm before pressing it into the soil. The wishes are never spoken aloud — that would break the spell. But the Botanist has observed the children's lips moving and has learned to read them. Common wishes include: 'I wish it rains clean,' 'I wish Dad comes home,' 'I wish the lights never go out,' and, from Sonya, every time: 'I wish Masha lives forever.' The Botanist does not correct the children. She has learned that a seed planted with a wish grows at the same rate as a seed planted without one. But the child who planted it checks on it more often.' The fuller reading keeps the wish private even while the record reports its pattern. A seed does not become a promise machine. The Botanist’s restraint matters because she can know the growth rate and still understand that a child’s extra visits are a real consequence. A candidate passage may show the Botanist pretending not to hear a new wish, a child changing the words while keeping the ritual, or Sonya checking a pot because Masha’s name is inside it. The source supports hope, grief, observation, and ordinary care. It does not establish clean rain, family return, power continuity, immortality, plant growth bonuses, or a wish system. Keep the spell as children’s language, keep the Botanist’s measurement modest, and let attention be the human change the seed makes. The seed ritual can be shown at the edge of ordinary greenhouse work, where the Botanist knows that a private wish does not alter the growth rate and still makes room for it. A child may cup the seed longer than necessary, not because the seed can hear but because releasing the wish is difficult. The Botanist can read moving lips and choose not to repeat what she has understood. That choice protects the ritual’s privacy even when the record has already named common wishes. Sonya’s Masha wish can recur without becoming a spell that saves the dog. A later return may show the child checking a pot before checking their own ration, or may show a seed failing while the wish remains meaningful. The page should keep biology and hope in the same frame without making either one a joke. What changes is the frequency of care. A wished-for seed is visited more often because someone has placed part of a life inside it. Let the plant grow at its own rate and let the child’s attention remain the lasting consequence.
