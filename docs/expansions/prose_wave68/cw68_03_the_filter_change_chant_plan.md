## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Filter Change Chant is a local children’s folklore record in bunker_children_folklore_batch_2.json. Children assigned to help the Electrician carry filter elements sing a rhythm matched to the corridor stairs: pull the old one, push the new, check the gasket, screw it tight, and breathe the air meant to last. The Electrician says the children are better at the job than most adults because they do not complain about dust, and the chant has spread to adult maintenance crews during lower-level filter changes. This plan expands the chant as labor culture and shared rhythm while keeping all technical work under current maintenance authority.

## 2. Story question

When children sing a maintenance chant while carrying filter elements and adults adopt the rhythm below, what does a work song preserve when its words sound like instructions?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the folklore ID and exact prose. It does not establish the filter model, the children’s ages, the Electrician’s name, the actual maintenance procedure, whether children still assist, or whether every adult crew sings. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing folklore, child, electrician, ventilation, filter, maintenance, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local prose: 'Sung by children assigned to help the Electrician carry filter elements to the ventilation chamber. The rhythm matches the pace of walking down the corridor stairs: 'Pull the old one, push the new. / Check the gasket, make it true. / Screw it tight and seal it fast. / Breathe the air that's meant to last.' The Electrician says the children are better at the job than most adults because they don't complain about the dust. The chant has spread to the adult maintenance crews, who sing it during filter changes on the lower levels.' The fuller reading treats the song as a record of coordinated labor, not a procedure to repeat from prose. Its strongest content is the rhythm: a group moving together, carrying weight, and making stairs feel countable. The words borrow technical verbs because children hear the adults work, but the source does not grant the children or the plan authority over filter changes. A candidate passage may show the Electrician changing one lyric after a new part arrives, an adult crew singing too loudly, or a child realizing that a chant can make work feel easier without making it safe by itself. The dust remains a material fact, not a challenge tutorial. The final breath line can be comfort, hope, or an adult promise-shaped phrase, but it should not become an air guarantee. The record supports labor dignity, apprenticeship by proximity, and oral tradition crossing age lines. It does not establish filter maintenance steps, ventilation status, protective equipment, or a child work system. Keep the chant attributed, keep current owners responsible, and let the song carry people through the corridor without pretending rhythm can replace care.
