# EXPANSION 154 — Plot 114 Stays 114

## A gift of bean starts and a disputed number both ask whether a piece of ground can belong to the person who tends it.

### Wave 29: Where a Public Life Holds

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 29 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_the_allotments` — The Allotments.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.

**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Allotments are a civic garden with two hundred numbered plots, a caretaker’s hut, a posted waiting list, and soil worked in rotation. A location record says four waiting-list names were marked off after those people were alive and farming there; a dated journal from Nadia Fossey says the list was four names shorter than the previous spring, and that the same pencil was supposed to mean good news. Her entry also records a neighbor giving her bean starts without payment and her planting them in an empty end of her row, unsure she would be the one to pick them. Separately, plot watcher Dara Mewn insists that plot 114 is still 114 even though a proposed plate bears a different 114. The plan keeps Nadia’s gardening and Dara’s numbering dispute distinct while asking how a public list and a private row can both be honest records. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When a list says ground is being given to people and a plot marker says the ground has changed hands, whose number tells the truth?

## 3. Verified local anchor

## 5. Human center

A row is both a food-growing place and the work someone has done in it. Nadia’s neighbor offers starts with no payment because losing the row feels possible; Nadia plants them anyway. Dara’s insistence on a number is not paperwork for its own sake: a wrong marker can assign someone else’s ground. The stories share a noticeboard but do not need to be the same argument. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.
