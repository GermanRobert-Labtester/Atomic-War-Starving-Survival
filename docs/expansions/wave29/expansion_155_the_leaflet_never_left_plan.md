# EXPANSION 155 — The Leaflet Never Left

## The presses stopped, the bundles stayed banded, and a recalled edition became more desired than the pages anyone actually received.

### Wave 29: Where a Public Life Holds

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 29 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_printworks` — The Printworks.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Printworks is a building where production ended before distribution. Its web presses are seized solid, while pallets of leaflets remain banded in the loading bay. The location description says the leaflets were printed in the first week, included instructions and warnings, and contained one edition that was recalled; it does not identify that edition or reproduce its text. The Printworks codex describes the undelivered paper as the final broadcasts in print, says eleven tonnes were printed the week before, and notes that people know it can be used as tinder but dislike doing so. The two accounts differ in their time phrasing. This plan keeps that seam visible and follows the absence of a readership: people can want a text they never received without the plan inventing what it said. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

Can a warning still be a public message if the paper never reached the people it was meant to address?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location row says the web presses are seized, the loading-bay pallets of leaflets remain banded, the text includes instructions and warnings, and one edition was recalled; it says those leaflets were printed in the first week and the paper and ink remain legible three years later. The codex entry calls the paper final broadcasts, gives the phrase “remain in your homes, await further instruction,” and says eleven tonnes were printed the week before and never distributed. These two timing descriptions are not reconciled here, and the exact recalled edition, its text, author, and recall reason are unspecified. A seasonal paper-nest rat infestation exists as a separate conditional catalog row; it is not assumed active. Paper-making discovery records are separate from leaflet content and are not reproduced.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the banded pallets undelivered as described. Preserve both source time phrases with their provenance instead of choosing a chronology. The recall is a fact in the location entry, but the edition and reason remain unknown. The codex’s broadcast wording is attributed to that codex and is not expanded into a complete leaflet. The paper’s possible use as tinder is an observation, not an instruction or a moral test. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a gap between authorship and audience. A warning was prepared for people who did not receive it; collectors now want the recalled edition for reasons the source does not specify; other people see the stacked paper as a material temptation. The prose gives each perspective room without making one reader the authority over what the words mean. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Let a proposed loading-bay copyist speak in inventory language, a collector speak about wanting a missing edition without explaining an unsupported motive, and an unnamed neighbor remember that no leaflet reached their door. These remain editorial voices, not catalog characters. Keep the surviving phrase short and attributed; never write a whole fictional announcement as if it were recovered. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location and Printworks codex are the cited narrative sources. The seasonal infestation row and paper-making discovery records are separate authorities and should not be drawn into a scene without a current consumer and provenance check. No leaflet inventory, scavenging reward, press-repair action, route, or recovered-edition quest is proposed. Verify any current text owner before placement. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the proposed summary if an existing content surface selects it, or leave the pages untouched. There is no choice to recover, publish, burn, or decode a recalled edition. A collector’s desire does not become a quest trigger, and the paper’s physical presence does not grant access to a new item catalog. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not invent the recalled edition’s contents, author, title, audience, or recall reason. Do not resolve the timing difference between the location and codex. No printing instructions, fire-starting method, paper salvage quantity, rat encounter, faction propaganda claim, or hidden message. Keep process records distinct from the undelivered leaflets. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
