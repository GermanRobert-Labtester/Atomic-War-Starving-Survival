# EXPANSION 152 — The Star Changes Hands

## Mira counts a line, keeps a mark on one token, and eventually gives that small sign of luck to a stranger.

### Wave 29: Where a Public Life Holds

## 1. Expansion thesis

Ration Plaza is a civic square with queue lines repainted often. The line forms at six in the morning whether or not anyone has announced a distribution; the regional account says the queue formed before the local authority formalized it. Three dated journal entries at this location keep the expansion close to one child’s changing attention: Mira counts 206 people and marks her token with a star; later she notices a public quiet around the almshouse without understanding it; later still she gives the starred token to a woman short by one and hopes the woman gets to eat. Her mother changes the family’s next queue day, for reasons the journal does not explain. This plan adds no rationing rules. It follows how a child notices gestures adults leave unexplained. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What can a child make of a line that teaches her its rules one person at a time, while leaving the reasons for those rules out of reach?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location describes pavement queue lines repainted often and a line that forms at 0600 whether or not a distribution is announced; it also gives a radiation figure that is not interpreted here. The Ration Plaza codex says the queue formed before the authority formalized it. Faction-war journal entries authored by Mira at days 482, 518, and 543 establish three distinct observations: a count of 206 people and a star drawn on her token; a quiet day associated with the almshouse whose reason her mother does not explain; and a later transfer of the starred token to a woman short one token, followed by her mother’s statement that they will queue the morning after tomorrow. Do not invent why Denner failed to wave, why the woman was short, why the queue timing changes, or what happened at the almshouse.

## 5. Human center

The center is Mira’s attention moving between a number, a missing wave, a quiet crowd, a small star, and a hungry stranger. Adults remain partial from her point of view: her mother can change a plan without giving a reason; Denner’s silence remains unexplained; a woman can be known by her radio without being named. The story gives the child’s generosity weight without making her responsible for the district’s scarcity. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.
