# EXPANSION 153 — On Paper, the Debt Grows Quieter

## A season of water, a shorter ledger, and a bargain whose arithmetic is cleaner than its conscience.

### Wave 29: Where a Public Life Holds

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 26 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_terrace_pumphouse` — Terrace Pumphouse.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Terrace Pumphouse controls irrigation head for the south slope, and its own description says control of the pump means control of the season. Later authored records make that leverage specific. On day 565, Barrow Fennick brokers an arrangement in which irrigation water is informally guaranteed to the Garrison first in exchange for three of his outstanding debts being overlooked. Four days later, the Rebuilders lose reliable access to the season’s irrigation water regardless of whether the arrangement was exposed. Fennick’s day-567 journal describes a debt line shortening and a discomfort he writes down anyway. This plan stays with the cost of a clean ledger entry and the people living downstream of a bargain. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When a debt is paid by shifting a season’s water away from other people, what does an honest ledger owe the person who writes it?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location says the pumphouse supplies irrigation to the south slope, that water is spoken for, and that a recently cut rifle loophole covers the approach. The location uses rads per hour; no dose interpretation is added. The day-565 event `evt_d565_hydro_leverage_break` names the Central Garrison and Rebuilders, describes Fennick’s agreement, and offers three player choices: expose it, let it stand, or try to broker the same terms for the Exchange. Its day-569 stage says the Rebuilders lose reliable access to the season’s irrigation water regardless of publicity, while Fennick’s ledger loses one line. Fennick’s day-567 journal describes his conflicting thoughts. Earlier day-230 and day-250 Hydro Barons events are separate disputes with their own conditions; do not combine their claims with this later agreement.

## 5. Human center

The human center is not a villain’s confession. Fennick has a debt relieved; the south slope depends on irrigation; the Rebuilders bear a seasonal loss. The event describes keepers squeezed from two sides and an agreement made quietly. The prose can hold those interests without making the player’s choice secretly erase the cost. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Fennick’s existing journal voice includes a dry qualification—“on paper”—and the discomfort of recording a trade he is not proud of. Any new line should remain plain, self-aware, and specific. An unnamed water keeper can speak in measurements without giving procedures. Avoid turning the pump into a battle map or a technical tutorial. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player may encounter the existing event and choose among its existing terms. This plan adds no fourth option and no way to prevent the documented day-569 result through prose. A reader may notice Fennick’s journal or a posted summary if an existing owner selects it; reading does not change the bargain. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not conflate the Central Garrison/Rebuilders agreement with the earlier Hydro Barons disputes. Do not promise a personal outcome for Fennick or a new remedy for the Rebuilders. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
