# EXPANSION 151 — Four Words and the Press

## The sign is plain, the press outlived its orchard, and two sisters keep a working agreement without narrating it for strangers.

### Wave 29: Where a Public Life Holds

## Batch brief

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Cider Press is a stone barn built around a screw press that still turns. The location record is emphatic about what its output is not: it is not for the mouth. It lists several possible uses—oil, sterilizing spirits for wounds and instruments, or a fuel blend—without establishing one exclusive product or providing a recipe. The named operators, Ada and Ruth Cartwright, have their own boundary: Ada speaks and negotiates; Ruth has not spoken since Hour Zero and does the work; in a late moment she hands Ada a four-word note that is not about herself. This plan treats that note as private and its contents as unknown, and makes the sisters’ labor visible without explaining Ruth away. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How do two people make shared work legible when one of them has already decided which words belong to her?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location describes an oiled screw press, a sound beam, a lost orchard, output not meant to be drunk, a maintained sign saying so, and three possible industrial or care-related purposes for the product. The character record for the Cartwright Sisters identifies Ada as speaking and negotiating and Ruth as doing the work while not speaking since Hour Zero. It says that late in their long partnership, Ruth gives Ada a written note four words long and not about herself; it does not disclose the words or reason. An infestation catalog includes a seasonal cellar mold event at this location; it is not necessary to stage that event in this plan. Radiation and output claims stay as source details, not instructions.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The note remains four words long and its text is not supplied by the source. Do not turn the sign into a recipe, identify which product use is current, or describe procedures for producing oil, sterilizing spirits, fuel, medical care, or handling contamination. The sisters’ work does not prove the orchard is restored. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a long partnership that does not owe visitors its private explanation. Ada negotiates for apples and boiler fuel while protecting a boundary around Ruth. Ruth’s work can be precise and present without being translated into an inner monologue by someone else. The press and its sign carry the public part of their agreement; the four-word note remains theirs. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Give Ada direct, practical speech about terms and timing. Do not make her speak for Ruth’s feelings. Any proposed line attributed to Ruth must be explicitly nonverbal or written only where the source supports it; do not invent the four words. New barn hands remain anonymous and editorial. Keep sensory language to stone, oil, wood, paper, and the press’s stopped or working weight. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.
