# EXPANSION 134 — The Grass Around All Forty

## Visible care is kept separate from the identity no record can supply.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_apiary_rows` — The Apiary Rows.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Apiary Rows are described through counts and care: forty white boxes, thirty-eight silent, two live, and grass cut around every box. The person responsible remains unknown, and the location says not to ask and not to take from the wrong box. A historical entry adds another time layer: two hives survived the first winter and the Militia posted a guard. This plan makes no mystery of the keeper and no procedure out of the bees. It is a set of notes about boundaries, memory, and the difference between a past guard and a present name no one can give. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What does a visitor owe to care whose keeper has chosen not to be known?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_apiary_rows` — The Apiary Rows; danger 3, travel 1.5 hours, 18 rads/hour. `locations.json` records forty boxes, thirty-eight silent and two live, grass cut around all forty, and an unnamed keeper. `world_history.json` “Two Hives” is dated Exchange+1Y and says the Militia posted a guard.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The location says someone tends two live hives and cuts the grass around all forty; nobody knows who, and visitors know not to ask or take from the wrong box. The history entry belongs to Exchange+1Y, when the Militia treated two surviving hives as strategic infrastructure. Do not identify the current keeper as militia, beekeeper, or faction. Dead boxes contain wax, frames, and propolis; this is not permission to collect them. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a boundary maintained without a sign. Care appears in cut grass; the person remains unknown. Readers can notice labor without turning it into gossip or a puzzle to solve. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use quiet field-note language: white boxes, a cut line, two living colonies, and a sentence passed along by visitors. Do not imitate a bee guide or use the hives as a whimsical metaphor. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Use the location, dated history entry, location seed, and expedition record. None establishes a new keeper, harvesting interaction, honey output, or route. Keep current unknown stewardship separate from historical guard duty. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may notice the grass, read the dated entry, or respect the instruction not to ask or take from the wrong box. Add no social check, keeper reveal, harvest option, or reward. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not name the keeper, claim the Militia still guards the field, change hive counts, or add bee-handling instructions, honey yields, pollination effects, crop bonuses, faction access, or new animal events. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
