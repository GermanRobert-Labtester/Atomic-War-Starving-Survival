# EXPANSION 137 — No Name Beside Turned Back

## A trail guestbook carries an unexplained line through changing claims on the pass.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 19 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_shrine_switchback_waystation` — The Switchback Waystation.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Switchback Waystation is a lean-to, water barrel, and board holding three years of names and dates in a dozen hands. One line says “turned back,” with no name or explanatory date. Other records place the board in changing history: a Garrison fee, Toma Reyes’s insistence that the shrine takes tribute on its terms, refunds traced by name, and later records describing a crater. This plan treats the board as a record with a life, not proof the trail stayed unchanged. It preserves each time layer and refuses to supply the missing name or reason. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What does a community keep when a shared record lasts longer than the shelter that held it?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_shrine_switchback_waystation` — The Switchback Waystation; danger 4, travel 2.5 hours, 31 rads/hour. `locations.json` describes the lean-to, barrel, three-year guestbook, and unnamed “turned back” line. `faction_war_events.json` contains D522 toll/refund and later Plan 25 broken-route contexts; `faction_war_dialogue.json` has a day-591 pilgrim exchange about Toma Reyes and a crater.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the guestbook line unnamed and unexplained. In D522, the Garrison charges an escort fee; Toma says the shrine takes tribute on its terms; after the order is rescinded, she tracks refunds by name from the board. The later Plan 25 context describes a crater and changed travel. Day-591 pilgrims say Toma no longer uses “spared” and disagree about what fell. Do not claim the board survived destruction, connect the line to a named pilgrim, or decide who chose what fell. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The center is keeping names and settling debts as authority changes around a trail. Toma’s authored action is concrete: she finds those who paid and returns what she can. The unnamed line remains outside anyone’s authority to explain. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use practical language of guestbook, fee list, rest stop, and remembered phrase. Keep Toma’s words tied to their authored stages. Later pilgrims may question a word without converting uncertainty into supernatural certainty. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Location, war events, day-591 dialogue, quests, and a final-wish location requirement share this id in different contexts. Chronology includes a later crater, so any implementation must verify stage and physical status. This plan restores no shelter and promises no readable board. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

Preserve the authored D522 choices and refund stage. Add no choice about the unnamed line and do not rewrite Plan 25 outcomes. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not identify who turned back, assign a reason/date, claim the board survived the crater, resolve who chose what fell, invent names, alter refunds, or add route, climbing, radiation, or survival instructions. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
