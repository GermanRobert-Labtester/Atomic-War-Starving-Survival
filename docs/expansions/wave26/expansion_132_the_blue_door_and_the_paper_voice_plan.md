# EXPANSION 132 — The Blue Door and the Paper Voice

## Several records share a small shelter, but no single voice settles all that happened inside.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `family_bunker_backyard_shed` — Family Bunker: Backyard Shed.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

A false floor, radio, tape recorder, and letter anchor the backyard shed. Three recordings follow a father, Maria, Ana, and a child finishing a game; a separate distress chain names twelve-year-old Lena. A completion narrative says the family made it out, while the final tape leaves its child unsure what to do. This expansion is not a new answer. It is the work of keeping each label attached to the voice that carries it and allowing neighboring records to remain in tension. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When records point to one shelter but leave their voices in tension, what can a careful reader repeat without pretending to know more?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`family_bunker_backyard_shed` — Family Bunker: Backyard Shed; danger 2, travel 1.0 hours, 6 rads/hour. `locations.json` names a false floor, family shelter, radio, recorder, and letter. `cassette_sets.json` holds the three-part Martinez Family Recordings; `moral_choice_quests_distress.json` and `radio_distress_signals.json` hold Lena’s separate branch. `events.json` says the full tape ends “we made it out.”

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the cassette parts and order. The father names Maria and Ana; Part 3 gives a child’s voice but not that child’s name. The distress chain names Lena and has exactly two outcomes: breach the blue door and bring her to safety, or leave it closed and her voice fades after two days. Its rescue follow-up says her mother woke and medicine arrived in time. The tape completion narrative says “we made it out.” Do not identify Lena as Ana or join endings without an explicit source. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is preserving words when the archive is difficult and unsettled. A copyist does not erase Lena’s fear, soften the child’s loneliness, or force an ambiguous ending into comfort. Give each record room to remain a record made for its own audience. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Stay close to tape hiss, paper, the board-game pieces implied by the voice, and the shelf. Readers speak briefly. Existing child lines remain sourced; do not invent child dialogue or tell the player what to feel. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Authorities are the location, cassette set, tape completion narrative, distress broadcast, moral quest, and follow-ups. They do not prove a new playback surface or route. Verify the owner and placement before selection. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The meaningful choice already exists: breach Lena’s shelter door or leave it closed. Add no third response, second rescue, or reversal. Other material is optional reading, not new moral choices. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No medical advice, door-breaching instructions, graphic illness, invented letter, fourth tape, survivor biography, or definitive family timeline. Keep tape, completion narrative, and radio branch distinguishable. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
