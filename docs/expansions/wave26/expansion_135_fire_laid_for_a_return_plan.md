# EXPANSION 135 — Fire Laid for a Return

## An abandoned post keeps the shape of an expected morning without naming who would come back.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 19 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_snowline_station` — Snowline Patrol Station.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Snowline Patrol Station was left in good order: stove banked, fire laid, log complete, latch shut, windows closed against the wind. Three years of mornings passed and the stove was never lit again. Other records associated with the location carry different moments: a Garrison patrolmen’s exchange about wraps and lard, and an Office distress transmission from a snowline camp whose heaters failed. This plan keeps those moments in their own records instead of joining them into one final patrol or rescue story. Its center is an unfinished expectation held by objects that cannot tell us who expected to return. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What remains of an expectation when every object was prepared for a morning that never came?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_snowline_station` — Snowline Patrol Station; danger 6, travel 5.0 hours, 42 rads/hour. `locations.json` describes the Garrison post, banked stove, completed log, closed door, and instruments. `faction_war_dialogue.json` contains a day-494 exchange; `radio_intercepts.json` has SNOWLINE-CAMP-2, a separate distress signal triangulating to the node.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The location says the post was abandoned in good order, the log is complete, three years of mornings passed, and the stove was not relit. The day-494 dialogue about two patrolmen drying wraps is associated with this location but does not identify the last occupants. The Office SNOWLINE-CAMP-2 distress intercept says the line snapped and heaters died; it expires after three days. Do not connect caller to patrolmen or rewrite the log. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The center is the difference between preparation and return. A fire laid for morning is not proof anyone survived to use it. A completed log need not have quotable final words. Let objects carry absence without inventing a lost family or rescue. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Keep the prose domestic and plain: latch, stove, dry socks, completed log. Preserve the source snippet’s rough humor and unequal comfort without extending it into biographies. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Anchors are the location, day-494 dialogue, SNOWLINE-CAMP-2, geodetic benchmark, and world seeds. They are different record types and time conditions. Verify route, consumer, and chronology before selecting text. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the record, compare sources, or leave the page closed. Add no expedition choice, weather procedure, rescue result, or occupant identity. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not write the log’s last entry, identify who laid the fire, stage the day-494 dialogue as the final moment, or equate the distress caller with Garrison patrolmen. No route, radiation, cold-exposure, stove, or rescue instructions. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
