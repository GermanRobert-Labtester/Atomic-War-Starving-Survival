# EXPANSION 133 — The Seats Stay Folded

## A place that resembles a tragedy is read again through evidence that corrects it.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_drowned_cinema` — The Odeon.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Odeon holds a row-F waterline, an intact screen, folded seats, and an image that has remained grey for three years. Its description allows two sequences for the full house and rising water; an eyewitness codex describes an early shelter and later slow flood, then rejects the mass-grave reading. This expansion asks how a reader can acknowledge what a room suggests without claiming what its records disprove. The emotional turn is a reader correcting a first impression, not discovering a secret about the audience. The catalog gives shelter, water, and a screen; it also denies the death story the folded seats seem to invite. Quiet is not evidence. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How can a reader acknowledge what a room suggests while refusing to claim what the records disprove?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_drowned_cinema` — The Odeon; danger 7, travel 6.0 hours, 46 rads/hour at the waterline. `locations.json` records row F, folded seats, a cleaner balcony, a wound projector reel, and a grey image. `codex_entries.json` says it sheltered people in the first month, flooded slowly later, and nobody drowned here.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the location description’s two possible sequences beside the codex’s eyewitness account: first-month shelter, later slow flood, nobody drowned. Folded seats indicate use, not deaths. Do not name the film or describe the grey image. Other campaign quests sharing this location id do not place their named characters in the auditorium. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is restraint before an evocative room. People sat there; the codex says they did not drown there. Honor their use of the theater without inventing casualties. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Write from careful attention to row letters, metal, water, and screen light. Let a visitor revise a first impression when the codex corrects it. Keep added voices anonymous and brief. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Use the location, eyewitness codex, and limited physical seed data. Quest metadata sharing the node does not stage its campaign scenes in the theater. Verify any actual consumer or chronology before selecting text. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may compare the location text with the codex or leave the grey image unexplained. Add no investigation minigame, route, rescue, or choice. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No mass casualty, ghost, named movie, audience count, screen diagnosis, or new water/radiation instruction. Keep the codex correction visible wherever the scene risks implying the opposite. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
