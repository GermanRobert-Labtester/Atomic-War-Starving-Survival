# EXPANSION 136 — The Labels Are Exact

## Measured records can stay exact while the story around them remains incomplete.

### Wave 26: What the Evidence Can Carry

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `loc_ice_core_store` — Ice Core Store.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Ice Core Store is kept cold by geothermal bleed, not a power line. Long white cylinders carry typed labels for depth and year before present; the location calls the archive an exact record of a century of weather. Two associated sources widen the scale but do not settle it: an Office relay reports soot from the third exchange, thicker than modeled, while a hydrophone log records an eighty-five-second shelf-ice fracture. These are separate instruments and times. The expansion asks readers to copy measurements faithfully and resist turning several records into one grand conclusion. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When measurements are exact but the world they describe is damaged, what must a reader keep separate?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_ice_core_store` — Ice Core Store; danger 6, travel 6.0 hours, 40 rads/hour in the access tunnel. `locations.json` describes geothermal bleed, long white cores, typed depth/year-before-present labels, and a century of weather. `radio_intercepts.json` reports third-exchange soot. The discovery manifest points to `hydrophone_shelf_ice_calving_echo`, an acoustic record from Year 04, Winter Day 088.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep typed labels, two-meter tube spacing, three winters of temperature holding, and the stated century of pre-grey climate records. The Office relay reports successful extraction, a soot layer from the third exchange thicker than modeled, an intact data vault, and nothing left on the surface. The separate hydrophone record is a 24.5 Hz, eighty-five-second shelf-fracture signal. Do not say it caused the core result or establishes a new climate finding. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The center is stewardship of evidence after its makers can no longer speak through every instrument. A label is exact; interpretation still has limits. Let a reader copy one number accurately and remain humble about what it answers. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Favor labels, shelf notes, and measured phrases, then one plain human line from the relay. Keep scientific claims attributed. No new scientist is named, and no narrator pretends to examine a core. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Anchors are location, Office relay, manifest link to hydrophone record, and geodetic survey point. Their consumers and chronology differ. Verify any actual reader surface; add no research panel or new sensor authority. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the description and compare records. Add no sample-selection, analysis choice, skill check, discovery result, or climate intervention. Existing signal conditions stay with their owners. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No sample-handling procedure, radiation advice, climate projection, conversion, missing-core list, or new scientific result. Do not connect the acoustic transient to extraction or treat the benchmark as a route. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
