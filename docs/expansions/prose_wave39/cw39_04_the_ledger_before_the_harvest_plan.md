## 1. Expansion thesis

The seed vault antechamber is a room of held breath: outer doors failed, inner doors held, a ledger names lines and sources, non-irradiated wheat has never seen the sky, and tools rest where someone stopped. Existing expedition records already establish a recovery of forty-seven labelled, viable varieties and a ten-variety sample. This bank stays with the human work around that evidence: how to read a list, how to remember a team, and how to keep the future from becoming a possession.

## 2. Story question

## 5. Human center

The human center is the difference between stewardship and ownership. The teacher reads a list as an obligation to remember sources; the farmer sees a future that must remain humble; the engineer knows that a door can hold without becoming an invitation. The antechamber gives them a place to disagree without making the seeds speak for themselves.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded
Keep the selected passage near an ordinary task: a note read aloud, a price repeated, a warning copied, or a tool set down. Let physical detail carry the weight. The current source remains visible in the wording.

### Interlinked
If an existing consumer already exposes the anchor, a later journal, radio, codex, item, faction, or expedition passage may echo one sentence with its original attribution. The echo changes who hears it, not what the source says.

### Wild card
A proposed reader misremembers a detail and is corrected by somebody who was there. The correction should remain small and human. Never use the wild card to reveal a hidden route, secret operator, miracle device, or new canon casualty.
