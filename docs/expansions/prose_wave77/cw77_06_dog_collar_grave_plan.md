## 1. Expansion thesis

The Dog-Collar Grave is a local epitaph record about Masha, a dog who lived twelve years in the bunker with three legs, a child’s drawing by seven-year-old Sonya, and food scraps left at a dead apple tree just in case. This plan expands the memorial as companionship, childhood change, and conditional hope without adding an animal spawn, feeding mechanic, or pet authority.

## 2. Story question

When a cracked collar marked MASHA THREE hangs from a dead apple tree and children still leave scraps for a three-legged dog who outran most four-legged ones, how does a bunker remember an animal without pretending it will return?

### Editorial scale notes

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains epitaph_b2_dog_collar_grave (The Dog-Collar Grave). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Dog-Collar Grave from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Dog-Collar Grave record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Dog-Collar Grave should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. The later reader may notice a different word, remember a different cost, or decline to repeat the account. The catalog facts remain stable unless a current authorized system says otherwise.

**06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Dog-Collar Grave should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. Let a person have a practical task, a private limit, a bad day, a memory that is incomplete, or a reason not to answer. Keep the human center larger than the prop.

**08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention epitaph_b2_dog_collar_grave or the surrounding region. Confirm names, truth classes, branch outcomes, and display ownership. A matching phrase is not proof that a passage remains current.

**10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide animal care, feeding, tracking, veterinary, breeding, pasture, scavenging, collar, dog, child, or wildlife instructions. Do not add a pet spawn, animal companion system, feeding mechanic, Masha quest, or new epitaph authority. Preserve the exact record and its limits. Keep that limit visible in scene language. A character may be afraid, cautious, angry, or wrong without the prose becoming an operational guide.

**11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. It may be a torn page, a spoken recollection, a label, a pause at a threshold, or a later sentence repeated with one word corrected. It should not look like a system specification inside the fiction.

**12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Dog-Collar Grave should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.
