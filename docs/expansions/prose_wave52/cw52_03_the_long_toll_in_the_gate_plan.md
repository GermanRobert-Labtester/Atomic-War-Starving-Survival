## 1. Expansion thesis

The Long Toll begins with a gate whose upkeep is no longer covered by scrap tithes. Gatekeepers demand a permanent salt covenant from all outbound caravans. The quest moves through failing hydraulic counterweights beneath the span, negotiation of the salt-tithe percentage with caravan captains, and a final registry seal. Its choices endow the crossing gate with the covenant or reject the toll and force open transit. This bank keeps the gate as infrastructure, livelihood, and political pressure at once.

## 2. Story question

When a crossing gate’s upkeep outlasts the scrap tithes that built it, can a permanent salt covenant be called maintenance without becoming a permanent claim on every traveler?
