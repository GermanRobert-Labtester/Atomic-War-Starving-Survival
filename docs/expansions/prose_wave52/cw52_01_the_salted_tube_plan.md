## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Salvaged Accord begins with a pre-war non-aggression covenant signed by river baronies and preserved in a lead tube. The water wardens seek to ratify its terms again. The quest names the lower piers, the scalehouse, ferrymen, wardens, and a final choice to ratify the accord with a water seal or repudiate it and break the seal. This bank treats the document as a living burden carried by present people, not as a legal tutorial or a shortcut to permanent peace. It gives writers room to show what a promise sounds like after its signatories are gone.

## 2. Story question

When a pre-war non-aggression covenant survives in a tarnished lead tube, can people ratify old language without pretending the old world still exists?
