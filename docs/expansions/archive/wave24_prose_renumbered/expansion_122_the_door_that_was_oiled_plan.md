# EXPANSION 122 — The Door That Was Oiled

## A watched place is made legible through one small act of maintenance.

### Wave 24: What the Record Leaves

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `rural_gas_station` — Rural Gas Station.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Rural Gas Station is stripped, wind-swept, and already searched. Its description leaves one human trace that survives the picking: someone has slept in the workshop bay and oiled the door hinges, so the station is watched even when empty. A separate distress chain names Olen and preserves a rescue-or-ignore outcome. This plan keeps those records in view without assuming every trace belongs to the same person. It makes the hinge a quiet center for a story about attention, privacy, and what a reader can know from a maintained door. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When a place is described as watched even while empty, whose presence can a record honestly claim?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`rural_gas_station` — Rural Gas Station; danger 3, travel 1.5 hours, base radiation 15 rads/hour. `locations.json` describes stripped pumps, missing shop glass, ash in the aisles, mostly empty fuel drums, and an anonymous sleeper oiling workshop hinges. Linked anchors: `exp_report_rural_gas_station_01` (day 36, `the_hunter`); `freq_distress_156_8` and `quest_moral_distress_injured_trader` (Olen, with explicit rescue/ignore outcomes). These records are related to the location; they do not prove the hinge worker and Olen are the same person.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The signal fragments name Olen and the moral quest defines two outcomes: dispatch a rescue party and he survives/shares battery reserves; ignore his coordinates and the signal cuts out. The answered and rescue-success follow-ups also differ. Do not alter that branch, add a neutral outcome, or identify the anonymous hinge-maintainer as Olen without explicit source authority. The expedition report’s practical recommendations are not new instructions. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the small labor of oiling a hinge at a station that has been stripped. That act may be care, habit, or a wish to make one return easier, but the plan does not choose an unknown author’s motive. Olen’s urgent radio voice remains its own story with the outcomes already authored. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Keep the shop’s wind and the door’s small sound close to the page. Let the radio arrive in fragments and then be quiet. Write the station as a place where practical scarcity and private attention coexist, never as a loot list or a puzzle about who is watching. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 8. Player agency

Drafts may clarify what a reader knows before or after that choice, but may not soften, reverse, or add to its outcomes. Other beats are optional reading and can be left unread. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
