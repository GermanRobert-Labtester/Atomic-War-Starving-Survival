# EXPANSION 126 — Open to All Who Need to Remember

## A public dedication, a small drawing, and a ledger whose unfinished pages stay in their owner’s hands.

### Wave 24: What the Record Leaves

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_ash_dune_cemetery` — Ash Dune Cemetery.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Ash Dune Cemetery’s day-100 entry is explicitly a dedication, not a death. Its public stone says the place is tended by the undertaker and open to all who need to remember; Lima-1 adds a drawing with green spots where turnips grow. This plan develops prose around that public act of tending while keeping the drawing distinct from the location description’s statement that nothing grows. It also protects a separate self-written entry by the undertaker, which the source says he showed to no one. No future death, private disclosure, or new grave is proposed. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What does a cemetery mean when its own inscription says it is for the living?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`location_ash_dune_cemetery` — Ash Dune Cemetery; danger 5.0, travel 3.0 hours, base radiation 60.0 rads/hour. `locations.json` gives the stark physical description. `narrative/undertaker_burial_records.json` contains `burial_025`, a day-100 dedication explicitly “not a death,” the public entrance inscription, its attendance list, and Lima-1’s drawing. `burial_024` is the undertaker’s private self-written entry; its note says he showed it to no one.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the day-100 dedication separate from a burial. The stone states “ESTABLISHED DAY 36” and “OPEN TO ALL WHO NEED TO REMEMBER.” The dedication epitaph says the cemetery is for the living. Lima-1’s green spots appear in a drawing; do not turn them into a new physical growth claim against the location description. Do not disclose or dramatize `burial_024`, invent the undertaker’s death, or alter any other ledger entry. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is tending as a public act whose meaning comes from its inscription, attendance, and drawing. People can keep memory without being made to perform grief for the player. The undertaker’s private page remains private, while the dedication he recorded for the community remains public in its existing source. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use public language that is plain enough to be read aloud and small enough to leave space after it. Avoid repeating graphic causes of death, spectacle, or a narrator that turns every person into a lesson. The cemetery’s own epitaph already gives its meaning; let prose approach it gently. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Existing anchors are the location record and the current burial ledger, especially public dedication `burial_025`. The private record `burial_024` is a continuity boundary, not a proposed player discovery. Verify an existing content owner and reader before selecting any public-facing draft. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the existing inscription and dedication, notice Lima-1’s drawing, or leave the place in silence. No new memorial construction, burial action, archive route, grief score, or choice is proposed. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not add deaths, causes, graves, attendees, or private pages. Avoid exposing `burial_024` as player-known: the source says the undertaker showed it to no one. Do not make the drawing evidence of a changed ecology, and do not turn location hazards into travel instructions. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
