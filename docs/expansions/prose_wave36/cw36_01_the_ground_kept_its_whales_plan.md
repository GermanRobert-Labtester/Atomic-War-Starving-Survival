## A fossil-root salvage story about the names people give to material they cannot replace.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Ash-Whale Carcass is not an animal. Its shape is a mineralized pre-war root network, black above the ash and hazardous in the hollows beneath. Scavengers already work it in careful shifts. The story follows people who must describe, sort, and trade material without letting an evocative name become a false account of what they found. The durable content is a chain of small decisions about labels, provenance, and the sentence that survives a sale.

## 2. Story question

When a resource resembles a creature, what does careful work owe to the difference?
