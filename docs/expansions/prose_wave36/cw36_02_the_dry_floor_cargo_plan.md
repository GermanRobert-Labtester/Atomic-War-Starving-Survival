# EXPANSION CW36-02 — The Dry Floor’s Cargo

## A submerged hardware salvage story where usable equipment matters more than the data it once held.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Submerged Data Center distinguishes drowned servers from the raised floor that stayed dry. Its own description says the value is not the data but working hardware pulled slowly from a room that is electrocuting itself. A prose arc can make that distinction personal without inventing an archive recovery: divers disagree about what to bring back, what not to promise, and how to tell a buyer that intact is not the same as tested.

## 2. Story question

What counts as a recovery when the room that held the city’s memory now offers hardware one tray at a time?

**Friction:** A sorter calls the area dry; a tender reminds them that the warning about electricity still belongs to the same location.

Candidate player distinction: Label a tray as intact, or leave performance unclaimed until the actual item owner has evidence.

### 4. Solar cells in the dark

A stack of solar cells has never seen the sun; the phrase carries an expectation the plan refuses to fulfil.
**Friction:** A buyer wants to count each panel as future power, while the diver has only seen a stack in a flooded facility.
Candidate player distinction: Describe the stack as found, or decline to make a generation promise.
Return possibility: A later reading keeps absence of use separate from proof of damage or function.

### 5. The gear that changes hands

**Friction:** A visitor assumes any traded set works the same; a tender knows each exchange has its own history.
Candidate player distinction: Speak about trust in the handoff, or keep the gear’s capabilities outside this story.
Return possibility: The next visit can remember a relationship without gaining a new equipment rule.

### 6. A room that is not an archive

The submerged racks once carried city networks, but the source directs value toward the hardware rather than the information.
**Friction:** A listener asks for a name or message; the diver says no readable record has been established.
Candidate player distinction: Decline to make a person’s life into salvage, or note that no data claim is being made.
Return possibility: Later readers know the silence was chosen, not a hidden clue.

### 7. One tray at a time

The location describes hardware pulled one tray at a time; the pace is in the source, but no total is supplied.
**Friction:** The shore buyer asks how many trips remain; the log keeper can only record a tray that was actually accounted for.
Candidate player distinction: Use a single handoff as the whole event, or leave future quantities open.
Return possibility: The next ledger page stays usable without becoming a production forecast.

### 8. The room that electrocutes itself

The location warns that the room is slowly electrocuting itself, without providing an approach procedure or a controllable hazard model.
**Friction:** A buyer wants a risk estimate; the crew will not invent a measurement or instructional sequence.
Candidate player distinction: Let the warning stand as a reason for uncertainty, or end the exchange before it becomes guidance.
Return possibility: A future reader sees the boundary before seeing a salvage claim.

### 9. The sealed case on shore

A case arrives at the surface and enters a different context, where a label can quietly grow beyond its evidence.
**Friction:** The sorter wants a condition word; the trader wants a saleable one.
Candidate player distinction: Write “recovered,” “watertight,” or another witnessed description only if the object supports it; do not infer operation.
Return possibility: The following sale conversation begins with provenance instead of certainty.

### 10. The unlit stack

**Friction:** One reader hears failure; another hears an unanswered question.
Candidate player distinction: Preserve the exact absence of exposure or omit interpretation from the record.
Return possibility: A future reader can add new evidence without rewriting the original observation.

### 11. A buyer asks for memory

Someone waiting at the edge of the trade asks whether a server can return a forgotten contact or record.
**Friction:** The crew refuses a promise about data whose state the location description never establishes.
Candidate player distinction: Close the negotiation on hardware value, or let the buyer walk away with the question unanswered.
Return possibility: The later callback shows that a refusal to promise can be remembered as care.

### 12. The dry-floor ledger

A final ledger describes what has been brought out and leaves the facility itself unresolved: racks below, equipment above, danger still present.
**Friction:** The crew and buyer no longer disagree about what is known; they disagree about whether that is enough to call a trip worthwhile.
Candidate player distinction: End with the observed handoff, the unsold case, or an unfilled return column.
Return possibility: The content closes on equipment and trust, not on a recovered network or a new route.
