# EXPANSION CW36-04 — A North That Won’t Stay Put

## A crater story about records that admit when instruments cannot be trusted.

### Prose Wave 36: Accounts That Keep Their Limits

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Magnetic Crater’s location description is explicit about its strange local conditions: a persistent magnetic storm, metal drawn toward the rim, a crowbar pinned to the slope, compasses and dosimeters that misbehave, and tungsten bars and RTG batteries at the strongest field. This story does not solve the crater or instruct a retrieval. It follows people trying to keep a map, a warning, and a sales claim from becoming the same kind of statement.

## 2. Story question

What does an honest record preserve when the instruments that should settle an argument become part of the uncertainty?

### 2. A compass that cannot settle the page

Compasses spin with the magnetic disturbance, so an arrow is not a shared answer in the crater’s description.
**Friction:** One visitor wants the map to look clean; another wants the confusion left legible.

**Friction:** A trader wants the number alone; the reader insists that the stated reliability problem travels with it.
Candidate player distinction: Quote the location-level rate with its warning, or decline to make a person-specific claim.
Return possibility: A later reader cannot use a detached number as a personal measurement.

### 4. The tungsten bars

**Friction:** A buyer begins to calculate profit; the returning visitor refuses to price access as if it were guaranteed.
Candidate player distinction: Discuss a hypothetical sale only as a question, or close the account before it becomes a retrieval plan.
Return possibility: The callback is a negotiation about uncertainty, not a successful extraction.

### 6. A bearing written from elsewhere

### 7. The rim changes the argument

The strongest field lies at the core, and the described objects are placed there; no proposed character needs to test the claim.

### 8. The metal sorter’s word

**Friction:** The dispute is small but consequential: verbs imply whether the object was placed, trapped, or moved.
Candidate player distinction: Choose a neutral description or attribute each interpretation to its speaker.
Return possibility: A later trade label avoids converting a local impression into the object’s history.

### 9. The map copy sold twice

**Friction:** A buyer wants certainty attached to the copy; the carrier knows the original included an instrument warning.
Candidate player distinction: Send the full margin, remove the bearing, or refuse to sell a confidence the note does not contain.
Return possibility: The callback makes provenance part of what is traded.

### 10. A report without a center

**Friction:** The audience asks for a complete account; the writer has only partial observations.
Candidate player distinction: File a deliberately incomplete report or leave the conclusion unsigned.
Return possibility: The next report can add evidence without overwriting this one.

### 11. No one retrieves the conclusion

The tungsten and batteries remain location facts; this draft does not say they are removed.
**Friction:** One worker considers whether leaving the objects unclaimed is failure; another sees restraint as a valid outcome.
Candidate player distinction: End with an unsatisfied buyer, an untested question, or a note passed to a future owner.
Return possibility: Nothing in the callback grants a salvage result or creates a new hazard state.

### 12. The map keeps its blank

On the final return, the map still has a place where north could not be certified and a sentence naming why.
**Friction:** The reader accepts that an honest blank can carry more than a false arrow.
Candidate player distinction: Keep the blank, annotate its scope, or stop copying the map.
Return possibility: The crater remains unexplained; the people leave a better account of what their account cannot do.
