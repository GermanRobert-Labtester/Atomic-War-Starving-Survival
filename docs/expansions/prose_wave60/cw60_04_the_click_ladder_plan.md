# EXPANSION CW60-04 — The Click Ladder

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 60: Thresholds, Marks, and Listening

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_geiger_click_ladder — The Click Ladder.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Click Ladder is a local graffiti posting triggered by friction_hot_token_confiscation, available from day 5 with weight 1.0. It records ten chalk tick marks rising on a door jamb like ladder rungs, green circles around one through four, red wax-pencil crossings over five through ten, and RUN scrawled at the top. This plan treats the ladder as a compressed record of fear, threshold, and someone trying to make a changing sound visible. It does not interpret the marks as calibrated measurement or provide movement advice.

## 2. Story question

When chalk rungs climb a door jamb, the first four are circled in green, the next six crossed in red wax pencil, and RUN is written above them, how should a survivor read a warning without turning it into a radiation guide?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, triggerWorldFlag, minDay, and weight. It does not establish what made the clicks, what the colors mean, who drew the word, or whether the threshold is safe. The prose preserves the marks and routes candidates only through existing graffiti, token, hot-zone, radiation, or narrative consumers. The fuller reading treats the click ladder as a record of interpretation under pressure. Ten marks look objective because they are orderly, but the colors and the word above them reveal a person trying to make uncertainty behave. The first four circles can be comfort, memory, or a line that once felt survivable. The later crossings can be fear, correction, or an argument with an earlier self. The source does not tell us which. The door jamb is a strong surface because it is both ordinary architecture and a place people pass while carrying decisions. A candidate passage may show a hand hovering near the first rung, a reader counting twice, or a second person refusing to treat the marks as a promise. None of those moments should become movement advice. The word RUN can be allowed to frighten the reader without becoming a route, an objective, or a button. The hot-token-confiscation flag gives the posting a social context for fear and authority, but it does not explain the clicks or authorize a new radiation scale. Keep the green and red descriptions exact and attributed to the unknown writer. Do not translate them into safe and unsafe states. A return can show the chalk fading, the wax catching dust, or a later reader adding no mark because the old ladder has become too heavy to edit. That is a consequence of attention, not a persistent meter. The plan should keep the human body present: someone remembers a sound, swallows before crossing a room, or chooses to ask what the wall means. It should not ask the player to reproduce the warning or test the threshold. Recheck the current hazard and narrative owners before placement. If a passage cannot preserve the difference between an authored fear record and a calibrated measurement, omit it. The ladder is valuable because it shows how people draw a boundary when they do not possess a reliable answer. It should remain a drawing, a warning, and an open question. The final image should remain a ladder drawn by a frightened hand, not a calibrated instrument. Let the reader notice the red wax, the uneven circles, and the word above them, then decide whether to believe the wall, the witness, or the silence after the clicks. ## 5. Human center

The emotional center is a person trying to draw time before time becomes panic. The colored marks look like an answer but remain a personal record whose scale nobody can verify. The word RUN is loud because everything above it has been made quiet and geometric.

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains graffiti_geiger_click_ladder (The Click Ladder). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Click Ladder from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Click Ladder record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Click Ladder should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. **06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Click Ladder should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. **08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention graffiti_geiger_click_ladder or the surrounding region. **10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide radiation, Geiger, threshold, movement, escape, contamination, door, measurement, color-code, or emergency instructions. **11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. **12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Click Ladder should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Click Ladder because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention graffiti_geiger_click_ladder by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.
