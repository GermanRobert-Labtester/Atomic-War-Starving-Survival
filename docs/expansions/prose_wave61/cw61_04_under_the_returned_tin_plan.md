# EXPANSION CW61-04 — Under the Returned Tin

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 61: Radio, Labor, and What Returns

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_keepsake_returned_note — Under the Returned Tin.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Under the Returned Tin is a local graffiti posting triggered by friction_stolen_keepsake, available from day 45 with weight 0.7. It says the tin came back and nobody saw it return, that the invisibility was the point, that the contents are one button short, and that everyone has agreed aloud this is a fair price for learning the bunker has a thief who pays. This plan treats the note as a communal attempt to price harm and closure without adding a justice system, confession route, or keepsake inventory.

## 2. Story question

When the tin returns unseen with one button missing, and the room agrees aloud that the button is a fair price for learning the thief pays, what does forgiveness cost when it is spoken as accounting?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, triggerWorldFlag, minDay, and weight. It does not establish the tin’s owner, the missing button, the thief’s identity, the agreement’s sincerity, or what fair means to anyone who was hurt. The prose preserves those limits through existing keepsake, return, theft, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "the tin came back. nobody saw it come back, which was the point. what was inside is one button short. the thief kept the button. we have all agreed, out loud, that this is a fair price for learning we have a thief who pays." The expanded reading treats the returned tin as proof of an action and not proof of a settlement. Nobody saw it come back, which means the room receives the result without receiving the scene that made it possible. The missing button gives the return a small physical remainder. It can be a private souvenir, a sign of spite, a token of payment, or a detail whose meaning the speaker has not decided. The plan should not select one. The phrase fair price is the room speaking aloud to make a complicated harm easier to carry. Repetition does not guarantee agreement. Someone can say fair and still feel watched, grateful, angry, or unconvinced. A candidate passage may let the tin be handled in silence, set beside a blanket, or mentioned in a conversation that stops before forgiveness. These details keep the keepsake human without introducing an inventory or restitution system. The stolen-keepsake flag can support an existing return context, but it does not authorize a button item, a thief reputation, or a negotiation route. A later reader may know only that the tin is back and one piece is gone. That limited knowledge is enough to alter the room’s attention. It is not enough to declare justice complete. The group that agreed out loud should remain a broad source voice, not a named council or vote. Dissent can be a pause, a refusal to repeat the sentence, or a person who touches the lid and says nothing. The plan should give each response dignity. The strongest ending keeps the missing button unresolved while letting the returned object move through ordinary life again. Verify source attribution and the existing keepsake consumer before placement. If the consumer cannot distinguish return from repair, keep the vignette editorial. The tin can come home while the argument remains under it.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. **03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. **04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. **05 — return scale.** A return to Under the Returned Tin should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. **09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention graffiti_keepsake_returned_note or the surrounding region. **12 — emotional movement.** The movement through Under the Returned Tin should be felt as a change in attention. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.
