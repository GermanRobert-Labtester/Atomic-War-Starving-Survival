# EXPANSION CW61-03 — The Thief Knows This Wall

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 61: Radio, Labor, and What Returns

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** graffiti_keepsake_thief_knows — The Thief Knows This Wall.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Thief Knows This Wall is a local graffiti posting triggered by friction_stolen_keepsake, available from day 30 with weight 0.7. Its first-person text says the writer knows the wall is there and reads it every day on the way to the pump, identifies a tin under the third-bunk blanket where people will not look because they have already looked, and says the return will happen because the writer decides, not because the wall decides. This plan treats the note as a tense claim of agency and attention, without adding a theft investigation or hidden-item route.

## 2. Story question

When an anonymous thief says the tin is under the third-bunk blanket, says the wall has been read every day, and promises to return it by decision rather than coercion, what kind of agency can an accusation leave behind?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The posting record establishes the title, exact text, triggerWorldFlag, minDay, and weight. It does not establish the tin’s contents, the thief’s identity, the pump route, the third bunk’s occupant, or whether the promise is sincere. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing keepsake, theft, bunk, pump, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local text: "i know this is here. i read it every day on the way to the pump. the tin is under the third-bunk blanket where you will not look because you have already looked. when i bring it back it will be because i decided to, not because the wall decided for me." The expanded reading protects the thief voice from becoming a puzzle narrator. The writer says the wall is read every day on the way to the pump, which makes routine part of the confession, but it does not prove the writer knows every person who passes. The third-bunk blanket is named as a place where people have already looked, and that sentence can carry embarrassment, accusation, or a simple observation about expectation. It should not become a hiding tutorial. The promise to bring the tin back is equally careful. The writer claims a decision, not a command from the wall, so a later passage can ask whether agency survives after public attention has arrived. The keepsake itself should stay bounded. The source does not tell us what is inside, who owns it, or whether its return would repair anything. The stolen-keepsake flag offers an existing narrative context, not a reason to add a search route, a culprit identity, or an item ledger. A scene may put the pump sound in the distance, show a blanket edge disturbed, or let a reader trace the words without touching the bunk. Those are ways of making the note felt. They are not instructions for finding, confronting, or recovering an object. A return can show the tin absent, present, or discussed by someone who was not there when the note was written. The meaningful change is what the room believes about choice. One person may hear a boast, another a plea, and another a warning that the wall cannot make anyone honest. Keep all three possible without naming the thief. The strongest close gives agency back to the anonymous speaker while acknowledging the harm done to the person whose keepsake is missing. Recheck current keepsake ownership, source text, and consumer before selection. If the existing route requires a determinate culprit or item outcome, use the prose as reference and do not force the ambiguity into state.

### Editorial scale notes

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains graffiti_keepsake_thief_knows (The Thief Knows This Wall). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Thief Knows This Wall from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Thief Knows This Wall record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Thief Knows This Wall should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. The later reader may notice a different word, remember a different cost, or decline to repeat the account. The catalog facts remain stable unless a current authorized system says otherwise.

**06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Thief Knows This Wall should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. Let a person have a practical task, a private limit, a bad day, a memory that is incomplete, or a reason not to answer. Keep the human center larger than the prop.

**08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention graffiti_keepsake_thief_knows or the surrounding region. Confirm names, truth classes, branch outcomes, and display ownership. A matching phrase is not proof that a passage remains current.

**10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide theft, hiding, searching, pump, bunk, confrontation, coercion, surveillance, or recovery instructions. Do not add a thief identity, hidden-item mechanic, search route, keepsake inventory, or new stolen-keepsake flag. Preserve the exact posting and metadata. Keep that limit visible in scene language. A character may be afraid, cautious, angry, or wrong without the prose becoming an operational guide.

**11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. It may be a torn page, a spoken recollection, a label, a pause at a threshold, or a later sentence repeated with one word corrected. It should not look like a system specification inside the fiction.

**12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Thief Knows This Wall should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Thief Knows This Wall because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention graffiti_keepsake_thief_knows by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

The strongest callback is often a correction. Someone says a word too confidently, another person supplies the source’s narrower wording, and the room continues. That is enough movement for a scene whose subject is memory.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

Where a source names an institution, show its paperwork and its consequences without making the institution omniscient. A form can be current and still miss a person. A schedule can be obeyed and still be unjust.

The handoff question is simple: can a reviewer point from each important claim to a current source and from each proposed passage to an existing consumer? If either answer is no, keep the passage in editorial review.

The document’s length does not widen the canon. It gives the writer room to choose among small moments at The Thief Knows This Wall while keeping the source boundary visible in every form. A longer bank is useful only when its alternatives remain honest and distinct.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.

The last editorial pass should ask whether the passage could belong to someone standing near The Thief Knows This Wall with limited time, imperfect memory, and no need to impress a reviewer. If the answer is yes, the bank has done its work: it has made a place available for play without pretending the place belongs to the plan.
