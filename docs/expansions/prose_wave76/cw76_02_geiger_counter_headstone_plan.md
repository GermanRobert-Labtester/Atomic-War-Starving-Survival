## 1. Expansion thesis

The Geiger Counter Headstone is a local epitaph record. A pre-war Geiger counter is bolted to a river stone with copper wire and clicks irregularly when wind moves ash across its probe. The stone remembers Polina’s perimeter walks, maps, knowledge of hot spots, and the cost her body absorbed. This plan expands the memorial as labor and measurement without adding a radiation mechanic or operational reading.

## 2. Story question

When a counter still clicks in ash wind beside a stone that says Polina knew every hot spot but her body absorbed more than she recorded, what does a tool remember after its user is gone?

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains epitaph_b2_geiger_counter_headstone (The Geiger Counter Headstone). The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Geiger Counter Headstone from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. **04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. **08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention epitaph_b2_geiger_counter_headstone or the surrounding region. **11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. **12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Geiger Counter Headstone should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Geiger Counter Headstone because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention epitaph_b2_geiger_counter_headstone by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

The strongest callback is often a correction. Someone says a word too confidently, another person supplies the source’s narrower wording, and the room continues. That is enough movement for a scene whose subject is memory.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

Where a source names an institution, show its paperwork and its consequences without making the institution omniscient. A form can be current and still miss a person. A schedule can be obeyed and still be unjust.

The handoff question is simple: can a reviewer point from each important claim to a current source and from each proposed passage to an existing consumer? If either answer is no, keep the passage in editorial review.

The document’s length does not widen the canon. It gives the writer room to choose among small moments at The Geiger Counter Headstone while keeping the source boundary visible in every form. A longer bank is useful only when its alternatives remain honest and distinct.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.

The last editorial pass should ask whether the passage could belong to someone standing near The Geiger Counter Headstone with limited time, imperfect memory, and no need to impress a reviewer. If the answer is yes, the bank has done its work: it has made a place available for play without pretending the place belongs to the plan.
