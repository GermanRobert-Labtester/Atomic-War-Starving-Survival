# EXPANSION 76 — FORTY-ONE, CORRECTED

## A prose-first content expansion plan for Lina

### Wave 15: The Ordinary Measure

## 1. Creative promise

This expansion develops the existing Lina arc from the queue at Municipal Baths through shelter lessons and a later medical apprenticeship. Lina is seven, perhaps eight. She keeps a count adults have stopped keeping, wants to be useful rather than treated as charity, and expects an answer to be given twice when it matters. The story recognizes her competence without making her responsible for the adults around her.

The player can offer her a place in the shelter with her work and terms honored, or leave supplies with the queue's keeper with no conditions attached. In the recruited branch, later quests let the player teach or find a teacher, then confirm an apprenticeship. The prose makes those choices feel like the same child's life over time, not three separate reward gates.

## 2. One-sentence story

A child corrects the player's count at a temporary queue outside the baths, and the player must decide whether to offer a home with respect for her work or support the queue she has chosen, then later learn that teaching her means accepting that she checks the adult too.

## 3. Scope

This is a narrative expansion of current content, not a new child labor, education, health, water, or shelter system. It uses the existing three NPC quests and their current choices. It does not change recruitment conditions, day gates, the dead state, or the availability of the baths.

The story adds scene text, dialogue options, proposed journal lines, and branch-aware aftermath. It must map to existing character, encounter, quest, and NPC-arc owners. If the current state model cannot support an optional follow-up, remove that follow-up rather than adding a parallel flag.

## 4. Verified anchor: Lina

The character record identifies Lina as a foundling, seven or perhaps eight, at loc_public_swimming_baths. She was found keeping count of a queue because the adults had stopped. She hums when frightened and stops when she notices. She learns what she is shown once, then teaches it back wrong on purpose to see whether the adult was paying attention.

She wants to be useful, not charitable, and wants someone who answers twice. Her offers include queue order, small hands in small spaces, and the honest version of events. She will not say thank you on command or pretend not to be afraid. Her signature line is: “I counted. It is forty-one and you said forty. One of us is wrong and I showed my work.”

## 5. Verified anchor: the baths

The Municipal Baths description says the deep end was drained in the first winter and covered with mattresses as a shelter because the tiles held heat. The baths were later given up: the same tiles carried coughs and arguments so far that people left. The mattresses remain, and wet marks show where the roof drips. The site has thirty-eight rads per hour, moderate danger in the location description and danger six in the expedition row, with four distance ticks and two hours of travel.

The story does not show the building as a permanent sleeping camp in the present. Lina's queue encounter takes place at the baths, but can be staged at the entrance or a temporary distribution line. The old mattress rows are a visible history, not proof that a hundred people still sleep there.

## 6. Verified anchor: the current encounter

The existing NPC encounter is titled “Forty-One, Corrected.” Its description has Lina tally the player's column, correct the player's own number, and wait to see whether adults laugh, argue, or fix the count. The available choices offer her a place in the shelter with her work included and her terms honored, or leave supplies with the queue and let her keep her count of them.

The encounter attaches to the current quest and its choice IDs. This plan preserves those choices. It does not add an option to take the counting book, order her to follow, or make the player the queue's owner.

## 7. Verified anchor: the first quest

“The Girl Who Counts” is the existing first arc quest, available in its listed early-day window. Its synopsis says Lina is useful and the queue feeds useful; the shelter is the first place that has asked what she wants, which is to be useful and not charitable and to have answers given twice.

The take-in consequence says she considers the offer out loud and to the player's face, packs what fits in a tin, does not say thank you on command, and counts the shelter column out the gate accurately. The leave-supplies consequence says she writes the player's column in her book with supplies received, date, and condition. Both are fixed outcomes.

## 8. Verified anchor: later lessons

“Lessons Twice Given” is the existing shelter quest after the first encounter. The player may teach Lina themselves—arithmetic, doses, and the counting of small mercies—or find her a proper teacher among the shelter's survivors. In the first outcome, she corrects the player's sums in front of smaller children and copies dose tables into her book. In the second, she audits the teacher for a week; both parties report being the stricter one.

The content must respect her age. She can learn, teach back, ask questions, and correct an adult without being placed in charge of medical care. The player is still the responsible adult when she is a pupil.

## 9. Verified anchor: apprenticeship

“Small Hands, Steady Ones” is the existing late quest. It can confirm Lina's apprenticeship and put her name on the infirmary roster. The consequence says the roster entry is in her own hand, she checks ward counts twice daily professionally, and she hums almost never.

This plan uses that outcome as authored canon but does not introduce an earlier apprenticeship or treat her as a trained medic at the baths. Any scene that involves dressing a wound, reading a dose, or catching a medical error belongs to the late apprenticeship context and should be reviewed by the current medical content owner.

## 10. The central human tension

The adults have let a child carry a count because the count matters and because they stopped doing it. Lina has turned that responsibility into a way to be useful. The player can offer her another life without pretending the queue has no claim on her time, or support her current place without using supplies to buy her affection.

The story does not make either choice into a simple rescue or abandonment. The shelter offer includes her terms, work, and a place among its residents. Leaving supplies includes no conditions and leaves her at the baths. The player must listen to what the choice says and not demand gratitude as proof that it worked.

## 11. The child is not the queue's authority

Lina keeps an accurate count; that does not make her responsible for who receives supplies. Adults decide which supplies are available, who is in line, and what to do if needs conflict. The player can ask her to verify the count, but not to allocate rations, make medical triage calls, or take blame for an adult's shortage.

The encounter may show her correcting a number. It should not show adults treating her as a clerk they can leave alone with a high-stakes decision. Her usefulness is real. It cannot become a substitute for adult care.

## 12. The line's physical setting

The current location supports the former pool, tile, mattress rows, roof leaks, and the sound that once drove sleepers away. Stage the present queue outside the drained deep end or near the entrance where visitors can leave. Do not fill the water or make the queue a new camp.

A simple distribution line can form for the encounter without claiming a permanent institution. The source establishes that Lina is at the baths and keeps a queue; the exact supply being distributed is not specified. Use generic “supplies” in dialogue until a current quest or inventory source identifies them.

## 13. Count before character introduction

The player sees a column of people waiting and a small book held against Lina's chest. She counts aloud, reaches the player, and says forty-one. The player says forty. She asks them to show their work. The scene lets her make the correction before an adult introduces her.

The first response menu can offer laugh, argue, check, or ask her to count again. The “check” path shows the player recounting. The count is forty-one. A player who laughs is not locked out, but Lina asks them to count properly. A player who argues receives the same evidence: the names are marked, and one person had been missed.

## 14. What forty-one means

The number belongs to the current line, not to the whole bath population, shelter, faction, or city. The scene should never say “forty-one people live here.” Lina counted those waiting in the moment. A later queue may have a different number.

This precision protects her skill. Her correction is not magic foresight. The player said forty because they missed someone; Lina showed her count. The content should not turn the phrase into a prophecy, a code, or an answer to a hidden census question.

## 15. The missed person

The person missing from the player's count can be a quiet adult standing behind a post or a person carrying a bag that obscures them. This is a proposed staging detail. Do not assign the person a medical condition or make them invisible in a way that implies supernatural movement.

Lina points to where the person stands and asks whether the player wants to count again. If the player does, they get forty-one. If they say the person is not really in line, Lina asks who decides that. The adult queue caller can answer. Lina does not decide the person's place herself.

## 16. Lina's test

Lina may intentionally repeat the player's answer incorrectly: “So you counted forty because the person behind the post is not waiting?” The player can correct her, ask her to repeat the line, or notice that she is testing whether they listened. She then says that teaching something once is enough only if the other person can show it back.

The moment draws from the character record but must not turn her into a tiny lecturer. She is a child playing a test she invented. The adults can smile, but they should not make her perform for the room. If the player is confused, an honest “I don't know what you mean yet” lets her explain.

## 17. The queue's keeper

A role-labeled queue keeper stands near the entrance and is responsible for the supply handoff. The source does not name this person. They are not Lina's parent or legal guardian unless a verified character record establishes that relationship. They can say that Lina has been keeping the count and that the player can leave supplies with them.

The keeper does not speak for Lina's wishes. They can confirm whether the supplies were received, show the date, and preserve the queue's order. The player must address the child directly when making the shelter offer, then let her decide.

## 18. A first answer from the keeper

When the player asks who is in charge, the keeper says, “Of the supplies or of Lina?” If the player asks about the supplies, they answer. If the player asks who decides where Lina lives, they say that Lina has been asked and the player should ask her. The line gives the player a clear boundary around the child's decision.

The keeper can be imperfect or tired without being negligent. Their prior failure to count is a source fact, but not proof that no adult cares for Lina. The story should not invent abuse or abandonment beyond the current description. It is enough that adults stopped counting and she stepped in.

## 20. The book's page

## 21. A player who praises her

The player can say the count is good, say she was right, or say “thank you for correcting me.” Lina may answer that the count is right and ask whether the player wants to check the next row. She does not have to smile or thank the player in return.

If the player says “you're very grown up,” Lina says she is a child who counted correctly. This line is proposed and should be kept only if it sounds like her. The story resists praise that turns competence into adult responsibility. Praise the work without asking her to carry the room.

## 22. A player who laughs

If the player laughs at being corrected, Lina watches to see if they recount. If they do, she accepts the correction. If they keep laughing, she turns the book away and asks the queue keeper to continue. The player is not punished with a child crying. The consequence is that the player has made it harder to work together.

A later return can still resume the encounter if the current quest supports it. The player does not unlock a special apology reward by being kind. A simple correction is enough: “I laughed. I should have counted again.” Lina can answer, “Now you did.” The scene moves on.

## 23. A player who argues

If the player says the line only has forty people, Lina asks them to count each person they can see. The keeper confirms the queue includes the person behind the post. The number remains forty-one. No dice roll or perception statistic is used.

The player can insist, but the scene should not reward denial with a different count. Lina may ask the keeper to settle the line and then step aside. Adults retain responsibility for moving the distribution. The player may remain skeptical, but the text does not lie to accommodate their choice.

## 24. A player who admits uncertainty

If the player says they are not sure, Lina offers to count with them. She gives one number at a time and waits for the player to repeat it. If they lose track, she starts again without scolding. The test becomes a small collaborative task, not an assessment of intelligence.

This path demonstrates the “answer twice” want. The player can ask her to repeat a count, and she does. If the scene uses subtitles, repeat the full count visibly rather than making the player remember an unvoiced list. The content should support comprehension rather than gate the quest behind arithmetic.

## 25. The first ask: “What do you want?”

After the count, the player can ask why Lina is keeping order. The exact phrasing is supported by the current quest synopsis, though the spoken line is proposed.

The player can ask whether she wants to come to the shelter, whether she wants the queue to keep going, or whether she wants to tell the player what would make the offer fair. The shelter offer is not a rescue speech. The player can say there is a place and explain what Lina would be asked to do. The current choice wording already includes her work and her terms. The story should let her ask what “place” means: where she sleeps, who answers when she asks, whether she must stop counting, and whether people will call the supplies charity.

The player should answer only from the shelter's actual current capacity. If they do not know where she would sleep, they can say so and ask the current shelter authority. ## 27. Lina counts the offer

When the player makes the offer, Lina considers it aloud, the way the current quest consequence specifies. This is not a child reciting a contract as a puzzle. It is her way of checking that an adult means what they said. The adult may correct their own wording. Lina may ask whether she can keep her book. The shelter offer is not complete until she has been heard.

## 28. What fits in a tin

The current outcome says what she packs fits in a tin. The story can show her choosing a small tin and checking each object once. The player can offer to carry the tin, ask whether she wants to carry it herself, or step aside. Lina decides. If the player carries it without asking, she says, “That's mine.” The player can hand it back. The scene gives her possession and choice without inventing a special heirloom.

## 29. She does not say thank you on command

An adult in the queue may say that Lina should thank the player. Lina does not. The player can tell the adult she does not owe a performance, say nothing, or ask the adult not to speak for her. Lina continues checking the tin.

The scene should not make a thank-you the emotional payoff. She can later show appreciation through her own choices, but she must not be forced to say words because an adult expects them. On the take-in branch, Lina counts the shelter column as it leaves the baths. The player stands beside the adult responsible for the group, not at the head of it as a new authority. Lina calls the count out loud. It is correct. The adult checks it independently and confirms the number.
