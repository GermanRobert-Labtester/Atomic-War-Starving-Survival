# CONTENT EXPANSION CW32-05 — The Button Kept for South

## An unsent letter sits beside a watch schedule whose names are rewritten by people who still have to take a shift.

### Prose Wave 32: The Paper People Leave Behind

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first playable-content expansion plan with scene drafts, diegetic records, conversation fragments, and conditional callbacks.
**Content bank:** 32 story beats with four alternative authored forms per beat (128 candidate passages).
**Current location anchor:** `loc_forward_roster_camp` — The Forward Roster Camp.
**Tone:** material, restrained, human, and careful with uncertainty.
**Canon sensitivity:** current location and linked authored records define known facts; old plans or JSON presence do not prove a current runtime route.
**Scope:** game-content plan and original prose drafts only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Forward Roster Camp is pitched in the narrow space between the Exchange and the Garrison checkpoint. Its watch schedule is chalked on a container door in a hand that writes like meeting minutes; four names fill a shift, then get crossed off and rewritten as people rotate. A sealed unsent letter from Private Colm V. says the courier line is for orders and does not go south. It preserves a button for a boy and a sentence addressed to Maren. Elder Sava also has a record association with this camp, including conditional late states about mediation or withdrawal. The expansion keeps those threads separate unless a current source joins them: no one is made the letter’s recipient, child, author, or subject by proximity. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

What does an unsent promise mean when the rota keeps moving and the road named in the letter does not?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The location description gives the camp’s position between two authorities, its unsigned exterior, and a chalk watch schedule with four names per shift that are crossed out and rewritten. The discoverable letter is sealed and unsent, authored by Private Colm V. and addressed to Maren; it mentions a boy and a button but does not identify either. Elder Sava’s character and arc records associate her with this location and with existing conditional states. No record links Sava to Colm’s letter, Maren, or the boy. The expansion treats these as parallel records rather than one family or chain of command.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The letter remains unsent and sealed in the source. Maren and the boy have no names in the document; the text does not authorize a surname, location, or current fate. The schedule has a four-person shift pattern but the location excerpt does not give a stable roster or exact time. Elder Sava’s arc state depends on existing choices and completed quests; do not flatten her initial, pressured, mediator, and withdrawn states. Her association with the camp does not connect her to Colm’s letter. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the difference between a promise carried and a duty performed. A person can keep a button for a child and still fail to send the words that explain it. Someone else can cross a name off a watch and write another because the work must continue. Sava’s rituals turn meal, remembrance, and silence into repeatable labor; they are not a commentary on Colm’s private message. The camp becomes human through separate obligations sharing the same cramped ground. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Colm’s letter is private, plain, and unsent; do not rewrite it as an order or publish its recipient’s story. The camp schedule is brief and administrative. Sava speaks in practical daily units and does not sell grief as doctrine. If a scene uses her, select text only under the exact existing arc state. Do not add a Garrison commander or Exchange negotiator to explain the geography. New roster voices remain unnamed roles. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player can read the letter without opening it further, notice the schedule’s repeated edits, speak to Sava through an existing branch, or decline to carry a private message. They cannot send Colm’s letter, identify Maren, assign the boy to a family, or reorder the camp’s watch. Existing quest choices and conditions own Sava’s outcomes. Any choice about sharing the letter is prose framing unless a current system already handles its persistence. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not identify Maren or the boy, state that they received or did not receive the button, or infer the author’s eventual location. Do not connect Colm to Elder Sava, her faction, or the camp’s watch without evidence. Do not turn the chalk schedule into a new duty mechanic or operational security guide. Do not imply the camp’s position makes it neutral between its neighbors. No letter-delivery quest, roster state, patrol, combat result, or relationship effect is authored. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Disturbance — the envelope stays sealed

The letter’s unsent status is the opening condition; the plan does not make opening it a new player action.

### 2. Discovery — the chalked rota

Four names to a shift are rewritten as people rotate, with no fixed cast invented.

### 3. Interpretation — promise and duty

The button line and the schedule represent separate obligations, not a shared household.

### 4. Complication — Sava’s current branch

Her existing state changes what she can say, but does not explain the letter.

### 5. Choice — keep, quote, or leave private

The player can respect the letter’s unsent status rather than create a delivery route.

### 6. Return — the next shift is rewritten

The camp continues its work while the letter remains a private record with unresolved recipients.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Use the location schedule and sealed letter, with Sava appearing only in a verified state.

### Interlinked

A later Sava callback can discuss names read aloud only if the NPC arc and interaction owner expose that branch.

### Wild card

Build a two-column page where the left records a changing shift and the right preserves a sentence that was never sent; the columns never share names.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.

If that owner or consumer is absent, retain the prose as a draft rather than inventing a route or system. Character counts are Unicode code-point counts of the saved Markdown files.
