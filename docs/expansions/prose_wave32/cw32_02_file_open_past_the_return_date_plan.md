# CONTENT EXPANSION CW32-02 — File Open Past the Return Date

## The missing Convoy 9 manifest and the painful difference between a known death and an unknown load.

### Prose Wave 32: The Paper People Leave Behind

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first playable-content expansion plan with scene drafts, diegetic records, conversation fragments, and conditional callbacks.
**Content bank:** 31 story beats with four alternative authored forms per beat (124 candidate passages).
**Current location anchor:** `loc_transit_authority_hq` — Transit Authority.
**Tone:** material, restrained, human, and careful with uncertainty.

## 1. Expansion thesis

At the Transit Authority, a route map and a logistics file belong to different ways of counting movement. One record follows Convoy 9 from departure to an expected return, then reports that it did not return. A much later infirmary entry confirms that driver Pell died; the cargo and vehicle remain unknown, and the file remains open. The expansion is not a search-route quest. It is a prose story about what an institution can close, what it must leave open, and the people who read a status line after the person it names can no longer correct it. A pre-war merger with Civil Defense and the later use of transit systems as shelter infrastructure provide history, not a verdict on this trip. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

When one part of a record is settled years later, what does it mean to leave the rest open?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The Transit Authority location describes wall maps with annotations outside any published timetable, while the discoverable document is a Year 2 transport manifest for Convoy 9. It states that the convoy did not return, later records driver Pell’s death through an infirmary register entry, and leaves cargo and vehicle status unknown. The file is still marked open. World-history entries describe the authority’s merger with Civil Defense and the use of transit tunnels as shelters. These are separate source contexts; the prose does not treat the location’s enigmatic map annotations as the convoy route.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The manifest supplies its own date, route label, return expectation, and status. Driver Pell’s death is a later infirmary-recorded fact with a delayed entry; this does not establish what happened to the escort, cargo, or vehicle. “File open” remains the manifest’s status and does not create a player quest. The location’s strange grease-pencil marks are not mapped to Convoy 9. Historical merger text explains institutional context but does not prove who planned this trip or why it failed. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the clerk who must replace one uncertainty with one fact and resist replacing all the others. A name can be marked deceased and still belong to a file that is not resolved. Someone has to keep the record useful to the next reader without pretending that the cargo arrived or that every traveler’s end is known. Let the moral pressure sit in the difference between a late correction and a complete explanation. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

The manifest voice is compact and administrative. A later reader may ask plain questions, but the file answers only with its recorded fields. A proposed clerk speaks with care for categories and people alike. Do not write the logistics office as omniscient or the convoy as a heroic last stand. Keep the route and cargo details abstract in new prose, avoid operational directions, and do not turn the location’s map rumors into supernatural or secret railway canon. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player can read the manifest, compare the later death entry as cited by the source, ask what remains unknown, or decline to call the record complete. They cannot use this prose to locate Convoy 9, recover cargo, confirm the escort’s fate, or change Pell’s record. Existing expedition, map, and journal systems retain authority for any actual route or discovery. This expansion gives agency through how the player interprets and repeats a claim. Do not add a moral-choice menu simply to make quiet prose interactive. Preserve the player’s refusal, ability to leave, and the authority of the characters who own their words.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not quote the manifest as a travel guide or reproduce its cargo as a scavenging promise. Do not invent an explanation for the convoy’s non-return, a fate for its escort, or a location for the vehicle. Do not treat Pell’s delayed death entry as proof of where or why he died. Do not connect the map’s unusual annotations to this file without evidence. No quest, route, inventory cache, combat encounter, rescue, or persistent mission flag is authored. Keep the setting fictional and physically grounded. No absent person receives a biography merely to heighten emotion. Technical and medical context remains descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Disturbance — expected return passes

Open with the gap between an expected date and a non-return status, without staging the trip.

### 2. Discovery — the manifest fields

The player reads what was planned and what the office could confirm.

### 3. Interpretation — a delayed death entry

A later fact narrows uncertainty about Pell while leaving other fields untouched.

### 4. Complication — cargo remains unknown

The file cannot prove that the shipment was delivered, lost, or found.

### 5. Choice — repeat only supported facts

The player can preserve the distinction between a confirmed person and an unresolved convoy.

### 6. Return — the open file

A later reader sees that “open” remains meaningful even after a name is updated.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

One inspection of the manifest and a short clerk exchange; no travel scene.

### Interlinked

A conditional archive callback may present the later infirmary reference only if the existing consumer can preserve its delayed-entry date.

### Wild card

Structure the close as two status stamps on the same file: one dated much later, one still blank.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
