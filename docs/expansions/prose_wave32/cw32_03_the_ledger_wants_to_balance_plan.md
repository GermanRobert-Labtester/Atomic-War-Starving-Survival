# CONTENT EXPANSION CW32-03 — The Ledger Wants to Balance

## A market book carries an admitted discrepancy while the boats keep their distance from one another.

### Prose Wave 32: The Paper People Leave Behind

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Shallows makes trade across water without inviting anyone aboard another hull. Its nine boats are tied into a raft, and its etiquette is older than the market. A Salt Scales Syndicate ledger records an imbalance, a carrier’s denial, and a repair fund that appears through the difference; the account-keeper admits saying nothing. Nearby, Nomi Fisk trades across faction lines and explains the hull rule once. These records do not establish that Nomi took part in Harlan’s quarter or that the ledger’s discrepancy is theft. The expansion asks what trust costs when one person’s accounting keeps a shared place working and another person refuses a second explanation. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

How can a market keep trading when its book admits that its balance depends on what it leaves unsaid?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The location describes nine boats tied as a raft, hand-to-hand trade at gunwale height, and a no-boarding rule older than the market. The discoverable Harlan ledger names trade and an accounting discrepancy, says the discrepancy funds repairs at Lock Gate Four, and ends with the keeper’s admission that the ledger balances when it wants to. Nomi Fisk is a separate character record: she trades with all four factions, wants side-neutral cargo, offers transport and access, and will not explain hull etiquette twice. The source does not place her in Harlan’s transaction.

## 5. Human center

The human center is a market worker asked to trust a balance that admits it was shaped by discretion. The relay repair may matter to people who need the market, but a useful result does not erase the carrier who denies a shortage or the keeper who stays silent. Nomi’s boundary is quieter and clearer: she explains how to meet at the waterline once, and the other person has to decide whether they have listened. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Harlan’s ledger voice is precise until it reaches the point where he admits what he has not said. Nomi is practical and does not repeat herself for effect. The patrol record stays observational. Use proposed anonymous buyers only as editorial roles. They cannot force Harlan to disclose a motive or use a choice here to alter the Lock Gate Four repair fund. Existing faction and trade systems own prices and consequences. ## 11. Narrative sequence

### 1. Disturbance — a balanced book admits otherwise

Open on the ledger keeper’s final admission rather than treating the book as neutral arithmetic.

### 2. Discovery — the market rule

The player learns the location’s hand-to-hand norm and Nomi’s explicit boundary.

### 3. Interpretation — a short return and an unspoken choice

The ledger shows a discrepancy and a stated repair relation, but no complete explanation.

### 4. Complication — records that do not corroborate each other

The patrol debrief is placed nearby without being used as evidence for Harlan’s quarter.

### 5. Choice — ask, trade, or step back

The player can choose what they trust while existing owners keep all state.

### 6. Return — maintenance outlives the entry

A later reader sees the market still depends on recorded work, without treating the account as resolved.

## 12. Creative variants

### Grounded

Center the story on one ledger page and one clear exchange with Nomi.

### Interlinked

Use the patrol debrief as a second source only when the current archive surface preserves its date and author.

### Wild card

Make the central object a blank receipt line where the clerk could write who authorized the difference, then chooses not to invent a signature.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
