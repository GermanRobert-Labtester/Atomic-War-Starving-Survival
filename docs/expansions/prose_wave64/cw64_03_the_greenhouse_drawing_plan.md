# EXPANSION CW64-03 — The Greenhouse Drawing

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 64: Children’s Drawn Worlds

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** art_b2_003 — The Greenhouse.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Greenhouse is an authored artwork record in childrens_artwork_batch_2.json. A resident child, age ten, makes a detailed pencil drawing of the greenhouse interior, including bed rows, grow lights, mushroom shelves, chicken coop, and bee access tube. Labels identify each section and the proportions are accurate. The child has studied the Botanist’s work, and the Botanist says the drawing is more accurate than their own records. This plan expands the drawing as attention, apprenticeship, and the humility of an expert seeing a place through a child’s hand.

## 2. Story question

When a ten-year-old’s pencil drawing maps bed rows, grow lights, mushroom shelves, a chicken coop, and a bee tube more accurately than the Botanist’s records, what does learning look like when observation becomes a second archive?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the artwork ID, artist label, title, medium, day, tags, and exact description. It does not establish the drawing’s exact geometry, the child’s name, which records are surpassed, or whether the Botanist formally adopts the page. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing artwork, greenhouse, botanist, education, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local record: "A detailed technical drawing of the greenhouse interior. The drawing shows the bed rows, the grow lights, the mushroom shelves, the chicken coop, and the bee access tube. Labels identify each section. The proportions are accurate. The child has been studying the Botanist work. The Botanist says the drawing is more accurate than their own records." The greenhouse drawing can carry the quiet drama of an expert being made to look again. A ten-year-old has not produced a formal map in the record; the child has studied the Botanist work and rendered bed rows, grow lights, mushroom shelves, the chicken coop, and the bee access tube with accurate proportions and labels. That list matters because it turns routine into a chain of relationships. A bed row is not only a row, a light is not only a lamp, and a tube is not only a line. On the page they become parts of one room that the child has learned by patient attention. A candidate scene may begin with the Botanist comparing a familiar record against the drawing and discovering one corner that adults had stopped naming. The child need not explain the discovery. The praise can remain awkward, especially if the adult is used to being the one who knows. A conversation may ask whether the page is more accurate because it is newer, because it notices more, or because it has not yet become tired. No answer needs to become doctrine. The labels should stay attached to the artwork and should not be turned into a new greenhouse interface. The accurate proportions do not establish a technical coordinate system, crop yield, animal count, bee route, or lighting schedule. They establish a relationship between observed place and careful hand. A return passage can show the Botanist making room for the drawing beside older records, then consulting both without erasing either. The child’s apprenticeship remains informal and human. The page teaches the adult how to see one room again. That is enough consequence for the prose.

### Editorial scale notes

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains art_b2_003 (The Greenhouse). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Greenhouse from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Greenhouse record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Greenhouse should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. The later reader may notice a different word, remember a different cost, or decline to repeat the account. The catalog facts remain stable unless a current authorized system says otherwise.

**06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Greenhouse should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. Let a person have a practical task, a private limit, a bad day, a memory that is incomplete, or a reason not to answer. Keep the human center larger than the prop.

**08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention art_b2_003 or the surrounding region. Confirm names, truth classes, branch outcomes, and display ownership. A matching phrase is not proof that a passage remains current.

**10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide greenhouse, growing, lighting, mushrooms, chickens, bees, technical drawing, navigation, maintenance, or agricultural instructions. Do not add a greenhouse map, child apprenticeship system, crop authority, drawing mechanic, or new artwork owner. Preserve the exact record and its limits. Keep that limit visible in scene language. A character may be afraid, cautious, angry, or wrong without the prose becoming an operational guide.

**11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. It may be a torn page, a spoken recollection, a label, a pause at a threshold, or a later sentence repeated with one word corrected. It should not look like a system specification inside the fiction.

**12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Greenhouse should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Greenhouse because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention art_b2_003 by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

The strongest callback is often a correction. Someone says a word too confidently, another person supplies the source’s narrower wording, and the room continues. That is enough movement for a scene whose subject is memory.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.

Where a source names an institution, show its paperwork and its consequences without making the institution omniscient. A form can be current and still miss a person. A schedule can be obeyed and still be unjust.

The handoff question is simple: can a reviewer point from each important claim to a current source and from each proposed passage to an existing consumer? If either answer is no, keep the passage in editorial review.

The document’s length does not widen the canon. It gives the writer room to choose among small moments at The Greenhouse while keeping the source boundary visible in every form. A longer bank is useful only when its alternatives remain honest and distinct.

A final reading should sound like a person who has lived with the place, not like a catalog that has learned to emote. Keep the nouns concrete, keep the claims attributed, and let the last sentence leave a little air around the player’s choice.

The last editorial pass should ask whether the passage could belong to someone standing near The Greenhouse with limited time, imperfect memory, and no need to impress a reviewer. If the answer is yes, the bank has done its work: it has made a place available for play without pretending the place belongs to the plan.
