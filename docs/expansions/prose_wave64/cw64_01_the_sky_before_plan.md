# EXPANSION CW64-01 — The Sky Before

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 64: Children’s Drawn Worlds

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** art_b2_001 — The Sky Before.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Sky Before is an authored artwork record in childrens_artwork_batch_2.json. It is a large sheet of blue charcoal strokes with a sun circle and rays, a green rectangle for grass, and stick figures holding hands. The eight-year-old artist, Lima-1, says a mother told them the sky was blue, that they have never seen blue, and that this is what they think blue looks like. The Teacher has pinned it to the schoolroom wall. This plan expands the artwork as memory made from testimony, keeping the child’s image distinct from a verified surface view.

## 2. Story question

When an eight-year-old draws blue charcoal strokes, a green rectangle, and a sun above figures holding hands after hearing that the sky was blue, what does a child inherit from a color never seen?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the artwork ID, artist label, title, medium, day, tags, and exact description. It does not establish the mother, the schoolroom layout, the child’s later knowledge, or whether the blue charcoal matches any actual sky. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing artwork, child, schoolroom, sky, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local record: "A large sheet of paper covered in blue charcoal strokes. At the top, a circle (the sun) with rays. Below, a green rectangle (grass). In the middle, stick figures holding hands. The child says: My mother told me the sky was blue. I have never seen blue. This is what I think blue looks like. The Teacher has pinned it to the schoolroom wall." The page can be read as a small schoolroom weather system. Blue does not arrive as a verified sky; it arrives as charcoal pressed across paper by a child who has been given a sentence and asked to make a world. The sun at the top keeps its circle because circles are available forms, while the green rectangle gives the ground a firm edge. The holding figures make the picture social before any reader knows who they are. A future passage may linger on the Teacher lifting the page, finding a clean place on the wall, and choosing to preserve the child’s first attempt rather than improve it. The mother remains a voice inside the record, never a character to be completed by invention. A reader may remember a different description of blue, but that later description should sit beside the drawing rather than overwrite it. The child’s statement about never seeing blue is the ethical hinge: imagination has crossed a distance, and the distance must stay visible. If the drawing appears near another child’s account of ash, the contrast should be allowed to hurt without turning either page into a lesson. If it appears near a practical note about lamps, the difference between powered light and inherited sky should remain legible. The sun can be warm in the lyric sense without creating a temperature fact. Grass can be green in the child’s imagined image without establishing an outdoor biome. The figures can hold hands without becoming a family roster. A return passage may show the paper’s corners softening, a new child asking who made the blue, or the Teacher answering only that Lima-1 was told about it. Such a return keeps the artwork alive as a question. The source gives enough for wonder, witness, and restraint. It gives no permission to complete the world beyond the page.

### Editorial scale notes

**01 — source fidelity.** The anchor remains art_b2_001 (The Sky Before). Every proposed sentence must stay attached to the cited local record, and every inference must remain visibly an inference. The source can support a human moment without supporting a new mechanic, route, authority, or outcome.

**02 — material attention.** The strongest details in this bank are physical: a boundary, a surface, a line of sight, a document, a marker, a schedule, or a room that still has its old shape. Keep those details ordinary enough to be believed and specific enough to distinguish The Sky Before from another location.

**03 — voice separation.** The three roles in this plan do different work. One notices the immediate object, one protects the record’s limits, and one carries the emotional consequence. Do not let all three speak in the same polished register or give one person the authority of the whole The Sky Before record.

**04 — player space.** The player is allowed to observe before deciding what the observation means. A pause, refusal, question, or incomplete answer can be the passage’s consequence. Do not turn every emotional beat into a prompt, reward, relationship delta, journal flag, or measurable improvement.

**05 — return scale.** A return to The Sky Before should make the earlier passage newly legible, not rewrite it. The later reader may notice a different word, remember a different cost, or decline to repeat the account. The catalog facts remain stable unless a current authorized system says otherwise.

**06 — uncertainty.** Uncertainty is authored content here. It is not a hole that needs to be filled with a hidden culprit, a technical explanation, a secret route, or an invented survivor. When a source leaves a name, cause, or outcome open, the prose can make that openness felt without pretending to solve it.

**07 — dignity.** People associated with The Sky Before should not be reduced to the hazard, policy, machine, or record that surrounds them. Let a person have a practical task, a private limit, a bad day, a memory that is incomplete, or a reason not to answer. Keep the human center larger than the prop.

**08 — content scale.** The 48-beat bank is deliberately editorial. A production owner may select one passage, several passages, or none. The size of this document is a writing resource, not a request to implement every candidate or to create a parallel narrative registry.

**09 — continuity check.** Before placement, compare this bank against newer data, especially records that mention art_b2_001 or the surrounding region. Confirm names, truth classes, branch outcomes, and display ownership. A matching phrase is not proof that a passage remains current.

**10 — hazard boundary.** The plan’s boundary is explicit: Do not provide art, charcoal, sky, outdoor, color, child, schoolroom, sunlight, or memory instructions. Do not add an art system, surface route, color-learning mechanic, child profile, or new artwork authority. Preserve the exact record and its limits. Keep that limit visible in scene language. A character may be afraid, cautious, angry, or wrong without the prose becoming an operational guide.

**11 — diegetic surface.** A record, notice, conversation, or scene should look like something a survivor could encounter in the existing content owner. It may be a torn page, a spoken recollection, a label, a pause at a threshold, or a later sentence repeated with one word corrected. It should not look like a system specification inside the fiction.

**12 — emotional movement.** The movement through The Sky Before should be felt as a change in attention. The player begins by seeing an object or condition, then hears a person attach meaning to it, then decides whether to carry that meaning forward. Nothing larger is required for the passage to matter.

**13 — open ending.** Close on a human image rather than a verdict. A hand lowers, a page remains folded, a name is repeated once, a room continues its ordinary work, or a landmark stays where it was. The ending should leave room for the player and the current canon to do the next piece of work.

**14 — handoff.** If a content owner accepts a passage, preserve its attribution and keep the owner’s existing state, save, and routing seams. The handoff is complete only when the selected text, its source record, its trigger, and its audience all agree. Otherwise this remains editorial prose for review.

The proposed bank keeps returning to The Sky Before because repetition is part of survival writing. A person sees the same surface twice and notices a different cost. That change belongs to attention, not to an invented state.

A selected passage may mention art_b2_001 by its display name, by a local nickname already present in the source, or by an ordinary physical detail. Do not add a second identifier merely to make the prose sound larger.

Keep proposed objects portable in meaning but bounded in authority. A page can be carried, a marker can be seen, and a sentence can be repeated without creating an item registry, a universal rule, or a new save section.

Let silence have more than one cause in the prose: respect, fatigue, uncertainty, refusal, distance, or the simple fact that the current record ends. Never use silence as permission to invent a secret.

Where a source names a hazard, write the people’s relationship to that hazard rather than a method for defeating it. Fear, caution, grief, and disagreement are content; operational guidance belongs to an authorized system owner, not this bank.
