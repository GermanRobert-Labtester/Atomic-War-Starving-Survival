# EXPANSION 96 — A Bowl Before the Pass

## A prose-first settlement-content expansion for the Pilgrim’s Hearth’s peace-bound rest, the almoner’s careful labels, and the guide who will not turn a difficult pass into a promise.

### Wave 19: Places Kept by Hand

## 1. Creative promise

The Pilgrim’s Hearth offers shelter that does not demand testimony. Silas keeps a geothermal sanctuary, Hanna tends an apothecary, and Tobias guides travelers who need help with the ice-bound gullies. The prose honors warmth as a real kindness while refusing to make sanctuary, faith, or a guide’s knowledge into ownership of a traveler. This is a prose-first game-content plan. It offers candidate scenes, dialogue, notices, work records, and repeat-visit text. It is not a feature roadmap and does not claim that any proposed prose is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

At the mountain Hearth, a bowl of broth and a place to rest come before any account of who a traveler is or where they mean to go.

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_settlement_pilgrim_hearth` describes a mountain sanctuary warmed by natural geothermal steam radiators at Switchback Pass. Monks provide hot broth, herbal remedies, and peace-bound rest; the location record lists danger 2, 2.5 travel hours, and 10 rads per hour. Character data places Prior Silas, Almoner Hanna, and Tobias the Pilgrim there. Silas guards the priory and offers sanctuary, broth, and spiritual solace. Hanna compounds herbal teas, burn salves, and clean bandages. Tobias is a pilgrim scout who guides vulnerable travelers through ice-bound gullies. None has an NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. Do not conflate this settlement with other pilgrim characters or locations. The character records point to the location, and the location records provide the setting description. The selected characters have no current NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. Their authored presence does not prove that a player can reach the location through a particular route.

## 4. Fixed source facts and new proposal

The current records establish the sanctuary, geothermal warmth, offered food and remedies, and Tobias’s guiding role. They do not give a doctrine, an initiation, a specific traveler’s illness, or a guaranteed pass route. Do not invent any of those as canon. The offered teas, salves, and bandages do not establish a treatment outcome. All visitors, guest-book notes, conversations, and follow-up scenes are proposals, and the characters can decline to speak or guide. Existing display names, character biographies, wants and offers, location descriptions, danger ratings, and radiation values remain fixed. Every new visitor, line, record, working relationship, disagreement, and follow-up in this plan is proposed content. Do not turn a proposed scene into a claim about existing canon or a guaranteed gameplay path.

## 5. Emotional center

Hospitality can mean letting somebody sit down without making them prove they deserve the chair. A rest stop need not become a conversion scene, a medical examination, or a contract for future help. Silas can keep the room open; Hanna can explain what her labels say; Tobias can decline to guide when the route or the traveler’s condition does not allow an honest promise. The place is not a prop for the player’s growth. The people here keep their own reasons, rhythms, and right to refuse a conversation.

## 6. Point of view and voices

Keep the people distinct: Silas offers a room and a form of solace; Hanna names what is in her care; Tobias answers for his willingness and the pass, not for a guest’s choices. Warmth is not a claim of safety, and kindness is not an invitation to ask again after a refusal. Narration stays close to observable work: who carries a note, what is posted, when a task stops, and who is present to answer. Private motives belong in a person’s own words or a record they have agreed to share. Secondary characters keep distinct duties and do not become a chorus explaining the settlement.

## 7. Proposed content sequence

Begin with an ordinary arrival and the offered bowl. Let the guest choose whether to give a name, ask for a remedy, or request guidance. Later scenes can show Hanna’s labels, Silas keeping the quiet-room boundary, and Tobias explaining that the pass remains difficult. These are proposed content beats and do not claim an existing encounter or travel connection. This sequence is a writing proposal only. It does not introduce a quest ID, encounter registration, branching system, route connection, or persistent state. Dialogue can contain everyday responses, but no response is a hidden test or a substitute for an authored choice owner.

## 8. Human consequence without scorekeeping

A guest may leave rested without becoming a follower; a label may be corrected without a cure being promised; a route request may be declined without shaming the person who asked. Do not add a faith score, illness system, pilgrimage counter, route unlock, or healing guarantee. Let consequence appear in changed wording, a task assigned to its actual owner, a limit restated, or a return that is easier or more awkward. Do not imply numeric rewards, punishments, faction reputation, or new economic rules.

## 9. Location and safety discipline

No pass instructions, weather windows, medicinal recipe, dosing, or medical diagnosis. The prose can show a guide refusing an unsafe promise and a guest choosing to wait. Environmental risk stays grounded in the current location description. Do not teach hazardous access, underwater salvage, explosive handling, boiler repair, cliff travel, or any other dangerous procedure. A character can close a work area or refuse a request without the prose explaining a workaround.

## 10. Text and implementation boundaries

These are candidate prose fragments for a content review pass. Before implementation, verify the current data schema and consumer, author any needed IDs through the owning content path, and confirm route eligibility with current map and encounter authorities. Keep public and private records distinct. This plan changes no production JSON, code, save data, map, quest catalog, or master catalog.

Each story beat below is presented in four different forms: a playable scene, a diegetic record, a conversation, and a return vignette. These forms are alternatives for editorial selection, not four required encounters. Select a coherent subset, trim it for the game’s actual text surface, and remove any line that exceeds its speaker’s knowledge or consent.
