# EXPANSION 92 — The Salt Has to Dry

## A prose-first settlement-content expansion for Brine-Pan Hollow’s ration board, weighed curing salt, and the hands that keep the steam works in service.

### Wave 19: Places Kept by Hand

## 1. Creative promise

Brine-Pan Hollow can be shown through a shift change, a scale reading, and the patience required before salt is ready to leave the pans. Marshal Varn’s discipline, Elena Kosh’s exact trade, and Petyr’s maintenance work give the settlement three distinct voices without turning it into an economy feature pitch. This is a prose-first game-content plan. It offers candidate scenes, dialogue, notices, work records, and repeat-visit text. It is not a feature roadmap and does not claim that any proposed prose is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

At the tidal Brine-Pan Hollow, the council’s ration rules and the merchant’s honest measure meet the boilerman’s less visible work of keeping the curing salt possible.

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_settlement_brine_pans` is a fortified salt camp in a tidal estuary. Its location description says evaporation pans and steam condensers produce curing salt and preserved provisions under salter-council watch; the record lists danger 2, a two-hour travel value, and 15 rads per hour. Character data places Marshal Varn, Elena Kosh, and Petyr the Boiler there. Varn heads the salter council and manages pan shifts and salt rationing. Elena trades weighed drums of curing salt and preserved provisions. Petyr tends evaporation flues and steam condensers. Their character entries list trade wants and offers, but none has an NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter in the inspected catalogs. The character records point to the location, and the location records provide the setting description. The selected characters have no current NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. Their authored presence does not prove that a player can reach the location through a particular route.

## 5. Emotional center

Salt is not glamorous in a place where food must last. A weighed drum holds the labor of people who cannot all be named on its label. The emotional center is the distinction between rationing and punishment: Varn can keep a rule legible; Elena can show what a sale weighs; Petyr can stop work when a machine needs attention. No one of those jobs owns the whole settlement. The place is not a prop for the player’s growth. The people here keep their own reasons, rhythms, and right to refuse a conversation.

## 6. Point of view and voices

The settlement’s work is visible in turns, scales, and labels. Let the voice remain restrained: a clear rule may be kind without being soft, and a fair price may still be difficult to pay. Narration stays close to observable work: who carries a note, what is posted, when a task stops, and who is present to answer. Private motives belong in a person’s own words or a record they have agreed to share. Secondary characters keep distinct duties and do not become a chorus explaining the settlement.

## 7. Proposed content sequence

Open on an ordinary shift rather than a shortage. Let the player meet the public ration board, watch a fair measurement, and hear how a hold or correction is recorded. Later fragments can show a council discussion and an agreed handoff. These are candidate scene beats; no sequence or quest is present in the current catalogs. This sequence is a writing proposal only. It does not introduce a quest ID, encounter registration, branching system, route connection, or persistent state. Dialogue can contain everyday responses, but no response is a hidden test or a substitute for an authored choice owner.

## 8. Human consequence without scorekeeping

A person leaves knowing when a decision will be reviewed, a measure is corrected in public, or a job is handed to the next shift. Do not add a ration score, salt-production counter, settlement reputation, automatic trade discount, or a moral reward for agreeing with the council. Let consequence appear in changed wording, a task assigned to its actual owner, a limit restated, or a return that is easier or more awkward. Do not imply numeric rewards, punishments, faction reputation, or new economic rules.

## 9. Location and safety discipline

Keep the process description non-instructional. Steam, hot surfaces, brine, tidal footing, and radiation are setting conditions, not a player tutorial. Never provide a method for entering a boiler area, clearing a flue, or altering a condenser. Environmental risk stays grounded in the current location description. Do not teach hazardous access, underwater salvage, explosive handling, boiler repair, cliff travel, or any other dangerous procedure. A character can close a work area or refuse a request without the prose explaining a workaround.

Keep public and private records distinct. This plan changes no production JSON, code, save data, map, quest catalog, or master catalog.
