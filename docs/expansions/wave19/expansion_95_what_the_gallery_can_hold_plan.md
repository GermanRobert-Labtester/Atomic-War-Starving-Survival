# EXPANSION 95 — What the Gallery Can Hold

## A prose-first settlement-content expansion for Slate Hollow’s elected pit foreman, building-stone trade, and the lines a quarry crew keeps visible when work must stop.

### Wave 19: Places Kept by Hand

## 1. Creative promise

Slate Hollow’s underground work can carry tension without a collapse spectacle or a lesson in explosive use. Darek is responsible for shoring and extraction, Valya for a trade whose terms can be read, and Jarek for a specialized job whose procedures stay with the qualified worker. The content lets a stopped task remain a meaningful piece of the workday. This is a prose-first game-content plan. It offers candidate scenes, dialogue, notices, work records, and repeat-visit text. It is not a feature roadmap and does not claim that any proposed prose is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

At Slate Hollow, a stone order can wait while the crew decides whether the gallery is ready to carry another day of work.

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_settlement_slate_hollow` describes a subterranean quarry enclave cut into slate galleries in the High Scarp. Pit crews extract building stone and mill hones shielded from weather; the record lists danger 3, a three-hour travel value, and 15 rads per hour. Character data places Steward Darek, Valya the Factor, and Jarek the Blaster there. Darek is an elected foreman overseeing structural shoring and stone extraction. Valya trades structural slate, millstones, and fine oil sharpening hones. Jarek is an experienced blasting technician handling industrial charges and gallery adit clearing. The current catalogs inspected contain no NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter for this trio. The character records point to the location, and the location records provide the setting description. The selected characters have no current NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. Their authored presence does not prove that a player can reach the location through a particular route.

## 4. Fixed source facts and new proposal

The location description does not state that a gallery has collapsed or that the enclave faces an immediate emergency. Do not invent a trapped crew, a named accident, or an imminent blast. The quarry setting, building stone, mill hones, shoring, trade, and Jarek’s role are the source anchors. All work stoppages, order disputes, records, and return scenes in this pack are proposed. No explosive recipe, charge plan, or access procedure belongs in player-facing text. Existing display names, character biographies, wants and offers, location descriptions, danger ratings, and radiation values remain fixed. Every new visitor, line, record, working relationship, disagreement, and follow-up in this plan is proposed content. Do not turn a proposed scene into a claim about existing canon or a guaranteed gameplay path.

## 5. Emotional center

A closure can be a decision made by people doing their jobs, not a defeat imposed by the story. Valya may lose time on an order when Darek stops work; Darek may owe a clear explanation without disclosing a dangerous method; Jarek may accept that another person owns the final call on the gallery. The people carry the consequence without a disaster being staged for the player. The place is not a prop for the player’s growth. The people here keep their own reasons, rhythms, and right to refuse a conversation.

## 6. Point of view and voices

Keep each role audible. Darek’s authority is elected and accountable; Valya’s is commercial; Jarek’s is technical. No one explains the gallery to the player by turning dangerous work into a set of directions. Narration stays close to observable work: who carries a note, what is posted, when a task stops, and who is present to answer. Private motives belong in a person’s own words or a record they have agreed to share. Secondary characters keep distinct duties and do not become a chorus explaining the settlement.

## 7. Proposed content sequence

Begin with an ordinary order at the quarry gate. A sample and a public work status let the player understand why extraction and trade have separate owners. Later fragments revisit a stopped area only if current content establishes that it has reopened. The proposed sequence does not assert new accessible galleries or a travel connection. This sequence is a writing proposal only. It does not introduce a quest ID, encounter registration, branching system, route connection, or persistent state. Dialogue can contain everyday responses, but no response is a hidden test or a substitute for an authored choice owner.

## 8. Human consequence without scorekeeping

Show a public order carried forward, a trade date renegotiated, a task assigned to the next qualified hand, or a closed line left visible. Do not add production totals, a mining system, equipment durability, explosion minigames, or a hidden approval score. Let consequence appear in changed wording, a task assigned to its actual owner, a limit restated, or a return that is easier or more awkward. Do not imply numeric rewards, punishments, faction reputation, or new economic rules.

## 9. Location and safety discipline

Keep industrial work at the level of responsibility and outcome. Do not publish blast details, construction instructions, load limits, entry routes, or salvage directions. A character may state that a work area is closed without explaining how to enter it anyway. Environmental risk stays grounded in the current location description. Do not teach hazardous access, underwater salvage, explosive handling, boiler repair, cliff travel, or any other dangerous procedure. A character can close a work area or refuse a request without the prose explaining a workaround.

These are candidate prose fragments for a content review pass. Before implementation, verify the current data schema and consumer, author any needed IDs through the owning content path, and confirm route eligibility with current map and encounter authorities. Keep public and private records distinct. This plan changes no production JSON, code, save data, map, quest catalog, or master catalog.
