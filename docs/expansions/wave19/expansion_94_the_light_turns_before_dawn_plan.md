# EXPANSION 94 — The Light Turns Before Dawn

## A prose-first settlement-content expansion for Cape Beacon’s Fresnel lens, freshwater cisterns, kelp work, and the people who keep coastal traffic warned about the reefs.

### Wave 19: Places Kept by Hand

## 1. Creative promise

Cape Beacon is not only a lighthouse. It is a commune with water to account for, kelp to dry, optics to maintain, and people who must decide how much of their dangerous work becomes public. The content treats guidance as a responsibility with limits, not a promise that every sea passage is safe. This is a prose-first game-content plan. It offers candidate scenes, dialogue, notices, work records, and repeat-visit text. It is not a feature roadmap and does not claim that any proposed prose is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

At Cape Beacon, Lightkeeper Maren keeps the lens turning while Kira tends kelp and Orlov returns with salvage whose origin matters as much as its value.

## 3. Verified local anchor

`loc_settlement_cape_beacon` describes a coastal lighthouse community on a windswept bluff, with freshwater cisterns, kelp drying racks, and prism optics. It has danger 4, a 3.5-hour travel value, and 25 rads per hour. Character data places Lightkeeper Maren, Orlov the Diver, and Kira the Weaver there. Maren guards the tower and keeps its Fresnel lens turning to guide coastal traffic past reef hazards. Orlov salvages optical glass, brass fittings, and waterproof wiring from submerged shipwrecks. Kira braids kelp nets, dries iodine kelp, and manages the commune’s herbal dispensary. None has a current NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. The character records point to the location, and the location records provide the setting description. The selected characters have no current NPC arc, quest, or narrative encounter entry in the inspected catalogs. Their authored presence does not prove that a player can reach the location through a particular route.

## 4. Fixed source facts and new proposal

The source establishes the lens, the reef hazard, cisterns, kelp work, and the three occupations. It does not identify a named shipwreck, a current rescue, a particular crew, an exact tide, or a safe route. Do not invent these as history. Every new visitor, line, record, working relationship, disagreement, and follow-up in this plan is proposed content. Do not turn a proposed scene into a claim about existing canon or a guaranteed gameplay path.

## 5. Emotional center

A warning light is not a guarantee. It tells a person something about the coast; it cannot make the coast obey. Maren can be responsible for the lens, Kira for her plant and net work, and Orlov for what he brings back without any of them being responsible for a traveler’s decision to take a risk. The place is not a prop for the player’s growth. The people here keep their own reasons, rhythms, and right to refuse a conversation.

## 6. Point of view and voices

The commune’s labor is shared without being interchangeable. Give each person a different register: public warning, careful plant work, and salvage with uncertain provenance. Let the coastal setting remain present through sound and weather rather than explanatory lore. Narration stays close to observable work: who carries a note, what is posted, when a task stops, and who is present to answer. Private motives belong in a person’s own words or a record they have agreed to share. Secondary characters keep distinct duties and do not become a chorus explaining the settlement.

## 7. Proposed content sequence

A proposed visit begins on the bluff with the public view of the tower, then moves through the cistern and drying racks. A later scene can bring an object to the table and ask what may be recorded about its source. The sequence is candidate prose only; character presence does not establish map reachability or an encounter schedule. This sequence is a writing proposal only. It does not introduce a quest ID, encounter registration, branching system, route connection, or persistent state. Dialogue can contain everyday responses, but no response is a hidden test or a substitute for an authored choice owner.

## 8. Human consequence without scorekeeping

A public notice can clarify what the light does not promise; a cistern count can be read without exposing private household use; a salvage record can name what is known and leave the rest blank. Do not add navigation bonuses, an ocean route system, diving minigames, harvest output, or medical effects. Let consequence appear in changed wording, a task assigned to its actual owner, a limit restated, or a return that is easier or more awkward. Do not imply numeric rewards, punishments, faction reputation, or new economic rules.

## 9. Location and safety discipline

No navigation, diving, lens-servicing, or herbal-treatment instructions. The light can warn; it cannot guarantee. Present any dangerous task through a decision to wait, decline, or leave it to the responsible worker. Environmental risk stays grounded in the current location description. Do not teach hazardous access, underwater salvage, explosive handling, boiler repair, cliff travel, or any other dangerous procedure. A character can close a work area or refuse a request without the prose explaining a workaround.

Keep public and private records distinct. This plan changes no production JSON, code, save data, map, quest catalog, or master catalog.
