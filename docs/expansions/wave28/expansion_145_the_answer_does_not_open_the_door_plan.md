# EXPANSION 145 — The Answer Does Not Open the Door

## A powered intercom carries a human answer through a building that several records describe differently.

### Wave 28: What Survives the Record

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 25 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_arcology_sector_4` — Arcology Sector 4.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Arcology Sector 4 begins with a powered intercom and a person who still answers from behind a sealed door. The location describes descendants who stayed inside; dated expedition and journal records describe Warlord presence in the eastern wing, while a wildlife observer records vultures near the same floors. These records can coexist as partial views or reveal an unresolved continuity seam. The expansion keeps the voice behind the door unnamed and refuses to make one report speak for every resident. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What does a listener owe an unseen person when the intercom works but the records disagree about who occupies the building?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location record says the blast doors remain powered and closed, someone inside answers the intercom, and descendants of the pre-war elite never left. It describes trade under hunger and asks that negotiation remain polite. A day-68 journal by the Veteran and a day-70 expedition report led by the General document Warlord activity in the eastern wing, including cooking smoke and sentries; the report says the west wing was unoccupied during that approach. The Hunter’s day-66 wildlife log places vultures on the ninth floor of the eastern wing and notes that their presence may indicate carrion, but does not identify its source. These are dated, situated records; none identifies the intercom voice.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the intercom answer, resident-descendant description, Warlord occupation reports, and vulture log distinct. Do not identify the voice as a Warlord, a resident, or a particular descendant. The journal’s visual interpretation of black fabric as a possible flag is explicitly uncertain. The reports describe moments, not a current occupancy guarantee. Preserve the human need behind trade without inventing a price or history. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is conversation across a closed boundary. An answer can be a choice to keep talking, not permission to enter. Treat the residents as people rather than a puzzle or a caricature of pre-war wealth. The Warlord report and the vulture observation carry their own human and ecological uncertainty. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Keep intercom lines short and practical. A person listening cannot see the speaker. Report language stays dated and observational; no character suddenly explains the whole arcology. Let politeness sound like work done under pressure rather than a cure for danger. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location description, one intercom premise, the day-68 journal, the day-70 expedition report, and day-66 wildlife log are the authored anchors. The report does not establish an intercom contact, and the location does not name a visitor. No new door access, trade scene, faction relationship, or safe-route claim is introduced. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may answer, ask one question, wait, or end the call. No response opens a door or creates an alliance. Existing character or faction arcs retain their own owners and outcomes; these drafts do not add a new negotiation feature. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No entry tactics, weapons, surveillance, or route instructions. Do not expose the intercom speaker, name residents, infer that vultures prove a killing, or depict Warlords and descendants as one group. No invented trade inventory, coercion, or outcome. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
