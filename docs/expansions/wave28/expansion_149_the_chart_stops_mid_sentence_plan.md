# EXPANSION 149 — The Chart Stops Mid-Sentence

## A page breaks off before it can explain a person; the next reader’s duty is to keep that silence humane.

### Wave 28: What Survives the Record

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 25 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_hospital_psych_wing` — Hospital Psychiatric Wing.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Hospital Psychiatric Wing’s location description gives a small number of grave details: panic marked the first week, charts stop mid-sentence, visitors report feeling watched in empty rooms, sealed opioids were not distributed, and canvas restraints were folded. The plan treats these as environmental traces, not as license to invent patient histories, diagnoses, abuse, treatment, or a supernatural presence. Its narrative follows unnamed workers and readers trying to preserve privacy when records are incomplete. The drama comes from what they refuse to claim and how care can remain visible even when the chart cannot finish its account. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How can a record admit that it stopped without turning the person beyond its edge into a mystery for someone else to solve?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The only direct location record describes the Hospital Psychiatric Wing, panic in the first week, charts that end mid-sentence, visitor reports of feeling watched in empty rooms, sealed and undistributed opioids, folded canvas restraints, and a radiation figure of 50 rads per hour in the ward. No linked named patient, staff member, encounter, treatment record, or route was found for this anchor. The location details do not establish who used an item, why a record ends, whether a report is accurate, or what a visitor meant by watched. This plan does not convert the radiation value into advice or an access decision.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Preserve the location description without adding diagnoses, identifiable patients, staff biographies, reasons for the incomplete charts, use histories for the sealed medication or folded restraints, or proof of a presence in empty rooms. Visitor impressions remain impressions. The care context must never become a horror reveal, and object descriptions must not imply treatment, harm, or player loot. No new medical, inventory, access, or radiation mechanic is proposed. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the ordinary responsibility of handling another person’s incomplete record. A clerk wants to protect what cannot be reconstructed; a visitor wants their words copied without being mocked or promoted into fact; a custodian believes a folded object should remain described only as folded. Compassion appears through careful boundaries, private margins, and the choice to leave a page unfinished. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use low-volume language: a chart clerk’s precise omissions, a visitor’s first-person uncertainty, a custodian’s practical naming of what is in the room. No one performs an exposition speech about mental illness. Avoid clinical jargon, sensational verbs, diagnostic labels, and any implication that distress makes someone dangerous. Let the incomplete page remain a page, never a cliffhanger about a patient. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read a public-facing note if an existing owner chooses to place one, decline to read it, or ask who authored it. Do not make the player diagnose, treat, restrain, medicate, identify, investigate, or judge an unnamed patient. No response selection earns a resource or reveals private history. The absent person retains the right not to be reconstructed. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
