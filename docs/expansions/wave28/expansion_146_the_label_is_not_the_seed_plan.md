# EXPANSION 146 — The Label Is Not the Seed

## A cold archive preserves names and possibilities, while later reports disagree about what was recovered.

### Wave 28: What Survives the Record

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 25 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_subterranean_seed_vault` — Subterranean Seed Vault.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Subterranean Seed Vault was built to preserve agricultural memory beyond a human lifetime. Its records now disagree about the scale and form of what remains: thousands of tins in a pre-war history, foil-sealed lines in the location entry, 47 jars in one expedition report, and ampoules in another patrol sequence. A journal gives one jar a name and a human touch. A wildlife log places a molerat colony in the rear chamber, while another report calls that chamber unsearched. This expansion keeps preservation, uncertainty, and stewardship on the page without inventing a new recovery. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

When the archive names more seeds than any one report can count, how can a reader protect both the catalog and the people who hope to grow them?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location calls the vault a century-scale store of sealed seed lines and a ledger of what they were, with a heavily trapped approach and frozen doors. The pre-exchange history describes thousands of sealed tins catalogued by hand. Suki Tanaka’s day-61 journal describes labelled jars and one wheat jar; a day-63 expedition report records 47 sealed jars and 12 damaged jars with viability unknown. Patrol debriefs on days 61 and 79 instead describe 14 and then 3 seed ampoules, reporting cryogenic failure and a sealed vault. A day-52 wildlife log describes blind molerats in a rear chamber, while the day-63 report calls the rear chamber unsearched. A later day-88 patrol record reports a fatal tunnel collapse. These sources require chronology and content-owner review before any new recovery prose.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep each inventory with its source, date, and unit: tins, jars, and ampoules are not silently added together. Viability remains unknown wherever the report says so. Suki’s journal line remains hers. The molerat account is one wildlife log and does not override the separate rear-chamber status in the expedition report. Preserve the later fatal collapse as an existing record; do not add access, another casualty, or an unapproved route. No planting outcome is promised. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is someone holding a jar carefully because its label points to a future they cannot personally guarantee. The vault matters because people imagined that someone afterward would want to grow things again. The prose can honor that hope while respecting the botanists, scouts, and casualties who encountered different limits. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Use catalog language beside a small human gesture. Let Suki’s journal carry the emotional line and keep the survey voice exact about uncertainty. Never make the seed vault speak or convert a technical report into an extraction manual. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location, world-history entry, Suki’s journal, expedition report, seed-vault patrol records, and wildlife log are existing anchors. Their records conflict in inventory, status, and rear-chamber access. Confirm the narrative owner and chronology before selecting any additional prose. No new seed item, survey, retrieval route, or storage system is claimed. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read a label, accept that a count is uncertain, or leave the vault’s future to its current owners. No choice grants a new jar, changes viability, or decides which community receives the seeds. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No trap designs, entry steps, cryogenic handling procedures, planting instructions, crop-yield guarantees, medical advice, or new casualty. No combining counts, opening sealed stock, or converting a journal’s emotion into player dialogue. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
