# EXPANSION 147 — The Mine Mouth Waits

## Salt keeps winter stores alive, while every promise to bring it back passes beneath a roof that may not hold.

### Wave 28: What Survives the Record

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 25 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_collapsed_salt_mine` — Collapsed Salt Mine.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Collapsed Salt Mine is valuable for a practical reason: its dry salt helps preserve meat through winter. That work continues in the shadow of a roof that has been giving way for years. Existing content sends a repeatable supply quest into the galleries for materials needed by Petyr at the Brine-Pan boiler shed, and a separate final-wishes step asks the player to escort an unnamed person to the mine entrance. This expansion keeps the salt, the roof, the boiler request, and the unnamed wish distinct rather than turning them into a new mining system. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

What can a person promise about a mine whose salt matters to the living but whose roof keeps negotiating its surrender?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location describes a century-old mine, massive caverns, salt used to preserve regional meat through winter, ten rads per hour, scattered shoring timber, and a roof whose creak travels farther than light. The repeatable quest Boiler Descaling Haul is given by Petyr at the Brine-Pan boiler shed; it asks for neutralizing agents and copper scrap from the mine, with a seven-day cooldown and a salt reward. A separate final-wishes step says to escort {name} to the mine entrance. That placeholder does not identify the person, their wish, or any relationship to Petyr. No mine-field report currently supplies a named miner or a safe route.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the location’s salt-preservation role, roof danger, and acoustic detail. Petyr’s repeatable haul is a separate quest whose materials are requested for the Brine-Pan boiler; do not turn that into instructions for chemical handling or mine repair. The final-wishes placeholder remains unnamed and is not linked to the quest provider. Do not promise salt stock or a successful return. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is winter food made possible by work people keep accepting, and by a person who wants to see the mine mouth for reasons the data does not reveal. The writing should honor labor and uncertainty without romanticizing a dangerous roof as a rite of passage. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Let a boiler worker speak in supply-list language and let the escort request remain personal but incomplete. The mine’s creak can interrupt a sentence; no one explains exactly what will fall. Do not introduce an old miner or a voice trapped in the shaft. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The location record and the two linked quest references are the anchors. There is no direct expedition report attached to this location ID, and no confirmed route, mine inventory, or exact final-wishes character. Any selected scene requires the current quest and wishes owners to verify placement. No new harvest loop or mine-access feature is proposed. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may accept or decline Petyr’s existing haul, and may fulfill or decline the separate escort request where its owner permits. This plan does not add choices, cooldown effects, reputation, or rewards. The two references must not be merged into one visit. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No shoring procedures, roof testing, excavation, salt extraction, chemical use, or route planning. Do not name {name}, invent the wish, or imply that low radiation makes the mine safe. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
