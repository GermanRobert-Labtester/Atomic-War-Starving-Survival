# EXPANSION 144 — The Hiss Does Not Pause

## A steam-fed ruin holds the promise of heat beside records that refuse to agree on what can be approached.

### Wave 28: What Survives the Record

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 25 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `location_geo_thermal_plant_ruins` — Geo-Thermal Plant Ruins.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.
**Canon sensitivity:** cited location and linked content records define the available facts; any other route or content reachability remains unproven.
**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Geo-Thermal Plant Ruins are already loud before anyone speaks: steam keeps hissing through the site, and the ground refuses to feel settled. The location description promises valuable machinery, while dated reports disagree in units, position, and the possibility of approach. A journal hopes the plant could reduce the shelter’s fuel burden; a later field report describes warm roots and unusual birch growth, but its explanation is a hypothesis. This expansion keeps those hopes, observations, and uncertainties in separate hands. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How can a shelter preserve its hope for heat without turning a dangerous, contradictory set of records into a promise?

## 3. Verified local anchor

The location gives continuous steam, unstable ground, boiling mud, an ambient value of 45 rads per hour, and potentially valuable plant equipment. Elena Vasquez’s day-47 journal records visible steam and a hope that geothermal heat might reduce fuel use, while attributing the idea that the wells are self-feeding to Marcus. A day-46 patrol record describes 85 uSv/h at a stated distance and calls the perimeter lethal; a day-50 expedition report gives 45 uSv/h at the perimeter and notes fresh footprints but no hostile contacts. These are source-specific measurements, at different positions and in incompatible units; no conversion or safety conclusion is proposed. A day-70 wildlife report describes a warm-rooted birch grove and offers an ecological hypothesis. The discovery manifest also assigns boiler, hydrophone, and deep-borehole records to this producer although those records name distinct installations.

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Keep the journal, patrol report, expedition report, wildlife log, location description, and discovery manifest as separate sources. Do not normalize radiation units, compare the readings as if they were one survey, or reproduce approach instructions. The fuel saving is a journal hope, not a delivered result. Marcus’s well theory is second-hand speculation. The botanical account is an observer’s hypothesis, not established mechanism. Manifest entries about other facilities are not new observations at this plant. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is the distance between need and promise. A person short of fuel can be right to want heat and still be wrong to treat a hope as a working supply. The writing should respect that tension, and respect the people who choose to mark a boundary instead of entering it for a better answer. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, disagree with, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Let a journal speak in unfinished plans and let a patrol report speak in boundaries. The mechanic can hope, the observer can notice, and the archivist can decline to make their voices agree. Avoid exposition that turns industrial salvage into a tutorial. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

The site has a location and expedition row, plus several dated narrative records. The manifest’s producer links do not prove that every source record originates there, and the records differ sharply in equipment and setting. No new route, heat-system outcome, or new site-inspection sequence is implied. Verify the current content owner and record provenance before selecting any fragment. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the journal’s hope, read the patrol’s refusal, or decline to turn either into a plan. No passage makes a player responsible for recovering machinery or delivering heat. Do not introduce a new choice, reward, or settlement state. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

No procedures for entering the plant, testing ground, handling valves, measuring dose, routing steam, or salvaging equipment. Do not convert units or treat proposed engineering as proven. No claim that the birches clean contamination or make the ruin safe. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
