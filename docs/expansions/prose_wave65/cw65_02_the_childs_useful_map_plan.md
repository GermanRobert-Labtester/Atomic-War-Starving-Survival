# EXPANSION CW65-02 — The Child’s Useful Map

## A prose-first ecology-and-migration story collection about people, memory, and what the record cannot settle.

### Prose Wave 65: Children’s Instrumented Witnesses

## Batch brief

**Content type:** original game-content proposal with scene, diegetic-record, conversation, and conditional-return drafts.
**Content bank:** 48 distinct beat pairings, each with four alternative authored forms (192 candidate passages).
**Current local anchor:** art_b2_007 — Map of the Tunnels.
**Tone:** grounded, human, restrained, material, and careful about what a damaged record can prove.
**Scope:** game content only; no production code, JSON data, route, quest, flag, simulation, or save changes.

## 1. Expansion thesis

Map of the Tunnels is an authored artwork record in childrens_artwork_batch_2.json. A resident child, age eleven, makes a hand-drawn map of the bunker corridor system oriented correctly with north at the top. DORMITORY, KITCHEN, MEDICAL, WORKSHOP, GREENHOUSE, and GENERATOR ROOM are labelled. Ventilation ducts are dotted lines and water pipes are solid blue lines. The Architect says the map is 90% accurate and more useful than the official schematic for navigation purposes. This plan expands the drawing as practiced attention, partial authority, and the social question of who gets to make a place legible.

## 2. Story question

When an eleven-year-old maps the bunker with north at the top, labels every working section, and draws air as dots and water as blue lines, what does a useful map owe the people who trust it?

## 3. Verified local anchor and source records

The cited record establishes the artwork ID, artist label, title, medium, day, tags, and exact description. It does not establish the child’s name, the missing ten percent, current corridor changes, the official schematic’s condition, or whether the Architect formally uses the page. The prose preserves those unknowns through existing artwork, child, bunker, architect, map, journal, or narrative consumers. Verbatim local record: "A hand-drawn map of the bunker corridor system. The map is oriented correctly (north at top). Each section is labelled: DORMITORY, KITCHEN, MEDICAL, WORKSHOP, GREENHOUSE, GENERATOR ROOM. The child has included the ventilation ducts as dotted lines and the water pipes as solid blue lines. The Architect says the map is 90% accurate and more useful than the official bunker schematic for navigation purposes." The fuller reading treats accuracy as a relationship between a page and a lived route, not as permission to replace the current navigation authority. The child’s north arrow is a decision to make the underground world answer to a direction it cannot visibly confirm. Dotted air and blue water give hidden infrastructure a public grammar. The missing ten percent is valuable because it prevents the drawing from becoming a perfect diagram. A candidate passage may ask whether the absent portion is a forgotten door, a new obstruction, an unnamed room, or simply the child’s chosen silence. The Architect’s praise can carry practical respect and professional discomfort at once. The map is useful because someone looked at the place with fresh eyes, but usefulness does not settle ownership. A return can show a resident holding the drawing beside an official schematic, tracing the same corridor and finding that both documents remember different kinds of truth. The source does not establish a navigation UI, route reward, or map update system. It supports a child’s contribution to shared orientation and an adult’s humility. Preserve the labels exactly, keep the nine-tenths claim attributed, and let the remaining tenth protect the bunker from false completeness. The page can be placed near a public route notice only as a conversation between kinds of knowledge. The notice can be current and operational; the drawing can remain a child’s memory of corridors, pipes, and air. A passage may follow an adult tracing the blue line with one finger, stopping at the blank where the missing tenth begins, and deciding not to correct it in front of the child. Another reader may find the map more useful because the dotted lines show what official paper omitted, while a third reader may need the schematic because the artwork is old. These disagreements make the map social. The north arrow is not a claim that the bunker is simple. It is a promise that someone tried to give direction a place. Let the child’s page contribute attention without becoming a route authority.
