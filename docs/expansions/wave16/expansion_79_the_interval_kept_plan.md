# EXPANSION 79 — THE INTERVAL KEPT

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Emil Soren’s honest notebooks at the Understory Relay and the cost of naming a signal too soon.

### Wave 16: Marks That Let Others Pass

## 1. Creative promise

This is a patient investigation conducted openly enough that a patron cannot own the result. A repeated signal can be recorded and compared while remaining unexplained. Emil’s embarrassment over the early paper is not a confession for the player to cure; it is evidence he keeps beside later work. New passages supply listening scenes, notebook pages, and competing interpretations while leaving the receiver unknown. This is an authored content pack, not a feature roadmap. It supplies proposed playable prose, dialogue, field notes, and repeat-visit aftermath around current ASHFALL data. It does not claim any new line or scene is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

Emil Soren’s honest notebooks at the Understory Relay and the cost of naming a signal too soon. The player learns that a mark earns trust only when its limits remain visible to the person who has to use it.

## 3. Verified local anchor

The Understory Relay is a transmitter in a parking structure stairwell. Its broadcast location changes between stairwells to resist shutdown. The door log lists call times, never names, in multiple hands. Emil is a signals technician at this location. His record says he has four notebooks of repeats, published an early solution later found wrong, and warns patrons before accepting funding. He wants paper that takes pencil and reel-to-reel batteries. The quest offers funding a fifth notebook after reading its warning or returning the funding and removing the patron’s name. The late arc says the pattern may resolve to a station keeping time for someone; it does not identify that receiver. The current narrative encounter has no fixed location restriction in its record. The character’s authored location and quest chain are the narrative anchor; an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or new map connection.

## 4. New prose and fixed canon

The current character, location, quest, encounter, and arc facts above are fixed. Each new visitor, phrase, work slip, journal fragment, staging detail, and repeat visit below is a proposal. Recheck each proposal against current data before implementation. This plan changes no resource yield, faction ownership, route access, character death, or quest choice ID.

## 5. The emotional center

The reel turns at a steady speed. The stairwell lamp does not. Emil puts the early solution on top of four notebooks, face up, then lays the current interval table beside it. The correction does not erase the old answer; it explains what the first measurement missed. He asks the player to read the warning before deciding whether to fund another book. The receiver clicks once between ordinary static. Emil marks the time without explaining it. This small scene is the scale for the pack. The player can help, misunderstand, ask, or leave; the story does not confuse closeness with permission. The anchor character remains a worker with a life outside the player’s visit.

## 6. Dialogue sample

Proposed exchange: The player asks whether Emil still has the bad paper. Emil says it paid for the batteries. The player says he could put it away. Emil replies that the next reader might then think the fifth book began in a clean room. When asked whether he knew it was wrong, he says he knew it was attractive before he knew it was wrong. He turns the page toward the player and says the warning comes first: the next book’s corrections are part of what the patron funds. Player responses may be shorter or silent. The scene still works if the visitor says nothing. A pause does not imply that a hidden option waits behind it.

## 7. The current character arc

First, listen with the four notebooks and the early solution visible. Then let listeners disagree about repeats without making one of them a fool. Reach the existing funding choice. Later scenes show the fifth notebook’s continuing analysis or the boxed work after the refund. This plan adds no decoding interface or communications service. Keep first, evolved, and late states distinct. Do not show a late-state outcome before its required choice or quest completion. Do not create another hidden ending. If a player has not met the character, do not imply they have been waiting specifically for the player.

## 8. Point of view and scene grammar

The narration stays close to what the visitor can verify: the sound a part makes, where a line is marked, what remains on a page after correction, who chooses to wait, and who has begun the next task. It does not report another person’s private thoughts as fact. If a proposal gives someone a feeling, that feeling belongs in permitted speech or a personal record.

## 9. Cast and voice

Emil gives method before mystery. Listeners arrive with practical needs, memories, or curiosity, and none is automatically right about the carrier. The player can help with paper, power, and careful reading, but does not purchase a conclusion. Emil remains the person responsible for his own analysis and corrections.
## 10. Choice discipline

The current quest choices are the only major branches in this plan. Small responses may change the next line or which insert appears, but do not introduce a second hidden branch structure. The player can disagree, ask for clarification, or leave. Do not imply they chose an option absent from current data.

## 11. Human consequences without scorekeeping

Consequences appear in who returns, which wording changes, which task continues, and whether a boundary is respected. Do not invent a morale bonus, relationship meter, resource fee, or reputation effect. Mechanical consequences would require a separate evidence-based design decision.

## 12. Proposed content bank

The inserts below are candidate game text, not a promise that every entry will ship. A production pass should select, sequence, and shorten them to fit the current encounter and character-state owners. Each candidate keeps a particular voice, an observable action, and a branch-sensitive aftermath. Remove any insert that conflicts with the live state or duplicates an existing authored line.
