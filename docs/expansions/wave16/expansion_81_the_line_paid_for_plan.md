# EXPANSION 81 — THE LINE PAID FOR

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Veda Ro’s posted trap circuit, the standing fee for the maimed, and the boundary she sets around the fog month.

### Wave 16: Marks That Let Others Pass

## 1. Creative promise

A posted line is a promise that others must be able to inspect before they trust the ground. Veda’s mistake is not a puzzle to solve, and her boundary is not an invitation disguised as a test. The player may press the fog question only through the existing choice, but the prose does not make pressure a virtue. She sets a price, marks a route, and changes the terms after harm without pretending harm can be refunded. This is an authored content pack, not a feature roadmap. It supplies proposed playable prose, dialogue, field notes, and repeat-visit aftermath around current ASHFALL data. It does not claim any new line or scene is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

Veda Ro’s posted trap circuit, the standing fee for the maimed, and the boundary she sets around the fog month. The player learns that a mark earns trust only when its limits remain visible to the person who has to use it.

## 3. Verified local anchor

Veda’s character record places her at Survey Launch Kittiwake in the drowned region. The aground launch has an intact sonar rig and a logbook that continues after the Exchange, recording water, silt, and position. Veda is a trapper who runs a marked circuit with posted lines and a standing fee for the maimed. Her record says one line took a traveler in fog month; she paid the blood price unprompted and has not fogged the line since. Her quest offers buying tag paint and wire for the marked paths or pressing the fog question. The late arc distinguishes a guide whose fees enter a shared fund from a cold circuit whose routes are for strangers. The current narrative encounter has no fixed location restriction in its record. The character’s authored location and quest chain are the narrative anchor; an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or new map connection.

## 4. New prose and fixed canon

The current character, location, quest, encounter, and arc facts above are fixed. Each new visitor, phrase, work slip, journal fragment, staging detail, and repeat visit below is a proposal. Recheck each proposal against current data before implementation. This plan changes no resource yield, faction ownership, route access, character death, or quest choice ID.

## 5. The emotional center

The launch rests at an angle on a roof beneath the water. Paint survives on the posts because Veda tests every mark after rain. The sonar rig is mounted but silent, an ordinary machine switched off. Veda counts the player onto the path and points to the fee board. It states what is owed if a line injures someone, what is paid, and who may refuse the path. She asks the player to read the last line before crossing. This small scene is the scale for the pack. The player can help, misunderstand, ask, or leave; the story does not confuse closeness with permission. The anchor character remains a worker with a life outside the player’s visit.

## 6. Dialogue sample

Proposed exchange: The player says Veda told them the fee stands. Veda says it does. Asked whether the fee is paid to her or the injured person, she says she asks what the injured person wants the payment to do. Asked about fog month, she looks at the painted post and says it is not a route marker. The player says they can ask. Veda replies: You can. I can answer or not. Then she points out that the route stays here and her answer is not the price of passage. Player responses may be shorter or silent. The scene still works if the visitor says nothing. A pause does not imply that a hidden option waits behind it.

## 7. The current character arc

Introduce the marked path at the launch and let the player read the rules before walking. A visitor challenges the standing fee or asks to pass an unmarked area. Resolve the current quest choice. Follow either the pooled fee and guide route or the colder route after the question is pressed. No trap placement mini-game, injury counter, or map system is proposed. Keep first, evolved, and late states distinct. Do not show a late-state outcome before its required choice or quest completion. Do not create another hidden ending. If a player has not met the character, do not imply they have been waiting specifically for the player.

## 8. Point of view and scene grammar

The narration stays close to what the visitor can verify: the sound a part makes, where a line is marked, what remains on a page after correction, who chooses to wait, and who has begun the next task. It does not report another person’s private thoughts as fact. If a proposal gives someone a feeling, that feeling belongs in permitted speech or a personal record.

## 9. Cast and voice

Veda speaks directly about terrain, fees, and the work she accepts. The injured traveler has their own preferences about payment and disclosure; they are not a prop for Veda’s guilt. Other trappers may disagree about the fund’s reach. The Survey Launch remains a work site, not an oracle or a substitute speaker for Veda.
## 10. Choice discipline

The current quest choices are the only major branches in this plan. Small responses may change the next line or which insert appears, but do not introduce a second hidden branch structure. The player can disagree, ask for clarification, or leave. Do not imply they chose an option absent from current data.

## 11. Human consequences without scorekeeping

Consequences appear in who returns, which wording changes, which task continues, and whether a boundary is respected. Do not invent a morale bonus, relationship meter, resource fee, or reputation effect. Mechanical consequences would require a separate evidence-based design decision.

## 12. Proposed content bank

The inserts below are candidate game text, not a promise that every entry will ship. A production pass should select, sequence, and shorten them to fit the current encounter and character-state owners. Each candidate keeps a particular voice, an observable action, and a branch-sensitive aftermath. Remove any insert that conflicts with the live state or duplicates an existing authored line.
