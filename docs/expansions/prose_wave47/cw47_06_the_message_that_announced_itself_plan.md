## 1. Expansion thesis

Dead Man’s Loop is a three-day stale broadcast whose message announces itself as automated. It repeats word for word, down to the hiss between phrases, and finally explains that the operator set it up and walked away or was carried; the coordinates point to a clinic burned down weeks earlier. This bank treats repetition as both care and absence. The loop can continue a request after its author is unreachable, but it cannot tell the listener whether the operator survived, whether help was ever possible, or what waits in the burned clinic’s place.

## 2. Story question

When an automated distress message repeats that its operator cannot answer and finally points to a burned clinic, what does repetition preserve after the human voice is gone?
