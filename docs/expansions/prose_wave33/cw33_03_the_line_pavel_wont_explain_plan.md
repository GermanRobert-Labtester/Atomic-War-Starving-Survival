## 1. Expansion thesis

The Eastern Road has frost heave, abandoned vehicles and thinner ash than the low ground. Pavel Eren walks roads twice and sells map copies, but refuses to sell unwalked routes or explain one eastern-road line. Scout Lead Dara K.’s Year 2 report records a turnback at a collapsed overpass. A separate evolution event describes bridge debris and severed direct travel. This is about the ethics of a correction line, not a route guide. The bank below is intended to produce playable narrative texture: a player can discover, compare, question, refuse, or return to a passage while the existing game systems continue to own state. The fragments are written as content, not as a feature roadmap.

## 2. Story question

When a map is honest about what its maker will not say, is that enough?

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

Pavel distinguishes walked routes from one learned “the polite way” and will not explain. Preserve the silence. Dara’s report and bridge event have separate dates and authority. Broad passability does not override active blockage. Existing characters retain their authored identity, boundaries, and outcomes. New working voices remain editorial until an existing content owner approves them. Never fill a source gap solely to make the scene resolve.

## 5. Human center

A buyer wants a map to promise certainty; Pavel sells bounded knowledge. His refusal is not a puzzle. Dara’s report is a team decision, not a confession from absent people. Abandoned vehicles do not assign fates. The emotional pressure should come through what a person records, omits, repairs, asks, or leaves unsigned rather than a narrator naming what the location means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Pavel expects maps to be checked and does not tell an origin story. Dara’s report stays concise. Later readers compare dates and claims without impersonating witnesses. Keep each author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know what an unnamed visitor thought; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing established facts.

## 13. Alternative forms and editorial rubric

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not four required encounters. Scene drafts stage an observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical point of friction; consequence vignettes allow a later reader to recognize changed context. Select only forms that fit an existing content owner.

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep objects specific to this location. Let a line do practical work before it carries a theme. Remove exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and wording that could be mistaken for a new mechanic. Where existing game state matters, state it in the source owner’s terms and do not duplicate its authority.
