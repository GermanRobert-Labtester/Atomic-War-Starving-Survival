# EXPANSION 88 — A Floor Divided in Daylight

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Rika Dorn’s single offer to split a find, the injury that leaves her camp-bound, and the registry she chooses to keep or place in trust.

### Wave 18: Names Beside the Work

## 1. Creative promise

Rika’s skill is practical knowledge carried in a working life, not a license for the player to extract addresses. The story treats the split, the injury, and the registry as choices with owners. Its tenderness is concrete: who counts aloud, who stands watch, and whether a useful record stays with the person who can consent to it. This is a prose-first game-content expansion, not a feature roadmap. It offers proposed playable scenes, dialogue, records, and repeat-visit aftermath that can deepen the existing character arc. It does not claim any candidate text is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

Rika offers one clean split; what follows tests whether the player can distinguish a fair count from possession of the places and people that made the count possible.

## 3. Verified local anchor

Rika Dorn is a cache scavenger based at the Scavenger Guild’s shifting camp, marked by a stripped vehicle axle. Her three quests are `quest_arc_rika_01_cache`, `quest_arc_rika_02_trap`, and `quest_arc_rika_03_guild`. The first offers `rika_split` or `rika_take`; the second offers `rika_rescue` or `rika_her_registry`; the late quest offers `rika_book_routes`. Current prose establishes that a false cellar-floor rumor takes her ankle, that she becomes camp-bound, and that the registry contains addresses. The late state makes her a guild broker with first refusal and a public price. The encounter rows have no fixed location ID. The current encounter record has no fixed `requiredLocationId`; the authored character location and quest chain are the narrative anchor, while an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or a new map connection.

## 4. Canon boundary and content status

The first split happens aloud and on site by Rika’s count. If the player takes the whole cache, the existing outcome says she enters the player’s name in the guild registry as a fact, not an enemy. The evolved camp-bound ankle state is gated by `rika_split`; do not stage it on the `rika_take` rival branch. The second choice has a sharp recorded cost: either carry her out and stand camp watch while the ankle recovers, or trade the medic’s visit for registry pages; that latter outcome says the ankle heals crooked. The late guild-ally state requires `rika_rescue`, and the rescue branch leads to Rika dictating the registry by the third day. Do not soften, reverse, or sensationalize these facts. Existing location descriptions, character facts, quest prerequisites, choice IDs, and recorded outcomes remain authoritative. Every scene, visitor, object, note, follow-up, and line below is a proposal for review. Do not turn a suggested staging detail into a claim that a current route, event, schedule, or gameplay effect exists.

## 5. Emotional center

Rika can be loud about pain and still own the next decision. She directs traffic from the crate, rejects a pitying tone, and can decide who hears the registry. The player’s care matters because it does not turn her into a reward, map key, or debt that may be collected at will. The plan does not require the anchor character to disclose private history, become a companion, or resolve an unresolved absence. Care is expressed through practical action and respect for another person’s stated limit.

## 6. Point of view, voice, and cast

Rika is quick, exact about counts, and sparing with explanations. She knows the difference between a real cellar floor and a story about one. Her humor can cut through a negotiation, but she never becomes a puzzle dispenser. Camp members have their own standards about work, access, medical care, and what a guild record should disclose. Narration stays close to things the player could observe: a page turned over, a task interrupted, an answer given, a person choosing not to continue. Private thoughts belong only in a character’s own permitted record or spoken words. Secondary characters have distinct interests and are not props for the anchor’s arc.

## 7. Existing arc and choice discipline

Begin at the cache and let the split be the one unusual offer it is. Follow `rika_split` or `rika_take` without substituting a new branch. Stage the ankle and registry only when the live character state is the split-gated injured state and the second quest is eligible; never carry that scene into the rival outcome. The rescue and page-trade aftermaths remain distinct, and the guild-ally brokerage state follows only after `rika_rescue` and its prerequisite. Do not let first refusal or booked routes leak into the opening scene. Use only these current choices as the major branch structure: `rika_split`, `rika_take`, `rika_rescue`, `rika_her_registry`, and `rika_book_routes`. Minor line variants can reflect the resolved state, but do not invent a new quest, choice ID, resource cost, relationship value, ending, route, or persistent flag.

## 8. Proposed scene grammar

Each candidate insert below is conditional and modular. A content owner should choose a coherent subset, sequence it against the live quest state, and remove anything that repeats current text or exceeds the character’s knowledge. Scene order follows the existing prerequisites. A later-state insert cannot appear early merely because its prose reads well. The player can decline an exchange or leave; this does not unlock a secret response.

## 9. Human consequences without scorekeeping

A clean count does not make the cellar safe or grant the player its address. Taking the cache changes how Rika records the player’s name. Rescue is time, care, and watch; the registry work is something she decides to dictate. Trading for pages remains an exploitative choice with the recorded injury outcome, not a clever shortcut framed as equal exchange. Consequences appear in labor, access, wording, whether a person returns, and what a record does or does not say. No numeric reward or punishment is implied. Any mechanical effect would require separate authority and a current API review.

## 10. Location and safety discipline

Do not publish cellar addresses, route coordinates, entry points, floor-by-floor directions, or improvised rescue procedure. The location is a shifting guild camp; it is not a guaranteed map node for every appearance. Injury prose must not treat disability as a joke, punishment, or proof of character. Keep medical choices in the scene and avoid invented diagnoses. The prose must not teach a reader to enter a hazardous structure, handle energized equipment, cross an unverified route, evade a search, or expose private names. Safety language should remain human and contextual, not a generic warning panel pasted into dialogue.

## 11. Text standards and implementation notes

Keep lines readable in short dialogue boxes, preserve the current choice labels when quoting them, and avoid giving optional prose the appearance of a new objective. Proposed records need clear authorship and permission to be read. Repeat-visit text must be gated by current state rather than wall-clock assumptions. This plan modifies no production JSON, code, save data, encounter registration, map, or master catalog.

## 12. Content bank: proposed playable prose

The following material is a bank of candidate inserts, not a checklist of mandatory scenes. Each section contains a scene, a diegetic record, dialogue, and an aftermath variant around one distinct beat. These are deliberately separable: a final writing pass may select one form and leave the others in the archive. The condition in each item controls eligibility; current source state always wins.
