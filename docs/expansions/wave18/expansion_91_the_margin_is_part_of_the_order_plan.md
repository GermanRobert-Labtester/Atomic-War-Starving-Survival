# EXPANSION 91 — The Margin Is Part of the Order

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Marek Voln’s visible checkpoint log, the dated objections that make him a subject of procedure, and the warning that arrives before a sweep.

### Wave 18: Names Beside the Work

## 1. Creative promise

Marek is a corporal who keeps the book where petitioners can read it. His choices are not between an honest man and a corrupt machine; they are between different uses of procedure, each with people watching. The prose makes a written objection concrete while preserving the cost of the deserter branch and the limits of a warning. This is a prose-first game-content expansion, not a feature roadmap. It offers proposed playable scenes, dialogue, records, and repeat-visit aftermath that can deepen the existing character arc. It does not claim any candidate text is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

At Checkpoint Gamma, Marek records passage and disagreement in the same book; later, his own dated objections become the record that decides whether he remains at his post, escapes, or is made an example.

## 3. Verified local anchor

Marek Voln’s initial state places him at Checkpoint Gamma as a corporal who keeps posted terms and a public log. `quest_arc_marek_01_patrol` offers passage under the terms (`marek_cooperative`) or a loud written dispute (`marek_defiant`). The cooperative branch can lead to a deserter state and `quest_arc_marek_02_deserter`, where the player may provision Marek or report him. Provisioning can lead to the outside-ally state and `quest_arc_marek_03_watch`, which offers one choice to take the warning. The garrison checkpoint description includes monthly conscription lists, a Harven-signed bulletin, and an older VOSS sign. Existing expansion 06 develops Harven/Voss-era accounts at this location; this plan does not revise or adjudicate them. The current encounter record has no fixed `requiredLocationId`; the authored character location and quest chain are the narrative anchor, while an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or a new map connection.

## 4. Canon boundary and content status

Marek’s initial log records the player’s conduct and his margin answer. The deserter synopsis says he is sheltering where sweeps do not look first and has sent his field kit for safekeeping. Provisioning him leaves the kit and objections; reports from him later cover routes, sweeps, and command mood. Turning him in pays what procedure promised, posts the case where required, and makes passes arrive early enough for the queue to notice. His hardened state keeps him at the checkpoint with undated objections. The late warning state is conditional on the cooperative-then-provision path. Preserve all outcomes and keep them distinct. Existing location descriptions, character facts, quest prerequisites, choice IDs, and recorded outcomes remain authoritative. Every scene, visitor, object, note, follow-up, and line below is a proposal for review. Do not turn a suggested staging detail into a claim that a current route, event, schedule, or gameplay effect exists.

## 5. Emotional center

A record can make disagreement possible and also make it traceable. Marek offers no favor for compliance and no extra passage for eloquence. The player’s written protest receives an answer in the margin; the later choice about his field kit is not an abstract vote about institutions but an action involving a person who trusted the shelter with his belongings. The plan does not require the anchor character to disclose private history, become a companion, or resolve an unresolved absence. Care is expressed through practical action and respect for another person’s stated limit.

## 6. Point of view, voice, and cast

Marek answers with exact terms, dates, and the line of procedure that applies. His courtesy can feel severe because he does not make exceptions to soften a difficult answer. A petitioner can be angry or afraid without becoming a generic crowd. The log is written by a person, read by a queue, and changed by each new entry. Narration stays close to things the player could observe: a page turned over, a task interrupted, an answer given, a person choosing not to continue. Private thoughts belong only in a character’s own permitted record or spoken words. Secondary characters have distinct interests and are not props for the anchor’s arc.

## 7. Existing arc and choice discipline

Stage only the current choices in order: honor or dispute at the post; on the eligible deserter branch, provision or report; after the provision path, take the warning. Other current states—hardened picket, garrison example, recruited watch sergeant, outside ally, deceased—must remain source-governed. Do not combine recruitment with the informant path or write a new outcome for the unresolved queue. Use only these current choices as the major branch structure: `marek_cooperative`, `marek_defiant`, `marek_provisions`, `marek_turn_in`, and `marek_take_warning`. Minor line variants can reflect the resolved state, but do not invent a new quest, choice ID, resource cost, relationship value, ending, route, or persistent flag.

## 8. Proposed scene grammar

Each candidate insert below is conditional and modular. A content owner should choose a coherent subset, sequence it against the live quest state, and remove anything that repeats current text or exceeds the character’s knowledge. Scene order follows the existing prerequisites. A later-state insert cannot appear early merely because its prose reads well. The player can decline an exchange or leave; this does not unlock a secret response.

## 9. Human consequences without scorekeeping

A neat log entry is not a friendship. A written objection can be answered without changing the order. Provisioning Marek brings later warnings but does not erase the cost of being hunted; reporting him gets exactly what procedure promised and changes how the queue reads the shelter. The writing records those consequences without telling the player which one to feel proud of. Consequences appear in labor, access, wording, whether a person returns, and what a record does or does not say. No numeric reward or punishment is implied. Any mechanical effect would require separate authority and a current API review.

## 10. Location and safety discipline

Checkpoint Gamma is an active garrison post in the current description and carries radiation exposure. Do not provide an evasion route, sweep schedule, weak point, signal code, or tactical method. The warning state may establish that information arrived before a sweep while leaving operational specifics outside this plan. Do not connect Marek’s story to the older Voss sign or expansion 06’s contradictory accounts. The prose must not teach a reader to enter a hazardous structure, handle energized equipment, cross an unverified route, evade a search, or expose private names. Safety language should remain human and contextual, not a generic warning panel pasted into dialogue.

## 11. Text standards and implementation notes

Keep lines readable in short dialogue boxes, preserve the current choice labels when quoting them, and avoid giving optional prose the appearance of a new objective. Proposed records need clear authorship and permission to be read. Repeat-visit text must be gated by current state rather than wall-clock assumptions. This plan modifies no production JSON, code, save data, encounter registration, map, or master catalog.

Each section contains a scene, a diegetic record, dialogue, and an aftermath variant around one distinct beat. These are deliberately separable: a final writing pass may select one form and leave the others in the archive. The condition in each item controls eligibility; current source state always wins.
