# EXPANSION 90 — The Copy Costs Less Than the Question

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Pavel Eren’s walked routes, the eastern-road entry he will not explain, and the map copies that outlast his visits.

### Wave 18: Names Beside the Work

## 1. Creative promise

A map can be useful, accurate, and morally incomplete at once. Pavel sells distances he has walked and corrects his own copy in front of the player. The story keeps the one unexplained eastern-road entry attached to the people who did not return to correct it, without turning their absence into a clue that the player is owed. This is a prose-first game-content expansion, not a feature roadmap. It offers proposed playable scenes, dialogue, records, and repeat-visit aftermath that can deepen the existing character arc. It does not claim any candidate text is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

Pavel Eren sells the map he has walked, but the most useful line is the one whose history he will explain only once and never turn into a public route.

## 3. Verified local anchor

Pavel Eren is a Route Scavenger in the toll country. His character record says he is walking every road twice, once to learn it and once to trust it, and selling copies as he goes. He offers route copies, ford and collapse notes, and distances that hold up. The bio says a group he was following on the eastern road never came back to correct the safe-ford entry; Pavel will not say how he learned it was safe. `quest_arc_pavel_01_map` lets the player buy the map and ask nothing, or buy nothing and ask how he learned the road. The second outcome says he tells the player exactly once that he was following, then goes to walk it again “the polite way.” The late states distinguish the trusted broker with eleven annotated copies from Pavel’s unresolved absence. The current encounter record has no fixed `requiredLocationId`; the authored character location and quest chain are the narrative anchor, while an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or a new map connection.

## 4. Canon boundary and content status

Do not name the absent group, tell the player what happened to them, or expose the ford’s actual location, crossing instructions, or route. The choice to buy the copy is also a choice not to ask for the story; respect that separation. In the ask branch Pavel folds the map and speaks once. Later the trusted state says the shelter holds one of eleven annotated copies and that the eastern entry has never needed correction; the missing state says Pavel has not returned by his own schedules and his copy has not surfaced. Neither state explains the group’s fate. Existing location descriptions, character facts, quest prerequisites, choice IDs, and recorded outcomes remain authoritative. Every scene, visitor, object, note, follow-up, and line below is a proposal for review. Do not turn a suggested staging detail into a claim that a current route, event, schedule, or gameplay effect exists.

## 5. Emotional center

Pavel’s map is work, not a confession. A correction can make a copy better without making its owner public property. He has a responsibility to walk the route again in the polite way; that responsibility does not grant the player a right to follow, search, or decide what the absent group meant. The plan does not require the anchor character to disclose private history, become a companion, or resolve an unresolved absence. Care is expressed through practical action and respect for another person’s stated limit.

## 6. Point of view, voice, and cast

Pavel notices distances, worn folds, drying ink, and whether a mark was checked twice. He jokes about uncertainty as a way to keep it visible, not to make grief cute. His speech stays economical. The map reader can correct a road they actually walked, but that contribution does not turn them into an authority over people who are not present. Narration stays close to things the player could observe: a page turned over, a task interrupted, an answer given, a person choosing not to continue. Private thoughts belong only in a character’s own permitted record or spoken words. Secondary characters have distinct interests and are not props for the anchor’s arc.

## 7. Existing arc and choice discipline

The opening branch is exactly `pavel_buy_map` or `pavel_refuse_map`. The buy branch can support the evolved mapping season, in which shelter corrections are folded into his copies, and the trusted late state with annotated copies. The ask branch can support the single disclosure and eventual late-missing state. Never combine the two late outcomes or make absence proof of death. Use only these current choices as the major branch structure: `pavel_buy_map` and `pavel_refuse_map`. Minor line variants can reflect the resolved state, but do not invent a new quest, choice ID, resource cost, relationship value, ending, route, or persistent flag.

## 8. Proposed scene grammar

Each candidate insert below is conditional and modular. A content owner should choose a coherent subset, sequence it against the live quest state, and remove anything that repeats current text or exceeds the character’s knowledge. Scene order follows the existing prerequisites. A later-state insert cannot appear early merely because its prose reads well. The player can decline an exchange or leave; this does not unlock a secret response.

## 9. Human consequences without scorekeeping

A purchased copy may be useful without buying the story. A refusal of the copy followed by the question has a personal consequence; Pavel gives one answer and then chooses his next work. If he is later missing, the map copies continue circulating while his own copy does not surface. Do not add search objectives, map UI, waypoint tracking, route unlocks, or rewards. Consequences appear in labor, access, wording, whether a person returns, and what a record does or does not say. No numeric reward or punishment is implied. Any mechanical effect would require separate authority and a current API review.

## 10. Location and safety discipline

The eastern road has broken pavement, abandoned vehicles, and its own environmental risks. The source calls one ford safe, but the prose must not provide directions, coordinates, a crossing method, or a guarantee that current conditions match Pavel’s notes. His encounter has no fixed location restriction, and the toll-country map does not establish new travel routes. The group’s absence remains unresolved. The prose must not teach a reader to enter a hazardous structure, handle energized equipment, cross an unverified route, evade a search, or expose private names. Safety language should remain human and contextual, not a generic warning panel pasted into dialogue.

## 11. Text standards and implementation notes

Keep lines readable in short dialogue boxes, preserve the current choice labels when quoting them, and avoid giving optional prose the appearance of a new objective. Proposed records need clear authorship and permission to be read. Repeat-visit text must be gated by current state rather than wall-clock assumptions. This plan modifies no production JSON, code, save data, encounter registration, map, or master catalog.

Each section contains a scene, a diegetic record, dialogue, and an aftermath variant around one distinct beat. These are deliberately separable: a final writing pass may select one form and leave the others in the archive. The condition in each item controls eligibility; current source state always wins.
