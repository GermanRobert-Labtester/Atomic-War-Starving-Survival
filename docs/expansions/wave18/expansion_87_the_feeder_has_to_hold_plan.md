# EXPANSION 87 — The Feeder Has to Hold

## A prose-first game-content expansion for Anete Sarn’s grounded substation, the measured first feeder, and the regional schedule that depends on work nobody can skip.

### Wave 18: Names Beside the Work

## 1. Creative promise

At Substation Omega, a small light is meaningful only because the people responsible for it keep refusing unsafe shortcuts. Anete’s expertise is shown through preparation, refusal, fair accounting, and teachable standards rather than spectacle or technical exposition. This is a prose-first game-content expansion, not a feature roadmap. It offers proposed playable scenes, dialogue, records, and repeat-visit aftermath that can deepen the existing character arc. It does not claim any candidate text is already in the game.

## 2. One-sentence story

Anete Sarn keeps a dangerous yard responsibly grounded, asks for ordinary parts before promising power, and grows a regional grid at the pace that its workers can actually maintain.

## 3. Verified local anchor

Anete Sarn is a Grid Engineer associated with Substation Omega and the rebuilders. Her first quest, `quest_arc_anete_01_substation`, is titled “Live Grounds, No Copper.” It offers `anete_supply_parts`, `anete_rescue`, or `anete_pass`; later quests offer project backing or trained hands, then booking the shelter onto a schedule. The encounter `enc_arc_anete_substation` describes a swept compound and a reasonable parts list. The location record describes charged capacitors, an EMP hazard, 35 rads per hour in the yard, and a clear instruction to stay off the metal. It is a high-danger place, not a safe salvage tutorial. The current encounter record has no fixed `requiredLocationId`; the authored character location and quest chain are the narrative anchor, while an encounter row alone does not prove a guaranteed route or a new map connection.

## 4. Canon boundary and content status

Anete’s existing arc is careful, small, and affirmative about what is known: a sound compound, working grounds, no copper, a first feeder, later two waystation feeders, and a schedule. Her badges may be expired while her hands remain competent. Do not add a blackout disaster, accident, lost worker, heroic solo repair, or unrecorded electrical solution. The later project and schedule text should remain consistent with those two feeders and her existing standards. Existing location descriptions, character facts, quest prerequisites, choice IDs, and recorded outcomes remain authoritative. Every scene, visitor, object, note, follow-up, and line below is a proposal for review. Do not turn a suggested staging detail into a claim that a current route, event, schedule, or gameplay effect exists.

## 5. Emotional center

The first light does not come with a speech. It arrives on a schedule somebody wrote down, after the crew has signed what they checked and after Anete has told one trainee to go study before touching the next task. The emotional reward is that the work remains ordinary enough to be repeated tomorrow. The plan does not require the anchor character to disclose private history, become a companion, or resolve an unresolved absence. Care is expressed through practical action and respect for another person’s stated limit.

## 6. Point of view, voice, and cast

Anete speaks in bounded sentences. She distinguishes what was checked from what was assumed, and she does not decorate a refusal. She can be patient without turning into a lecturer. A trainee may be proud, embarrassed, irritated, or quiet, but their own words carry that response. The shelter’s people have schedules and concerns beyond this project. Narration stays close to things the player could observe: a page turned over, a task interrupted, an answer given, a person choosing not to continue. Private thoughts belong only in a character’s own permitted record or spoken words. Secondary characters have distinct interests and are not props for the anchor’s arc.

## 7. Existing arc and choice discipline

Start with the perimeter and parts list. Follow the exact first choice selected. If the project continues, show only the current project options: backing with surplus and reputation or lending trained shelter hands. The late schedule belongs after the existing project prerequisite and permits booking the shelter onto it. Do not conflate “offer shelter” with the supply-parts branch. Use only these current choices as the major branch structure: `anete_supply_parts`, `anete_rescue`, `anete_pass`, `anete_back_the_project`, `anete_string_conductor`, and `anete_book_switching`. Minor line variants can reflect the resolved state, but do not invent a new quest, choice ID, resource cost, relationship value, ending, route, or persistent flag.

## 8. Proposed scene grammar

Each candidate insert below is conditional and modular. A content owner should choose a coherent subset, sequence it against the live quest state, and remove anything that repeats current text or exceeds the character’s knowledge. Scene order follows the existing prerequisites. A later-state insert cannot appear early merely because its prose reads well. The player can decline an exchange or leave; this does not unlock a secret response.

## 9. Human consequences without scorekeeping

The crew waits through a pause; the learner returns with a study plan; a feeder stays unavailable until its owner has checked it; a schedule states what it can support. If the player passes, the compound stays secure for a while and lights continue to come and go on an unpublished schedule. Do not recast that branch as a punishment or claim the grid was permanently abandoned. Consequences appear in labor, access, wording, whether a person returns, and what a record does or does not say. No numeric reward or punishment is implied. Any mechanical effect would require separate authority and a current API review.

## 10. Location and safety discipline

Substation Omega’s yard is lethal-risk terrain with stored electrical energy and substantial radiation. Keep prose away from access instructions, rack identification, switching sequences, bypass methods, or scavenging technique. Anete offers a load calculation and switching orders as authored characterization; these plans do not reproduce them. A safe staging beat can show a closed boundary and a person choosing not to cross it. The prose must not teach a reader to enter a hazardous structure, handle energized equipment, cross an unverified route, evade a search, or expose private names. Safety language should remain human and contextual, not a generic warning panel pasted into dialogue.

## 11. Text standards and implementation notes

Keep lines readable in short dialogue boxes, preserve the current choice labels when quoting them, and avoid giving optional prose the appearance of a new objective. Proposed records need clear authorship and permission to be read. Repeat-visit text must be gated by current state rather than wall-clock assumptions. This plan modifies no production JSON, code, save data, encounter registration, map, or master catalog.

Each section contains a scene, a diegetic record, dialogue, and an aftermath variant around one distinct beat. These are deliberately separable: a final writing pass may select one form and leave the others in the archive. The condition in each item controls eligibility; current source state always wins.
