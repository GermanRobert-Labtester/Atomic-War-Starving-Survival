# EXPANSION 128 — The Stretcher Left Facing Out

## A place of unfinished exits, written without claiming who meant to return.

### Wave 25: What the Record Leaves

## Batch brief

**Content type:** prose-first location content plan with scene, record, conversation, and conditional revisit drafts.
**Quantity:** 18 story beats, four selectable forms per beat.
**Current location anchor:** `abandoned_hospital` — Abandoned Hospital.
**Tone:** material, restrained, observant, and careful about the source’s unknowns.

**Scope:** editorial text only; no production code, JSON, route, quest, encounter, flag, system, or save change.

## 1. Expansion thesis

The Abandoned Hospital description gives its most intimate image at the exit: a stretcher with one wheel, propped as if someone meant to come back for it. The plan follows that suspended intention without inventing a patient, rescuer, or final journey. It also keeps two other records in their own lanes: `exp_report_abandoned_hospital_01`, the veteran-led field report, and the stale automated distress signal `freq_distress_367_9`. Their differences and limits are part of the prose. No fragment turns the hospital into a medical tutorial or a route guide. The player-facing material should make the location feel inhabited by the act of observing, copying, disagreeing, and deciding what a sentence can support. The prose does not need a new system to create consequence: it can change the reader’s interpretation of a record while leaving the world state untouched.

## 2. Story question

How can a story honor an unfinished act without inventing the person who began it?

## 3. Verified local anchor

`abandoned_hospital` — Abandoned Hospital; danger 6, travel 2.0 hours, base radiation 35 rads/hour. `locations.json` describes the collapsed east wing, buried pharmacy door, charged radiology basement, open morgue drawers, and a one-wheeled stretcher propped against the exit. Linked but separate records: `exp_report_abandoned_hospital_01` (the_veteran, day 42) and `freq_distress_367_9` (“Dead Man’s Loop,” an automated stale signal that reveals this location).

## 4. Fixed canon and open space

The location description and field report do not align on every site detail; this plan preserves their provenance instead of reconciling them. The report is one expedition account, not a current instruction. The radio signal identifies itself as automated and repeats; do not attach it to the stretcher or invent a missing operator, patient, or rescue branch. The location description and linked records cited in this plan are fixed anchors. Existing characters retain their authored identity and choices; any additional working role is editorial only.

## 5. Human center

The human center is an object left ready for someone who may not have returned. A reader can feel the pull of that unfinished intention while accepting that the source gives no owner. The other emotional register is repetition: an automated call can outlast the people who once expected a reply. The emotion should come through what a person chooses to write, omit, copy, or leave unsigned rather than through a narrator announcing what the site means.

## 6. Voice and point of view

Keep the prose close to objects and document purpose: one wheel, an exit, a page that records one team’s visit, and a signal that repeats without breath. Avoid gore, medical instructions, haunted-hospital cliches, and omniscient claims about the absent. Keep the author’s knowledge local. A field note cannot know the history of an unnamed visitor; a later reader cannot recover a date that was never recorded; a title cannot create a route. Vary sentence length and register by document purpose, not by changing the world’s facts.

## 7. Placement and current reachability

Existing anchors are `locations.json`, `exp_report_abandoned_hospital_01`, and the radio signal `freq_distress_367_9`. The existence of these records does not approve new encounter placement or prove every proposed callback is reachable. Keep each source condition distinct. All fragments are optional editorial drafts. None is evidence that the location is currently player-reachable. If an editor later chooses any text, first verify the map/location owner, current content schema, actual consumer, and the source condition under which it can be shown.

## 8. Player agency

The player may read the description, compare a single field report with a location entry, or listen to the existing staged signal. No new hospital choice, patient rescue, treatment action, room route, or scavenging objective is proposed. Let the player decide whether to read, ask one practical question, correct a transcription, or move on. Do not create a moral-choice menu merely to make quiet prose feel interactive. The people who work at the site retain control over their words and papers.

## 9. Continuity, dignity, and safety

Do not offer triage, dosage, exposure, structural assessment, or entry guidance. Do not identify the stretcher’s owner or resolve the signal as a living caller. Avoid transferring field-report details into the location entry where the records differ. Any newly introduced working role remains anonymous and editorial. Existing named characters keep their source identity and boundaries. Unknowns remain unknown; no absent person is given a biography to heighten emotion. Keep technical context descriptive and non-instructional.

## 10. Existing hooks and implementation boundary

**Existing content anchors:** the current `locations.json` record and the linked catalogs named in section 3. **Unverified:** any player-facing route, encounter, text consumer, state condition, or return trigger beyond what those records explicitly establish. **Classification:** editorial content proposal; no implementation category is claimed until an owner and consumer are verified.

This plan changes no code or game data. Do not add a parallel discovery registry or a new route solely to host these drafts. The plan is review material, not authorization to create a new gameplay authority.

## 11. Variation and selection

The four forms under each beat are alternatives, not a sequence of four required encounters. Scene drafts stage the observation; record drafts give it a plausible author and audience; conversation fragments expose a practical disagreement; revisit vignettes let a later reader recognize changed wording. Select only the forms that fit an existing content owner. Shorten freely while retaining the source facts and each fragment’s boundary.

## 12. Editorial rubric

A selected fragment should answer who made it, why they made it, who might read it, and what the author cannot know. Keep the physical object specific enough to belong to this location. The line should do practical work before it carries theme. Avoid exposition that a worker would not say aloud, repeated catastrophe language, unearned revelation, and duplicated record wording.
