# ASHFALL — Environmental Storytelling QA & Micro-Location Review
## Tasks F13–F16 Deliverable: Narrative Quality & Observation Discipline

---

## 1. Overview & Narrative Principles

In ASHFALL, micro-locations encountered along expedition routes are windows into the material culture, tragedy, and survival efforts of the post-exchange world. To maintain immersion and consistency across the 25 route encounters (20 Discovery, 3 Hazard, 2 Social), all descriptions adhere to an 8-dimension environmental storytelling standard.

### The 8-Dimension Rubric
1. **Observability (Physical Evidence Only):** Micro-location descriptions are restricted to what a scavenger standing on the frozen ground can perceive with human senses. Disallowed: omniscient authorial assertions (e.g. "who died here", "they fled in panic", "what must have happened"), moral judgments ("heroic", "tragic", "desperate"), and unobservable inner psychological states.
2. **Concrete Nouns & Physical Anchors:** Every entry is anchored in concrete materials: canvas, tarnished brass, vitrified glass, tallow, blistered paint, rubber weatherstripping, rusted rebar. Abstract nouns ("despair", "tragedy", "loss") are avoided.
3. **One Dominant Image:** Each discovery entry presents one clear, focal visual anchor rather than a cluttered catalog of post-apocalyptic tropes.
4. **Conciseness (≤ 3 Sentences):** Every entry is strictly 1 to 3 sentences, targeting 20–55 words for readability during rapid expedition turns.
5. **Tone Restraint (Emotional Restraint):** Bleakness emerges from factual arrangement of physical remains rather than sensationalist phrasing ("horrifying", "terrifyingly hollow", "grisly").
6. **Death-Image Diversity:** Avoids relying on mummified corpses, skeletons, or blood splatters as the primary shorthand for danger or tragedy. Uses abandoned tools, interrupted routines, faded notices, and physical erosion instead.
7. **Setting Consistency:** Grounds all details in ASHFALL's nuclear post-exchange timeline and cold-war survival technology.
8. **Mechanical-Language Exclusion:** Zero game terms (no stat mentions, dice rolls, hit points, or weight units).

## 3. Before / After Comparison Samples

### Example 1: `micro_ruined_greenhouse`
- **Before:** *"Shattered glass crunches underfoot in this ruined greenhouse. Desiccated seed trays lie beneath the rusted benches. In the corner, a heavy steel cabinet remains sealed—someone tried to pry it open with a crowbar, but gave up or died trying."*
- **Flaws:** "gave up or died trying" is authorial speculation; "crunches underfoot" breaks third-person observational frame.
- **After:** *"Shattered panes lie across rows of metal nursery benches. Desiccated seed flats sit under collapsed irrigation pipe. In the back corner, a steel storage locker bears heavy crowbar gouges around its bent hasp."*
- **Improvement:** Every detail is an observable physical object. The tool marks tell the story of the failed attempt without telling the reader what happened to the person.

### Example 2: `micro_field_kitchen`
- **Before:** *"A massive cast-iron cooking pot is frozen solid to its metal stand. Bent ladles hang from rusted wire hooks. Hundreds of desperate tally marks are gouged into the serving table, tracking rations that eventually ran out."*
- **Flaws:** "desperate" is an emotional label; "tracking rations that eventually ran out" is omniscient historical narrative.
- **After:** *"A cast-iron cauldron is frozen to its burner ring beside a row of dented aluminum ladles. Dozens of five-bar tally marks are cut into the zinc table surface with a knife. Stacks of stamped ration receipts lie rotted into pulp under the bench."*
- **Improvement:** Grounded zinc table, five-bar tallies, and pulped receipts provide concrete material evidence of rationing.

### Example 3: `micro_abandoned_barricade`
- **Before:** *"Traffic arrows were repainted twice in different faction colors, marking shifting territories. A mummified corpse lies slumped behind the barrier, clutching a rusted lockbox. The sandbags have frozen into a solid, bullet-pocked wall."*
- **Flaws:** "marking shifting territories" is authorial analysis; "mummified corpse clutching a rusted lockbox" leans on pulp death clichés.
- **After:** *"Chevron signs are repainted in faded green over older red bands on the concrete barrier. Frozen sandbags form a low wall topped with a coiled strand of concertina wire. An empty sentry stool and an open wooden ammo crate sit in the lee."*
- **Improvement:** Diversifies death imagery away from corpses. The sentry stool and spent ammo crate imply abandonment with restraint.

### Example 4: `micro_water_source`
- **Before (4 sentences):** *"A dented tin cup is bound to the rusted hand pump with frayed wire. Heavy, unnatural mineral staining marks the concrete basin. Someone wrote 'SAFE' in charcoal, alongside a date from five years ago. The pump handle still groans when moved."*
- **Flaws:** 4 sentences (violates the ≤3 sentence hard ceiling); authorial interpretation ("unnatural mineral staining", "Someone wrote").
- **After (3 sentences):** *"A dented tin cup is wired to the spout of a cast-iron hand pump over a cracked catch basin. Streaks of reddish-brown mineral crust coat the lip beneath the drain. The word 'SAFE' is scrawled in charcoal on the pump housing above a faded date."*
- **Improvement:** Complies with sentence limit; specific mineral coloration and observable physical state.

## 4. Death-Image Diversity Audit

Across the 25 route micro-locations:
- **Direct Human Remains:** Restricted to exactly 2 entries where thematically necessary (`micro_improvised_grave` — mound with stones; `micro_dead_livestock` — animal remains in lime trench).
- **Substituted Physical Traces:** The remaining 23 entries use abandoned equipment, personal effects (child's shoe, crayon drawing, wedding ring, carved wooden bird), tool scars, chalk/pencil markings, and environmental weathering.
- **Result:** Avoids "skeleton fatigue" and gives poignant emotional weight when human remains or graves are encountered.
