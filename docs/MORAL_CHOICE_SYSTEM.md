# ASHFALL: ATOMIC WAR - MORAL CHOICE SYSTEM
## "The Weight of Survival"

*60 Quests, 8 Branching Paths, World Impact Without UI Clutter*

---

## 📋 SYSTEM OVERVIEW

### **Core Philosophy:**
"No single playstyle is correct. Your choices shape the world, but the wasteland adapts to all types of survivors."

### **Key Features:**
✅ **60 Moral Choice Quests** (12 per type: Sharing Supplies, Listening to Stories, Offering Comfort, Respecting the Dead, Trusting Strangers)
✅ **8 Branching Paths** (Very Evil, Evil, Slightly Evil, Neutral, Slightly Positive, Positive, Very Positive, Empathetic)
✅ **World Impact System** (Positive/Negative effects on factions, NPCs, and locations)
✅ **Journal Integration** (Completed quests show arrows: 🔺 Green = Positive, 🔻 Red = Negative)
✅ **Location Discovery** ("Completed" status with impact indicators)
✅ **No Active UI Tracking** (No quest log clutter, only journal entries)
✅ **8 Different Endings** (Based on moral accumulation)
✅ **Faction Reputation System** (Good/Bad factions react differently)
✅ **Survivor Memory System** (NPCs remember your choices)

## 📊 MORAL SCORE TRACKING

### **How It Works:**
- **No UI Tracking** - Moral score is invisible to player
- **Journal Integration** - Completed quests show arrows (🔺/🔻)
- **Location Discovery** - Shows "Completed" with impact indicator
- **World Impact** - Affects faction reactions, NPC dialogue, event triggers
- **Ending Determination** - Based on final moral score

### **Moral Score Calculation:**
```
Each quest has a base moral value:
- Very Evil: -10 to -20 per choice
- Evil: -5 to -10 per choice
- Slightly Evil: -1 to -4 per choice
- Neutral: 0 per choice
- Slightly Positive: +1 to +4 per choice
- Positive: +5 to +10 per choice
- Very Positive: +10 to +20 per choice
- Empathetic: Variable (depends on listening, not moral judgment)

Total Moral Score = Sum of all quest moral values
```

### **Moral Score Ranges:**
- **Very Evil:** -100 to -200
- **Evil:** -50 to -99
- **Slightly Evil:** -1 to -49
- **Neutral:** 0
- **Slightly Positive:** +1 to +49
- **Positive:** +50 to +99
- **Very Positive:** +100 to +200
- **Empathetic:** Variable (story-focused)

## 🌍 THE WORLD IMPACT SYSTEM
*"The wasteland watches. Not all at once, and never right away."*

### Design Goal
The moral score is **never shown**. No meter, no karma bar, no notification. The world itself is the only feedback the player gets: prices change, doors open or close, dogs bark or don't. The player learns their own shape the way people do — from how the world answers them.

### The Five Impact Channels
| Channel | What shifts | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Faction stance** | Raiders / Peacekeepers / Knowledge Keepers move on their reaction ladder | At path-band crossings |
| **Encounter tables** | Weights shift toward hostile or helpful encounters | Daily, per band |
| **Dialogue banks** | NPCs select kind / cruel / neutral greeting and vendor lines | On interaction, per band |
| **Location tags** | Locations where acts occurred gain reputation tags | On quest resolution |
| **Threshold events** | Bounties, shelter raids, gift caravans, rescue offers | At fixed score thresholds |

### Propagation Delay — Word Travels
Acts do not instantly rewrite the world. Effects spread at the speed of gossip:

| Act witnessed... | Effect arrives... |
|---|---|
| Directly (NPC present) | Immediately, locally |
| In a settlement | Next caravan departure, ±1 day |
| In the wild, no witnesses | Only if the affected NPC survives to tell it |

Deterministic rule: propagation delays are drawn from `ISeededRng` at resolution time and stored in the memory record, so saves replay identically.

### Location Reputation Tags
- `tag_remembered_kind` — vendors round down, children wave, watch rotations warn you early
- `tag_remembered_cruel` — doors close, prices climb, people cross the street
- `tag_remembered_fair` — nothing changes; the dead world stays dead

Tags attach to the **location** where the act resolved, not to the player globally. A player can be a saint in one county and a ghost story in the next — until gossip closes the gap.

## 🤝 THE FACTION REPUTATION SYSTEM

### The Three Archetype Factions
The moral system plays factions against each other along a moral axis:

- **Raiders** — respect strength and ruthlessness; never sentimental
- **Peacekeepers** — protect roads and settlements; keep the bounty books
- **Knowledge Keepers** — archivists and radio operators; keep records of everyone

### The Reaction Ladder
Every faction tracks the player on a six-step ladder:
`Hunt → Hostile → Wary → Tolerate → Welcome → Protect`

### Path × Faction Matrix
| Player Path | Raiders | Peacekeepers | Knowledge Keepers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Very Evil | Welcome | **Hunt** (standing bounty) | Erasure (struck from the record) |
| Evil | Tolerate | Wary | Wary |
| Slightly Evil | Tolerate | Wary | Tolerate |
| Neutral | Wary | Tolerate | Tolerate |
| Slightly Positive | Hostile | Welcome | Welcome |
| Positive | **Hunt** (contract taken) | Protect | Protect |
| Very Positive | **Hunt** (open contract, raised twice) | Protect | Protect (written into the record) |
| Empathetic | Wary (you know too much) | Welcome | **Protect** (you *are* a record) |

### Rules
- Ladder position derives from the path band at the daily reconciliation tick — no separate faction grind.
- Extreme bands trigger one-time **threshold events**: a bounty notice nailed to a board, a contract taken on the player, a Peacekeeper patrol camping the shelter's approach to defend it.
- Raiders never "Protect." Their ceiling is doing business. The Knowledge Keepers' cruelest weapon is forgetting you.

## 🧠 THE SURVIVOR MEMORY SYSTEM

### What NPCs Remember
Every witnessed act writes a memory record:

```json
{
  "witness_id": "npc_moral_widow_hana",
  "act_id": "quest_moral_share_family",
  "choice_index": 1,
  "valence": "kind",
  "severity": 2,
  "day_witnessed": 34,
  "propagates_on_day": 41,
  "decayed": false
}
```

- **valence** — `kind` / `cruel` / `fair`
- **severity** — 1 (a cold shoulder) to 3 (they will tell this story for years)
- **propagates_on_day** — when the gossip leaves this NPC's circle

### How Memory Is Used
- **Greetings:** a kind memory selects the warm dialogue bank, cruel the shuttered one; children run toward or away.
- **Prices:** vendors apply a small modifier per remembered act, capped at ±15%.
- **Quest gating:** some later moral quests (e.g., The Mysterious Woman) only fire for players with at least one remembered kindness.
- **Gossip:** on `propagates_on_day`, the memory copies into connected settlements' NPC pools along caravan routes, dropping one severity step per hop.

### Decay
Memories fade one severity step every 40 days — except severity-3 memories ("the day the courier carried the packet north"), which never decay. The dead keep their reputations. So do you.

## 🌅 THE 8 ENDINGS (FULL EPILOGUES)

Endings fire at the campaign's close. Priority: **Empathetic threshold → moral band**. A player who meets the empathy threshold gets the Storykeeper ending regardless of band — listening outranks the ledger.

### 1. "The Warlord" — Very Evil (-100 to -200)
*You hold eleven miles of river and everyone in it. Trade moves because fear moves it. The overseers send tribute; the camps send runners, and the runners are caught. There is a chair in the old hotel that no one else sits in, and a fire no one else stands near, and at night it is very quiet, because quiet is what you ordered. Peace, of a kind. Priced in graves. When you go — and you will go, the dose ledger sees to that — there will be no one left to carry the story, and the river will belong to whoever is hardest next.*

### 2. "The Survivor King" — Evil (-50 to -99)
*You outlived everyone who trusted you, which is a kind of winning. The storeroom is full. The walls are good. The dogs know your step. People trade with you carefully and never turn their backs, and you tell yourself that caution is just respect with the serial numbers filed off. Some nights you set two cups out and put one away. The winter takes longer to get you than it takes most. That was the plan.*

### 3. "The Neutral Survivor" — Slightly Evil (-1 to -49)
*You took what you needed and a little more, and no one died who wouldn't have anyway — you're fairly sure of that. Small cruelties, nothing anyone carves on a wall. When caravans pass they don't wave and they don't aim. You are alive in a world where that puts you ahead of most, and some nights, counting cans by lamplight, you almost believe it.*

### 4. "The Balanced Survivor" — Neutral (0)
*The ledger closes at zero. Some owed you; you owed some. A woman at the waystation still saves you bread. A man at the ford still spits. You kept your hands clean enough to eat with, which in this country is its own arithmetic. When they argue about you after — and they will, briefly — no one can agree, and the argument dies like a fire in rain. You sleep. That was the prize.*

### 5. "The Community Builder" — Slightly Positive (+1 to +49)
*There's a garden behind wire now, and a stove that draws properly, and kids who were feral in spring are stealing apples by fall like it's the old world. None of it would survive a serious raider company, and everyone knows it, and they plant anyway. They plant because you kept showing up. Half the walls are your hands. You never asked for the chair they built you at the long table, but you sit in it, and you look tired, and nobody asks you to be anything else.*

### 6. "The Savior" — Positive (+50 to +99)
*Your name is a password at checkpoints you've never visited. Caravans reroute to pass your shelter because raiders broke a contract on you once and the story ended badly for them — for everyone. You die tired, mid-list, with the lantern still lit, and the mourning is loud and genuine and completely unmanageable. They rename the waystation after you, then argue for a generation about whether you'd have allowed it. You wouldn't have. That's why they do it.*

### 7. "The Saint of the Wasteland" — Very Positive (+100 to +200)
*You became a story while you were still alive. Pilgrims leave things at your door — filters, seeds, a child's drawing of a star — and you can no longer save everyone, so you finally stop trying and just witness them instead, one at a time, which turns out to be its own kind of saving. The Knowledge Keepers write you into the standing record in ink that's supposed to outlast the paper. When you go, the radio nets go quiet for a full minute, all of them, on purpose. The wasteland has no saints. It had you.*

### 8. "The Storykeeper" — Empathetic (threshold, any band)
*You didn't save the world; you wrote it down. You know the widow's husband's name and the deserter's hometown and the tune the old man hummed when the fire got low. North station keeps a room warm for the courier. Somewhere a child who once told you a story about stars is telling it to other children, and your name is in it. The Knowledge Keepers bring you paper when they can. The last library in the world is a person, and the person is tired, and the stories are safe.*

### Ending Priority & Lock Rules
- `empathy_points >= 45` AND `quests_resolved >= 25` → **Storykeeper** (overrides band)
- Otherwise the band decides; a strict tie, or fewer than 20 resolved quests, falls to the Neutral-family ending nearest zero.
- No path locks before 20 resolved quests — a handful of early choices cannot strand the ending.

## 📐 MORAL SCORE RULES & EDGE CASES

1. **Hard cap ±200.** Overflow past the cap converts into a one-time faction legend effect (a bonus threshold event), not more score.
2. **No decay, no forgiveness.** Choices are permanent for the save. (A redemption questline is future work; this system's spine is that the world doesn't forget.)
3. **One resolution per quest per save.** Re-encounters of the same quest id replay only the outcome already chosen.
4. **Minimum sample for ending lock:** 20 resolved quests.
5. **Band crossings** are evaluated at the daily reconciliation tick, not mid-scene, so an act's consequences always land overnight.
6. **Randomized outcome branches** (did the child steal, did the crew pass by) resolve via `ISeededRng` at resolution time; the roll is stored in the save.
7. **Empathy is a separate accumulator**, granted by the choice, not the quest type — see below.

## 💙 EMPATHETIC PATH MECHANICS

- **empathy_points** accrue on listening and comfort choices regardless of the choice's moral sign; some sharing and dead quests grant small amounts.
- **Listener** (≥15): NPCs volunteer one extra secret per conversation.
- **Confidant** (≥30): strangers approach you unprompted — extra moral quest offers find you.
- **Storykeeper candidate** (≥45 + 25 resolved quests): the ending flag arms.
- Empathy modifies **lore yield, not power**: empathetic players learn more, trade better, fight less. The path is its own reward — less combat, more world.
- Capstone interactions: `flag_moral_messenger_kept` (quest 60, delivered unopened) counts double toward the threshold, as do any three of the four listening-capstone secrets.

### Tone Guardrail (binding)
The Respecting-the-Dead quests speak of "spirits" and "curses" in their prose. **Implementation must realize every such effect as morale and reputation modifiers** — the wasteland's superstition, never a mechanic. No supernatural systems, per project tone rules. A "curse" is a −1 morale drip and a cruel memory record. A "spirit at peace" is a location tag and one line of ambient text.

### Determinism Checklist
- All random outcome branches: `ISeededRng`, rolled once at resolution, stored
- Propagation delays: rolled once, stored
- No `System.Random`, no `Guid.NewGuid()`, no wall-clock reads

## ✅ WHY NO ACTIVE TRACKER (DESIGN RATIONALE)

- **Numbers optimize; stories linger.** A visible meter turns kindness into strategy. Hide the number and the player is left with the child's face and the empty pack — which is the point.
- **The journal is a mirror, not a scoreboard.** Arrows after the fact let players audit their past without being graded in their present.
- **The world is the UI.** Prices, dogs, doors, radio silence, chalk warnings — feedback that costs no screen space and can't be minimized.
- **Replay lives in the dark.** Because the bands are invisible, two players can finish with opposite legends and only find out by comparing journals.
