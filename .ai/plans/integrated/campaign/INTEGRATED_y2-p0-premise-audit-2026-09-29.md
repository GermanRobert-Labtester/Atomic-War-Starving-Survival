## 0. Prologue — The Ground Beneath

> *"Nothing is built in this package. Everything else depends on it. That is what a foundation is."*

P0 is the least glamorous card in the program and the only one nobody may skip. It writes two
documents and edits no source. Its whole product is the difference between a plan that knows where
it is standing and a plan that is guessing in a file that changed an hour ago.

F1–F21 come back **CONFIRMED**, **STALE**, or **CHANGED**. Three words, and the third one is the
one that matters. A premise that has changed is not a failure of the earlier audit; it is the
repository being alive, and the only sin would be to build on the old shape anyway.

**Tone & register.** Scrupulous, plain, unglamorous. The vocabulary is the audit: *premise, path,
line, census, constant, clamp, inert, window*. Prose should sound like someone checking a list
they have checked before and will check again. Never dramatise verification. Its drama is that
nobody notices it when it works.

**Mystery & texture.** P0.2's census of 360-day constants sorts them into *runtime clamp*, *inert
default*, and *content window* — three kinds of number that look identical in source and mean
three entirely different things. That taxonomy is the card's quiet intellectual payload. §6 keeps
what it cannot classify.

**The second layer.** The two documents this card writes are photographs of ground that is still
moving. That is the quiet paradox of the audit and also its dignity: every row is dated, cited to
path and line, and obsolete the moment somebody commits — and it is still the only thing standing
between twenty-one premises and a builder's guess. Trust is not a feeling here. It is a line
number that was true this morning.

## 1b. Texture, Mystery & Voice

*(Second prose pass. Narrative texture and writing guidance only — no authority, no claimed path,
no acceptance criterion. §6's register is unchanged; the fragments below are content candidates
and deliberate silences, not new recorded questions.)*

**What the player is never told.**

- Whether F1–F21 is the whole ground. The umbrella authors twenty-one premises and stops; P0 verifies what it was given and does not assert the list is complete.
- What a **CONFIRMED** costs the person confirming it. Verification is a promise made in a document that the repository will quietly try to invalidate; the card measures the distance and says nothing about the making of it.
- Whether the census found every 360-day constant or only the ones with names. An unnamed constant is still a constant. The taxonomy absorbs the difference and does not report it.
- Whether the decision packet is one page because the decisions are small or because the writer was disciplined. P0.5 drafts it and its length is not authored.

**Voice — sample fragments** (texture only; P0 writes two documents and no data rows — the audit's
voice lives in prose and must never be dramatised).

> "F7: CONFIRMED. The line number has moved twice since the last audit. F7 has not."

> "Found what we expected. Found two things we did not expect. The document records both and ranks neither."

> "A premise marked CHANGED is not a broken promise. It is the repository breathing. Write it down, and stop building on the old shape."

> "Everything else stands on this card. So does this card. Somebody wrote it standing on it."

**Design texture beats.**

- **Verification has no drama and must be given none.** The prose of an audit should be flat enough to trust. Its drama is that nobody notices it when it works.
- **Three kinds of number.** A runtime clamp, an inert default and a content window are typed identically in source. The census's whole craft is refusing to let them look the same on paper.
- **A foundation is invisible or it is a wall.** Two documents and no code is not modesty; it is load-bearing. Never let the card grow ambitions.
- **Dated, not eternal.** Every row is evidence as of a date. Rule 7 is the card's standing religion: re-verify before anyone builds.

**Third pass — the evidence chair (texture only; §6's register unchanged).**

**The shape of the polish.** The auditor's voice is the quietest in the programme and the most
load-bearing: a premise is not a fact until it carries a path and a line number. Prose should treat
every verified row as a small permission — *this may now be attempted* — and every unverified one
as a held door.

**What the audit leaves lying around.**

> "F-row, verified. The sentence changes nothing in the world and everything about what may now be
> attempted."

> "A premise audit is a promise to the next person: nobody will have to discover this twice."

> "Not established here. Three words that keep a programme honest for a year."

**Held silences (texture, not register rows).**

- What the unverified rows would have cost. Some premises fail late and quietly; the audit records
  them and stops, which is the whole of its ambition. Texture only.
- Whether the auditor ever wants to build. The role is read-only and the reading is the whole of
  it; the wanting is not modelled and must not be.
