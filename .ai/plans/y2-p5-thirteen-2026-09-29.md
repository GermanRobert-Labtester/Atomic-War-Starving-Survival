## 0. Prologue — Thirteen

> *"We called the twelfth bunk Thirteen because it was the one that made us a place."*

An outpost is a position. A waystation is a distance. A **hearth** is the first of them that has to
answer the question *who sleeps here tonight* — and answering it twelve times is what turns a
location into an address.

Thirteen is not a system. It is twelve bunks and a real place on a real map, and the moment of
binding is the moment the shelter acquires a second sentence in its own story. Everything in this
card is deliberately small: one position, four retro-bound outposts, one existing command. Small
is the correct size. Homes are not built in expansions; they are built in allocations.

**Tone & register.** Domestic and exact. The vocabulary is the household: *bunk, hearth, position,
location, allocation*. Prose should sound like a housing list kept by someone who knows every name
on it.

**Mystery & texture.** `definitions never persisted` (Non-Goals) is the card's quietest line: the
hearth's *description* is authored and its *life* is not stored. Nothing about who lived there is
remembered by this plan. See §6.

**The second layer.** There is a species of map on which a settlement is not a dot but an
*address* — somewhere letters could go, if anyone still sent them. Binding Allocation 13 to a real
location is the program's smallest data change and its largest fictional one: it converts a place
the player has been using into a place that could, in principle, be missed. The folklore is
already true before the plan arrives. Twelve bunks, counted to thirteen. The plan's only job is to
make the counting land somewhere. And a counting that lands is the smallest kind of home: an
address where the twelfth bunk is not the last thing said.

## 1b. Texture, Mystery & Voice

*(Second prose pass. Narrative texture and writing guidance only — no authority, no claimed path,
no acceptance criterion. §6's register is unchanged; the fragments below are content candidates
and deliberate silences, not new recorded questions.)*

**What the player is never told.**

- Why the twelfth bunk is Thirteen. The name predates the binding; the plan records the folklore and does not author it (P5-OM-1).
- Who chose the location. The allocation is numbered; the choosing is not modelled. Somebody walked the ground, or somebody sent word — neither is authored.
- Whether the four retro-bound outposts remember being unbound. Retro-binding is a correction in a file. Whether it is a homecoming is not asserted anywhere.
- What a hearth is when nobody is in it. `definitions never persisted` (Non-Goals) keeps the answer safely out of the reach of the engine, which is where it belongs.

**Voice — sample fragments** (content candidates for the P8 prose surfaces; `outposts.json` gains
fields, never prose).

> "Allocation 13. Twelve bunks, one hearth, one location that answers when you ask it where it is."

> "The name is not in the catalogue. The name is what people call it. Keep both."

> "We count to thirteen. Nobody counts to fourteen."

> "Retro-binding pass: four lines corrected. Somebody wrote 'at last' in the margin and then crossed it out, which is how things are written here."

**Design texture beats.**

- **Small is the correct size.** Twelve bunks and an address. Resist every instinct to grow the hearth into a system; the card is complete at its current size.
- **Domestic vocabulary is exact vocabulary.** Bunk, hearth, position, allocation — used precisely, never cosily. Warmth comes from accuracy, not adjectives.
- **Additive fields, absolute meaning.** The data change is small and additive; the fiction is allowed to treat it as an address, an arrival, a return.
- **Life stays unpersisted.** Who slept where is deliberately unrecorded. The hearth's story is told by content, never by state.

**The shape of the polish.** The card's warmth is entirely in its accuracy: a housing list kept by
someone who knows every name on it. Prose should never get cosier than a *list* — the hearth is
made of counted bunks and one sideways word in a margin. The mystery is that the counting has a
subject: thirteen is not a number anyone needs, and it is the number the shelter uses.

**What the hearth leaves lying around.**

> "Housing list, twelve lines and a thirteenth written sideways in the margin. The margin is where
> places are named."

> "Allocation 13: the description is authored. The living is not stored. Keep both."

> "A place is missed the way this is missed — by the twelfth bunk and the counting stopping at it."

**Held silences (texture, not register rows).**

- What the thirteenth bunk is for. The folklore is recorded and not authored (P5-OM-1); whether the
  count has a subject is the reader's, and the engine never asks. Texture only.
- Who would send letters to an address, if anyone. It is the species of map where letters could go
  (§0); the post is not modelled and the possibility is the whole warmth.
