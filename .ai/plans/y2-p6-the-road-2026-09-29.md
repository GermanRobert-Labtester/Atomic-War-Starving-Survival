## 0. Prologue — The Road

> *"Distance is the only tax a shelter cannot repeal."*

Year One is a settlement game in which everything is one screen away. The Road is the card that
removes that mercy. Supply becomes a **journey** — a convoy with legs, waystations and duration —
and the outposts stop being map pins and start being places that can run out.

Three sentences in the goal carry the whole design. *Supply is a journey*: you cannot fix a
shortage by clicking. *Hostile pressure comes from named sources*: nothing arrives without an
origin, and the origin can be found. *Overrun has a relief journey*: when a position falls, there
is a way back — expensive, timed, and real.

**Tone & register.** Logistical, weary, hopeful in a minor key. The vocabulary is the road:
*convoy, leg, waystation, tether, pressure, relief, overrun*. Prose should sound like a supply
officer who has learned that the interesting column is the one marked *days out*.

**Mystery & texture.** `WorldDangerRatingForDay` is a **stub** this card replaces, and the four
legacy outposts stay `tether` (DEC-Y2-06) — a word that implies a line under tension and never
explains what the line is attached to. §6 keeps the rest open.

**The second layer.** Every other system in the shelter converts effort into goods. The Road
converts effort into *time*, which is the one currency survivors understand perfectly and never
stop resenting. The named-source rule is the mercy hidden inside the cruelty: pressure that
arrives from somewhere can be reasoned about, planned for, and one day answered. The relief
journey is the design's quiet thesis — a lost position is not a game over but a debt, and debts on
this road are paid in days. And days are the only currency that is spent whether or not anybody
signs for them.

## 1b. Texture, Mystery & Voice

*(Second prose pass. Narrative texture and writing guidance only — no authority, no claimed path,
no acceptance criterion. §6's register is unchanged; the fragments below are content candidates
and deliberate silences, not new recorded questions.)*

**What the player is never told.**

- What the tether is tied to (P6-OM-1). The word is load-bearing and stays unexplained — tension and a fixed point, and nothing more.
- What a waystation is to the people who use it in the third hour of the fourth day of a leg (P6-OM-4). The system reads it as a node. The prose is permitted to know more than the model.
- What the hostile pressure wants. Named sources are mechanics; motive is not authored and must not be improvised by any future content pass.
- What the road was before it was a road. Its history is not recorded, and `WorldDangerRatingForDay` is not restored (P6-OM-5).

**Voice — sample fragments** (content candidates for `year_two_quests.json` follow-ups and P8 road
prose).

> "Manifest: eleven crates out, four days, two legs. The interesting number is the four."

> "Pressure from a named source. The name is on the sheet. The sheet does not say why."

> "Relief is also a journey. Write it down before it starts, like everything else that matters on this road."

> "The waystation has a stove and a book. The book is the more important of the two."

**Design texture beats.**

- **Never fix a shortage by clicking.** If supply could be solved at a distance of zero, distance would be decoration and the card would be a lie.
- **Origins can be found.** That is the design's mercy and the player's only real leverage. Nothing arrives without an address.
- **Quiet days stay quiet.** No unbounded RNG draws on them (Non-Goals). Boredom on the road is authored, and it is a resource.
- **The arithmetic is authored; its fairness is never discussed.** Let the cost be legible and leave its justice to the player.

**Third pass — days out (texture only; §6's register unchanged).**

*(Polish pass, non-contractual: wording and texture only — no authority, no claimed path, no
decision, no acceptance criterion, no verification step. §6's register is unchanged; the fragments
below are content candidates and deliberate silences, not new recorded questions.)*

**The shape of the polish.** A supply officer's voice: weary, hopeful in a minor key, and honest
about the column that matters. The prose should live in the *days out* tense — everything measured
from a place the writer is not — and treat every named source as an address with no motive behind
it. The road's mercy is legibility; its cruelty is that legibility does not stop the walking.

**What the road leaves lying around.**

> "Manifest, leg three. The column marked *days out* is the only one the supply officer reads twice."

> "Waystation: stove and book. Somebody has added a third thing to the shelf and it is on no
> inventory."

> "Tether, printed on four lines of the sheet and nowhere explained. The tension is legible. The
> fixed point is not."

**Held silences (texture, not register rows).**

- What the fourth day of a leg is like. The system reads a node and the prose is permitted to know
  more (P6-OM-4); the knowing stays in the prose and must never be modelled. Texture only.
- Where relief comes from when there is nothing left to send. The relief journey is real (§0); the
  empty convoy is not authored and the road's last page stays unwritten.
