## 0. Prologue — The Seal at the Foot of the Shaft

> *"We dug down for water and found a door that had been closed from the other side."*

Every shelter in this world has a bottom. This one has a *foot* — a place where the digging stops
being about survival and starts being about something that was already there. The shaft was sunk
with a purpose. Someone brought the drawings. Someone paid for the concrete. And then someone
decided that whatever the shaft reached should stay reached-for and never reached.

The four levels below are not ruins. Ruins imply abandonment. These were *closed*: curtain,
scrubbers, archive, vats — a sequence, a procedure, an order of operations that somebody
carried out and did not finish, or finished too well.

**Tone & register.** Dread by omission. The horror is in the paperwork: a Rule that says what you
may not do, a Tell that says something is still happening, a Toll that is always denominated in
something you would rather not spend. Never describe the thing. Describe the *schedule* the thing
kept. A weekly purge has a cadence; cadences imply attention; attention implies something still
reading the ledger.

**Mystery & texture.** Nothing here is a monster and nothing here is a combat encounter. The
plan's hardest rule — *no monsters or combat* — is also its strongest atmospheric asset. What is
down there is a **system**, and systems can be understood, and understanding them is what costs
you. The deferred abyssal rows, the vault audit pair, the clock-drift contradiction and the
Sleeper are all held behind signatures precisely because the moment they are explained they stop
being frightening. §12 records what will never be answered.

## 1. Goal & Outcome

> *Design intent: the player should come to dread the number on the panel — `D3`, `D4`, `D5` —
> long before they understand what is beneath it. Depth is a countdown that runs upward.*

- **Goal:** (a) a **Seal** noticed at the foot of the existing shaft; (b) four authored **levels** (`curtain`, `scrubbers`, `archive`, `vats`), each with a Rule, a Tell and a Toll; (c) **Readings** whose noise is bounded by the existing dosimeter error band, so a level is *Read* only when two readings agree; (d) **Descent Plans** (five keys, Forced allowed) and **Shifts** with exposure applied only through existing owners; (e) **Hold / Reseal / Lose** with recoverable consequences.
- **Outcome (observable):** on a fixed seed, a shelter with depth 5, a discovered audit copy, one calibrated dosimeter and three free survivors can plan and open `curtain`; its toll appears as a range until a second agreeing reading; a shift applies dose and stress through the owners; an unpaid toll leaks up the shaft and is recoverable; save/load mid-plan and mid-hold is exact.
- **Non-Goals:** no monsters or combat; no procedural maps; no vault access mapping; no new resource; no direct health write; no new save section; no new routed panel; no change to the shaft blueprint or depth cap; no use of `ExpansionTunnel`; no Unity.
- **"Done":** §6 acceptance passes via `bin/run-scoped-tests`; ship-dark parity holds; handoff lists untouched shared paths.

## 1b. Texture, Mystery & Voice

**The Rule, the Tell, the Toll — as a literary form.**

Each level is authored as a three-line poem with teeth. The Rule is what is forbidden. The Tell is
what proves the Rule is still enforced. The Toll is what it costs to break it anyway. Keep them
equal in length and parallel in syntax in the data — the repetition *is* the dread. Players learn
the shape after level one and then read level three with their shoulders up around their ears.

**What the player is never told.**

- Whether `curtain`, `scrubbers`, `archive` and `vats` were always in that order. The ledger stores
  levels by id; it does not store a history of the naming.
- Whether the *Read* verdict is true. Two agreeing readings prove an instrument is consistent. They
  do not prove an instrument is honest.
- What the vats were for. A weekly purge has a cadence; a cadence implies a product; the product is
  never named and the data must never name it.
- Whether the archive's clock-keeper is a person. It is a *toll*. Tolls are collected.

**Voice — sample fragments (content candidates for `deep_level_events.json` / `deep_level_readings.json`).**

> "Reading taken at 04:10. Second reading at 04:10. The second reading was not taken by us."

> "Rule: do not stop the scrubbers. Tell: the air tastes of pennies when someone does."

> "Toll: one hour, unaccounted. The ledger will accept it. The ledger will not record whose."

> "We resealed the curtain on the ninth day. On the tenth day the curtain was already resealed."

**Design texture beats.**

- **Ranges before numbers.** A toll shown as a range is a promise about the world's honesty. The
  moment it resolves to a number, the player should feel the instrument has *committed*, not that
  the UI has improved.
- **Forced is a moral verb.** Skipping the reading is not a shortcut; it is a decision recorded
  against the run. `forced` must be visible in every later projection, like a scar in the ledger.
- **The Shaft is a corridor, not a map.** No procedural geometry. Depth is a number and a mood.
- **Recoverability is the horror's leash.** Every loss state can be undone. That is what makes the
  player keep going down — the knowledge that they could have stopped at any time.

### TD-P2 — Readings (Core, pure)
- `Reading = truth ± noise(errorBand)`, seeded fork keyed `(day, levelId, deviceTag)`; **Read** when two readings agree within tolerance; Disagreement lines from data.
- **Accept:** determinism; a wider error band yields more disagreements; no writes to the calibration owner.

### TD-P3 — Descent Plan and opening (Core + host)
- Five keys (reading, instrument, air, crew, time) + stability margin; Forced when reading/instrument are skipped; crew and time never skippable; opening commits atomically through the owners' commands.
- **Accept:** a plan missing crew/time/air/stability fails with a reason code; a Forced open marks `forced` and uses the top of the toll range until Read.

### TD-P4 — Shifts and exposure (host adapter)
- Bounded party (≤3), one shift per level per day; dose via `RadiationSystem.Expose`, stress via `AddStress`, air via the atmosphere input; "turn back" forfeits the find.
- **Accept:** each effect calls exactly one public owner method; no health write; conservation of any stock consumed or yielded.

### TD-P5 — Hold, reseal, lose (Core + host)
- Daily tick: toll due, standing benefit (if any) applied through an owner's public API, leak after N unpaid days, reseal under duress.
- **Accept:** a Lost level is always resealable; benefits absent when not Held; deterministic.

## 6. Acceptance criteria
1. Core authority + host owner + event path + persistence + observable outcome agree (CLAUDE.md "integrated").
2. Ship-dark parity: no deep rows → shaft, radiation, stress, atmosphere and archive outputs identical on a saved corpus.
3. Determinism: identical readings, disagreements and leaks on replay (`CampaignStreamIds` fork; never `System.Random`).
4. Save round-trip mid-plan, mid-shift and mid-hold; old saves load.
5. No writes to any owner except through its public API; no health write; no new resource.
6. Range → number: a toll shows as a range until Read and never shows a number the truth does not support.
7. No deferred archive/abyssal row activates unless its decision is signed; no vault access mapping anywhere.
